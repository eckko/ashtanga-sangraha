# Book notes: Aṣṭāṅga Saṅgraha (Vāgbhaṭa), Sūtrasthāna chapters 1-5
- Source: a digitised study text (1888 Marathi-printed edition, poor scan, proofread). Its Sanskrit is noisy: keep printed tokens, flag typos.
- Chapter 1 Āyuṣkāmīya, 2 Śiṣyopanayanīya, 3 Dinacaryā, 4 Ṛtucaryā, 5 Rogānutpādanīya. The text mixes anuṣṭubh (16 syllables a half-verse line) and,
  in places, upajāti (22 a line); some units are prose (formula "athāto ... vyākhyāsyāmaḥ", the closing "iti ... adhyāyaḥ" line, definitions in ch. 4).
- Compound drug and food lists are common: gloss "drug name" or "food" unless transparent. Never identify a plant, never state an effect or a dose
  beyond the Sanskrit. Health content: translate the text as the text says ("the text states" only in notes).
- The Hindi layer of the book is often word-for-word with brackets and Latin letters: write your own natural Hindi.
- The study text numbers lines C.L (chapter.line); `line_ids` give them. The first line of each chapter is the formula "athāto X-adhyāyaṃ vyākhyāsyāmaḥ".

## Decision (grouping)
Units keep the edition's own two-line pairing (C.L, C.L+1). Where a sentence runs across units, drafts note it and authors keep each question to what the unit itself contains. No re-pairing.

## Orthography note (from chunk 14)
A trailing "-ā" can be "-āḥ" with the visarga dropped before a voiced consonant (e.g. sāgarākārā bhūḥ, ākulā diśaḥ). Not a typo; do not flag.
