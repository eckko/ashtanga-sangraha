02.06.1 | Now, therefore, we shall explain the bodily division of the sirās (vessels).
02.06.2 | Ten root-sirās (mūlasirā), bound to the heart (hṛdaya), carry ojas (vital essence) to all the limbs (aṅga) and sub-limbs (pratyaṅga).
02.06.3 | For the activities (ceṣṭā) of the body are bound to those.
02.06.4 | But those, having gone two aṅgulas, an aṅgula, half an aṅgula, a yava (barley-grain) and half a yava, being dispersed like the spreading of the sevanī (sense uncertain) of a tree's leaf, become seven hundred.
02.06.5 | Of them, four hundred are in the limbs (śākhā).
02.06.6 | Among those, sixteen are not to be pierced.
02.06.7 | One hundred and thirty-six in the antarādhi (trunk, probable).
02.06.8 | Among those, twenty-two.
02.06.9 | One hundred and sixty-four above; among those also, fifty.
02.06.10 | Of them, a hundred in each thigh (sakthi). Among those, one should not pierce the jāladharā,
02.06.11 | and (ones) going across and within.
02.06.12 | Thirty-two in the śroṇi (hip region), having the guda (anus) and meḍhra (penis) as their seat.
02.06.13 | Of them, two each in the two groins (vaṅkṣaṇa) and in the two kaṭīkataruṇas, thus eight; on a conjectural reading, one should guard (them) from the knife (śastra).
02.06.14 | Sixteen in the two sides (pārśva); of those two, one each going upward, named pārśvasandhi.
02.06.15 | Twenty-four in the back (pṛṣṭha).
02.06.16 | Of them, on both sides of the pṛṣṭhavaṃśa (spine), two and two going upward; those are four.
02.06.17 | Twenty-four in the belly (udara).
02.06.18 | Of them, above the meḍhra (penis), on both sides of the romarājī (line of body hair), two and two; those also are four.
02.06.19 | Forty in the chest (vakṣas).
02.06.20 | Of them, two in the heart (hṛdaya).
02.06.21 | And two and two in the stanamūlas and the stanarohitas.
02.06.22 | One each in the apastambhas and the apalāpas (on a conjectural reading), thus.
02.06.23 | Those are fourteen.
02.06.24 | Twenty-four in the neck (grīvā).
02.06.25 | Of them, four and eight are named marma (vital spot).
02.06.26 | Two in the two kṛkāṭikās and two in the two vidhuras, thus sixteen; one should not touch (them) with the knife (śastra).
02.06.27 | In the two jaws (hanu), sixteen; of them, two connected with the joint (sandhi).
02.06.28 | Sixteen in the tongue (jihvā).
02.06.29 | Below them, two perceiving taste (rasa) and two setting speech in motion; those are four.
02.06.30 | Twenty-four in the nose (nāsā).
02.06.31 | Of them, two perceiving smell (gandha); and of those very ones, one in the palate (tālu); thus three.
02.06.32 | Fifty-six in the two eyes (netra).
02.06.33 | Of those two, the two actions of opening (unmeṣa) and closing (nimeṣa): two, two; and two in the two apāṅgas (outer corners of the eye); thus six.
02.06.34 | Those that go to the nose and eyes, however, are sixty in the forehead (lalāṭa).
02.06.35 | Of them, four going along to the keśānta (end of the hair, probably the hair-line); two in the two āvartas; and one in the sthapanī; thus seven.
02.06.36 | In the two ears (karṇa), sixteen; of them, two carrying sound (śabda); those very two also in the two śaṅkhas (temples); of them, two gone to the joint (sandhi) of the temple.
02.06.37 | Twelve in the head (mūrdhan).
02.06.38 | Of them, two in the two utkṣepas; one each in the sīmantas; one in the adhipati; thus eight: one should avoid (them) with the knife (śastra).
02.06.39 | Likewise (on a conjectural reading of the first word), the small, crooked, intermingled and knotted ones, and those in the joints, are not to be dealt with by the knife (śastra).
02.06.40 | Of them, each according to its own, by a division into fourth parts, a set of a hundred with seventy-five in addition carries Vāta and blood (anila-rakta).
02.06.41 | This division is also in Pitta, śleṣman (Kapha) and pure blood.
02.06.42 | The doṣas, remaining thus, favour (support) the body.
02.06.43 | But by reversal (vyatyaya) they afflict.
02.06.44 | There, the dusky-brown and reddish, throbbing, fine (ones), momentarily full and momentarily empty, carry Vāta-blood (vāta-rakta).
02.06.45 | Blue, yellow and dark (ones), warm to the touch, quickly discharging: Pitta-blood (pitta-rakta).
02.06.46 | Fair, unctuous, steady, cold, itching (ones): Kapha-blood (kapha-rakta).
02.06.47 | In a mixture of the signs: blood of saṃsarga (combination) and sannipāta (union).
02.06.48 | Even, hidden, unctuous, rohiṇī (literally 'rising'; sense here uncertain) (ones): pure blood (śuddha-rakta).
02.06.49 | The dhamanīs (vessels, probably blood vessels), however, are twenty-four.
02.06.50 | By these, this body is moistened, like a garden by water-carrying (ones) and like a field by canals (kulyā).
02.06.51 | And by these the navel is surrounded, on a conjectural reading like the hub of a wheel by its spokes.
02.06.52 | Therefore the prāṇas (vital breaths) are established especially in the navel; and since it, on a conjectural reading, is also within a seat (adhiṣṭhāna)
02.06.53 | Of those dhamanīs indeed, on a conjectural reading, ten have spread upward, ten downward, four crosswise.
02.06.54 | Then, by them, the limbs and parts, each according to its own, are held and nourished.
02.06.55 | Of these, the upward-going (ones), having reached the heart (hṛdaya), each come to be threefold.
02.06.56 | Then, among the thirty, two and two, carrying Vāta, Pitta, Kapha, blood (rakta) and rasa (the nutrient fluid).
02.06.57 | By eight, sound, form, taste and smell are grasped (perceived).
02.06.58 | By two and two, one speaks, makes a sound (ghoṣa), sleeps and wakes.
02.06.59 | Two carry tears; and two, resting on the breasts: a woman's milk (stanya), a man's semen (śukra).
02.06.60 | The downward-going (ones), situated in the pakvāśaya (the seat of digested matter, probably the large intestine region), likewise come to be threefold.
02.06.61 | There, the first ten: as before.
02.06.62 | Two carry food, with the intestines (antra) as their resting-place; two, water; two, urine; two carry semen (śukra); and two release.
02.06.63 | Those very two: of women, ārtava (menstrual blood).
02.06.64 | Two that expel stool (varcas, on a conjectural reading), bound to the large intestine (sthūlāntra); but the remaining eight, running crosswise, satiate the sweat (sveda).
02.06.65 | The four that go crosswise, being divided, become very manifold (in very many ways); this was stated before.
02.06.66 | The srotas (bodily channels, openings) of men are nine: the two ears, two eyes, two nostrils, mouth, anus (pāyu) and urinary passage; and three other (ones) of women: the two breasts and the blood-passage (rakta-patha, probably the passage of the menstrual blood).
02.06.67 | And the other inner srotas (channels): seats (āyatana) of life, thirteen; seats of prāṇa (vital breath), water, food, the dhātus and the malas.
02.06.68 | There, of the prāṇa-carrying (ones), the root (mūla) is the heart (hṛdaya) and the great channel (mahāsrotas).
02.06.69 | They become vitiated by wasting (kṣaya), dryness (raukṣya), thirst, hunger, exertion (vyāyāma), holding back of urges (vega-dhāraṇa) and the like.
02.06.70 | Then breathing (ucchvasana): excessively released, obstructed, agitated, scanty or frequent, with sound and pain.
02.06.71 | There, the treatment (sādhana) is as in śvāsa (breathlessness; probably dyspnoea of the asthma type).
02.06.72 | Of the water-carrying (ones), the root is the palate (tālu) and the kloma (identity disputed).
02.06.73 | They become vitiated by āma (sense uncertain here), fear, excessive drinking, heat, dry food, holding back of thirst (on a conjectural reading) and the like. Then excessive thirst, dryness of the mouth, ringing (kṣveḍana, probably tinnitus-type) in the ears and seeing of darkness.
02.06.74 | There, the medicine (auṣadha) stated for thirst (tṛṣṇā).
02.06.75 | Of the food-carrying (ones), the root is the āmāśaya (seat of undigested food, probably the stomach) and the left side (vāma-pārśva).
02.06.76 | In their vitiation, one should understand all as stated in the Mātrāśitīya.
02.06.77 | Of the rasa-carrying (ones), the root is the heart (hṛdaya) and the ten dhamanīs (vessels).
02.06.78 | Of the blood-carrying (ones): the liver (yakṛt) and the spleen (plīhan).
02.06.79 | Of the flesh-carrying (ones): the sinews (snāva, probably tendons or ligaments) and the skin (tvac).
02.06.80 | Of the fat-carrying (ones) (medas): the two kidneys (vṛkka) and the flesh (māṃsa).
02.06.81 | Of the bone-carrying (ones): the jaghana (hips or loins region, probable) and fat (medas).
02.06.82 | Of the marrow-carrying (ones): the joints (parvan) and the bones (asthi).
02.06.83 | Of those that carry śukra (semen): the two breasts, the two testicles (muṣka) and the marrow (majjā).
02.06.84 | Of those that carry urine: the bladder (basti) and the two groins (vaṅkṣaṇa).
02.06.85 | Of those that carry dung (śakṛt): the pakvāśaya (seat of the digested, probably the large-bowel region) and the thick intestine (sthūlāntra, probably the large intestine).
02.06.86 | Of those that carry sweat (sveda): the fat (medas) and the hair-pores (romakūpa).
02.06.87 | Of these, the various vitiation, the unwholesome food and conduct (vihāra), the complications (upadrava) and the treatment one should know through the doṣa and the rest; one should discern each by the sign of increase and decrease.
02.06.88 | But on the piercing of the srotas (channels): giddiness, fainting or confusion, tremor, raving, distension of the belly (ādhmāna), pain, distaste for food, thirst, vomiting, fever, on a conjectural reading flow of blood, retention of urine and stool, and death.
02.06.89 | Therefore, having refused one pierced in a srotas, one should treat (him).
02.06.90 | And (one) whose dart has been extracted, by the procedure for a fresh wound.
02.06.91 | Among these, the sirā (vessels, probably veins) and the dhamanī (pulsating vessels, probably arteries) are particular forms of the srotas only.
02.06.92 | But others maintain a difference (anyatva).
02.06.93 | Merely through mutual proximity, similarity of function and fineness, a non-distinction, as it were, is perceived among the sirā, dhamanī and srotas, even though their functions are distinct.
02.06.94 | Others, again, say:
02.06.95 | Srotas (channels), sirā, dhamanī, the carriers of rasa, nāḍī, paths, routes, ways, openings of the body, closed and open, places, seats (āśaya), abodes and dwellings: these are the names, in the body, of the spaces of the dhātus, perceptible and imperceptible.
02.06.96 | And these spaces.
02.06.97 | Agitated, they agitate the dhātus that stand in their seats and those that stand on their paths.
02.06.98 | They too (agitate) those and the srotas.
02.06.99 | The srotas and the dhātus (agitate) the dhātus.
02.06.100 | The vitiators of all these indeed are the vitiated doṣas.
02.06.101 | And they mostly become vitiated through a fault of agni (the digestive fire).
02.06.102 | Therefore the causes (nimitta): lifespan, health, strength, growth, complexion and ojas (vital essence).
02.06.103 | And dependent on that alone are the rañjaka (a kind of Pitta) and the rest, the bhūtāgnis (fires of the elements) and the dhātvagnis (fires of the dhātus).
02.06.104 | Likewise food too, digested (pakva) by that very (agni), attains the state of nectar (amṛta), and undigested, the state of poison (viṣa).
02.06.105 | Among these, a special kind of Pitta, named pācaka (the digesting one), is agni: so it was stated earlier.
02.06.106 | Others, again, say:
02.06.107 | Agni is the inner heat produced by the coming together of doṣa, dhātu and mala, whose seat and function are as indicated.
02.06.108 | That agni, the inner fire, kindled by samāna (wind), cooks the food eaten according to rule, drawn into the koṣṭha (storehouse, belly) by prāṇa wind, its mass broken apart by liquids, softened by sneha (oil or fat), lying in the āmāśaya (seat of the undigested, probably the stomach) like water and rice in a pot.
02.06.109 | That (food), however, though containing all tastes (rasa), as it is being cooked, first becomes sweet and stirs up Kapha, become foamy.
02.06.110 | Then, burnt (vidagdha), turned sour, falling away from the āmāśaya, (it stirs up) clear Pitta.
02.06.111 | And having reached the pakvāśaya (probably the large-bowel region), turned pungent, (it stirs up) the wind (anila, that is Vāta).
02.06.112 | And then, the food being thus moistened, five (unclear words), scattered by wind, each according to its own, cook, on a conjectural reading, only the five element-qualities (bhūtaguṇa) lying in the food.
02.06.113 | Those, cooked, again, each according to its own, nourish the element-qualities (bhūtaguṇa) that rest in the body and are of their own unaltered nature.
02.06.114 | And thus, from the digested food, as if from an oil having various medicines in its core, a clear essence named rasa and a waste (mala) named kiṭṭa are produced.
02.06.115 | And by those two, the essence and the waste, the srotas of the bodily qualities having just that nature, named dhātus, are each filled in succession in an unbroken stream.
02.06.116 | And from the srotas, according to division and according to need, the dhātus are nourished.
02.06.117 | Even with the successive entry of each later one, there is no wasting (apacaya) of the earlier srotas, which are nourished in due time by the proper use of food, with transformation.
02.06.118 | And then the clear essences (prasāda) and the wastes (mala) called dhātus each follow their own measure, according to age and body.
02.06.119 | There, the food-rasa (āhāra-rasa), scattered by vyāna (a wind), each according to its own, even (reading uncertain), being cooked in order in the dhātu-fires, immediately after the loss of its own state, bearing the dhātu name 'prāmraktādi' (unclear), timely and of unfailing measure of strength, having invigorated the body, nourishes the dhātus and the wastes of the dhātus.
02.06.120 | Now the refuse of food (anna-kiṭṭa): clear urine (mūtra) and thick dung (śakṛt).
02.06.121 | Of rasa the essence is blood (rakta); the waste (mala) is Kapha and lasīkā (probably lymph or tissue fluid).
02.06.122 | Of blood the essence is flesh (māṃsa); the tendons (kaṇḍarā, probably large tendons) and the vessels (sirā) too; the waste is Pitta.
02.06.123 | Of flesh the essence is fat (medas), the skins (tvacaḥ, plural) and vasā (muscle fat); the refuse is the wastes of the ear, eye, nose, mouth, hair-pores and genital organ.
02.06.124 | Of fat (medas), the essence (sāra) is bone (asthi), ligaments (snāyu) and joints (sandhi); the waste (kiṭṭa) is sweat.
02.06.125 | Of bone (asthi), the essence is marrow (majjā); the waste (kiṭṭa) is the hair of the head, the body hair (loman) and the nails.
02.06.126 | Of marrow (majjan), however, the essence is śukra (semen); the waste (mala) is the excrement of the eye (akṣi-viṭ) and the oiliness (sneha) of the skins.
02.06.127 | Of śukra (semen), the essence is ojas (vital essence).
02.06.128 | And through extreme purity there is absence of waste (mala) in this.
02.06.129 | Others, however, for this very reason do not accept a cooking (pāka) of it.
02.06.130 | Others, however, hold the essence of śukra (semen) to be the garbha (embryo) itself.
02.06.131 | On a conjectural reading: Wind (vāyu), however, through the abundance and scarcity of the fire (agni) and of the food, from this-and-that particular mūrcchanā (probably intensification), appears in the koṣṭha (storehouse, belly cavity), non-corporeal (amūrta), sound-possessing, abundant in slight sound, or small, five-natured (pañcātmā).
02.06.132 | And having thus done, all states (bhāva) in the body are made of food alone.
02.06.133 | By that alone, this body-machine (śarīra-yantra), thus, for a long time, as a deposit (nyāsa) within and without, with an unbroken flow, endowed with health, strength and growth, is kept going for as long as the taking up (upādāna) of action (karma).
02.06.134 | But otherwise, either, with the srotas (channels) bound by the throwing up (utkṣepa) of food-rasa and the like, it is cut off in the midst of the flow of the dhātus, which is not duly joined, or it becomes a vessel (bhājana) of disease (vyādhi).
02.06.135 | But others describe.
02.06.136 | On a conjectural reading: Of food, just as it is swallowed, there is the rush (pluti) through the throat-tube (kaṇṭha-nāḍī, probably the gullet); of that, having descended into the great hollow (mahānimna, probably the abdominal cavity), whichever part is licked over by the bodily fire (kāyāgni) is brought to cooking; of each such part alone, a trace of rasa called prasāda (clear essence), immediately upon its production, spread equally into all the dhātus by the enclosed and open (ones), through the doors, into the srotas with open mouths akāsanneṣu (unclear); the greater (part) goes first, and by turns follows in the others also.
02.06.137 | On a conjectural reading: Thus the food-rasa itself directly nourishes all the dhātus, by only some difference of time.
02.06.138 | But the dhātus do not pass into the state of another dhātu through the crushing (upamarda) of their own form.
02.06.139 | Agni (digestive fire), however, is of four kinds by difference of strength: sama (even), viṣama (uneven), tīkṣṇa (sharp) and manda (slow).
02.06.140 | Among these, the sama (even) one is that which properly digests food that has been taken properly alone.
02.06.141 | The viṣama (uneven) one digests even what is improperly taken, and even when properly (taken) does not digest.
02.06.142 | The tīkṣṇa (sharp) one digests quickly even what is improperly taken; that very one, increasing, becomes atyagni (excessive fire).
02.06.143 | The manda (slow) one, even what is properly taken, after showing the signs of āma (raw, undigested) — heaviness of the belly, flatulent distension (ādhmāna), constipation (vibandha), gurgling (āṭopa), intestinal murmuring (āntra-kūjana), dryness of the mouth, stiffness of the anus (pāyu) and the like — digests after a long time.
02.06.144 | On a conjectural reading: Even with external fire consisting of flame and embers, cooking is not always of one form; what to say of the heat lodged within, even when it is even (sama)! Therefore one should protect it with effort.
02.06.145 | Therein, equally with the best sātmya (what suits), each according to its own, having brought the others gradually to equality by means of the lesser (avara), one should protect (it) with the best (pravara), by the remedy for Vāta and the like stated in the doṣa-treatment section (doṣopakramaṇīya, probably a chapter title), and by the combinations of medicines and exercises prescribed in grahaṇī disease (grahaṇī-roga, probably a digestive disorder).
02.06.146 | And on this matter there are:
02.06.147 | Equal in colour to their own dhātu, round, thick and fine (ones).
02.06.148 | The srotas (channels) are long in form and like tendrils (pratāna, probably tendrils of a creeper).
02.06.149 | Food and also conduct (vihāra), whichever would be equal to the doṣas in qualities,
02.06.150 | and whichever is unlike the dhātus in quality (viguṇa), that is the defiler (pradūṣaka) of the srotas.
02.06.151 | Excessive flow (atipravṛtti), or obstruction (saṅga), or also knots (granthi) of the vessels (sirā, probably veins),
02.06.152 | vimārgapto (unclear) or going: the sign of the vitiation (duṣṭi) of the srotas.
02.06.153 | Fine like (those) of lotus stalks (bisa), and spread far.
02.06.154 | The openings (dvāra) of the srotas in the body, by which rasa is built up.
02.06.155 | Some say from a day and night, others from six days, others still.
02.06.156 | From a month, food goes on to the state of śukra (semen), by the sequence of cooking (pāka).
02.06.157 | But those that are vṛṣya (virility-promoting) and the like, through their prabhāva (special potency), at once make śukra (semen) and the like.
02.06.158 | Generally, whatever other medicine (bheṣaja) also does, from a day and night.
02.06.159 | Even (sama) when Samāna (a wind) stands in its own place; the fire (agni) is viṣama (uneven) when gone along a wrong path.
02.06.160 | Sharp (tīkṣṇa) when overcome by Pitta; slow (manda) when this is afflicted by Kapha.
02.06.161 | When the fire (agni) is extinguished (śānta), one dies; when well-ordered (yukta), one lives long, free from disease.
02.06.162 | When abnormal (vikṛta), one would be diseased; therefore one should protect it with effort.
02.06.163 | Thus the sixth chapter, named Sirāvibhāga (division of the sirā, vessels).
