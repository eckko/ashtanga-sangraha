02.01.1 | Homage to the auspicious Gaṇeśa.
02.01.2 | Homage to Dhanvantari.
02.01.3 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the putrakāmīya (relating to one who desires a son) Śārīra (relating to the body).
02.01.4 | Thus, indeed, Ātreya and the other great sages said.
02.01.5 | Now, a man of twenty-one years should marry, by a blameless rule, a girl of unlike gotra and of unlike ancestry, born in a family in which disease is non-transmitting; endowed with beauty, conduct and marks; not deficient; whose teeth, lips, ears, nails, hair and breasts are undamaged; whose constitution (prakṛti) is gentle and free of disease; not tawny and not reddish-brown; with limbs neither deficient nor in excess; of about twelve years; not bearing names of immortals, serpents, rivers, moving things, trees, birds, stars, or of other things not to be looked at and frightening; faultless and blameless.
02.01.6 | In her, of sixteen years, a man of twenty-five years should strive for the sake of a son.
02.01.7 | For then those two, having attained potency (vīrya), beget offspring endowed with potency.
02.01.8 | An embryo (garbha) placed by one of less than twenty-five years in one of less than sixteen years may reach destruction in the womb itself (kukṣisthaḥ, on a conjectural reading of the printed kukṣisya), or may be of short life-span, with little strength, health and means, or with defective sense organs; and in the ṛtu, in the first triad of nights, and likewise when it is vitiated, on a conjectural reading, injury of semen and menstrual blood (śukra and ārtava).
02.01.9 | There, the essence (sāra) of food that has been well transformed and has in order reached the marrow (majjan) obtains the name śukra (semen).
02.01.10 | That, like ghee in milk and jaggery in sugarcane juice, abides in the body, resting in the śukra-bearing kalā (a membranous layer), by pervading all the limbs, and especially in the marrow (majjā), the testicles (muṣka) and the breasts; stirred up by excitement, by saṃprahana (striking together), from the possession of the heart, having become a lump, it issues from limb to limb. That śukra (semen), gentle (saumya), unctuous, heavy, white, honey-scented, sweet, slimy, abundant, thick, and of the colour of any one of ghee, oil or honey, becomes fit for conception (garbhādhāna).
02.01.11 | Likewise, the blood itself of women, month by month, having reached the garbhakoṣṭha (probably the womb), flowing for three days, they call ārtava (menstrual blood).
02.01.12 | Through excessive continuation, whether out of season or in season, that very thing obtains the names asṛgdara (probably excessive menstrual bleeding), pradara (probably abnormal vaginal discharge or bleeding) and vyāpad (disorder), and the name raktayoni.
02.01.13 | In the management of that, one should look to the treatment of raktapitta (probably a bleeding disorder) and the remedy (pratiṣedha) for diseases of the private parts (guhya-roga).
02.01.14 | Of those whose passages are covered by Vāta and Kapha, not flowing; when that flows through Pitta-increasing measures, with semen or without semen, devoid of life, of a woman who resorts to Vāta-increasing (ones), showing the signs of pregnancy, it becomes one with gulma (an abdominal lump); in that case one should look to the treatment of gulma.
02.01.15 | Or sometimes, in a pregnant (woman) indeed, vātodara (Vāta-belly: probably abdominal swelling from Vāta) occurs; that subsides with Vāta-pacifying (ones).
02.01.16 | That very ārtava (menstrual blood), sometimes held back by gentle diets and regimens of a bulk-promoting (bṛṃhaṇa) nature, increases the belly (udara), free of complications indeed, as if occupied by an embryo.
02.01.17 | Because of which the deluded call her, who is not pregnant, pregnant.
02.01.18 | Then, when the blood flows through contrary (ones) or by chance, not seeing the body of the embryo, the ignorant say: 'carried off by beings (bhūta)'.
02.01.19 | Since the taking away of ojas (vital essence) by rakṣas (demons) occurs, but not of the body, from undesirability or from incapacity.
02.01.20 | For there is no perception of the activity of desire and power, and the removal of anyone's body by humans is not seen.
02.01.21 | And if some, endowed with excessive power, passing beyond even what is seen, proceed to cast off the body of the embryo, then how indeed would the prāptaviśarāḥ (unclear, on a conjectural reading) neglect its mothers?
02.01.22 | Ārtava (menstrual blood), moreover, which resembles hare's blood and lākṣā (lac) juice and, when washed, loses its colour, they call pure.
02.01.23 | And just as, of a young or an old tree, the flower and fruit, or of the flower and fruit of such a kind the smell, is not found, so indeed not of a man of less than sixteen or past seventy years are the emission of semen (śukra) and the rest.
02.01.24 | And of a woman of less than twelve or past fifty years, the rajas (menses), breast milk (stanya) and the rest.
02.01.25 | Then, having oleated a couple whose semen (śukra) and ārtava are pure, who are free of disease and mutually attached, and having cleansed them as prescribed, one should treat them with āsthāpana (decoction enema) and anuvāsana (oil enema).
02.01.26 | But especially, with preparations of sweet medicines containing ghee and milk, the man; with oil, the woman; and with Pitta-increasing meats.
02.01.27 | Thus indeed, by the likeness of qualities, the semen (śukra) and ārtava are nourished.
02.01.28 | The semens (retas) that are Vāta, Pitta, Śleṣma (Kapha), corpse-smelling, knot, pus, wasted, urine-and-faeces, however, are seedless (abīja).
02.01.29 | Among these: thin, rough, frothy, reddish, scanty, interrupted, with pain, and after a long time it is discharged, by Vāta.
02.01.30 | Slightly yellow, non-slimy, or blue, dahadviṣa (unclear), it issues by Pitta.
02.01.31 | Mixed with marrow (majjā), abundant, bound, and it sinks somewhat in water, by Śleṣma (Kapha).
02.01.32 | Corpse-smelling, scanty, by blood.
02.01.33 | Become a knot (granthi), by Vāta and Śleṣma (Kapha).
02.01.34 | Resembling foul pus, by Pitta and Śleṣma (Kapha).
02.01.35 | Wasted (kṣīṇa), by Vāta and Pitta.
02.01.36 | That has been stated before, with its treatment.
02.01.37 | Smelling of urine and faeces, by sannipāta (the three doṣas together).
02.01.38 | The ārtava (menstrual blood) also, when joined with the faults (doṣa) of śukra (semen), is itself seedless.
02.01.39 | Its sign and name as before.
02.01.40 | Among these, the corpse-smelling, knot, pus and wasted semens (retas) are curable with difficulty.
02.01.41 | But the urine-and-faeces śukra and ārtava is incurable, or the kuṇapa, granthi or pūya ārtava.
02.01.42 | Among these, the first three faults of śukra (semen) and ārtava one should treat, each according to its own, with the treatment of the doṣa.
02.01.43 | And with the remedy for the private parts (guhya-pratiṣedha), also both.
02.01.44 | In the Vāta-type fault (doṣa) of semen (śukra): drinking of ghee cooked with sukta, saindhava (rock salt) and sour fruit, with yava-kṣāra (kṣāra of barley) as the addition (prativāpa).
02.01.45 | Āsthāpana (decoction enema) joined with bilva (bael), vidārī (probably Indian kudzu, Pueraria tuberosa) and milk.
02.01.46 | Oil cooked with madhuka (probably licorice) and bhadradāru (probably deodar), as anuvāsana (oil enema).
02.01.47 | Oil cooked with milk and the juice (rasa) of kulīra (probably crab), as uttarabasti (injection through the urinary or genital passage).
02.01.48 | In the Pitta-type (one): drinking of ghee cooked with kāṇḍekṣu, śvadaṃṣṭrā (puncture vine) and guḍūcī (Tinospora cordifolia), with mūrvā and madhuka as the addition (pratīvāpa).
02.01.49 | Powder of trivṛt (turpeth) with ghee is the purgation (virecana).
02.01.50 | Āsthāpana (decoction enema) cooked with payasyā and śrīparṇī (probably gambhārī, Gmelina arborea), joined with milk.
02.01.51 | Oil cooked with madhuka and mudgaparṇī (probably Phaseolus trilobus), as anuvāsana (oil enema) and as uttarabasti.
02.01.52 | In the Kapha-type (one): drinking of ghee cooked with a decoction (kvātha) of pāṣāṇabhedaka (probably pāṣāṇabheda, Bergenia ligulata), aśmantaka and āmalaka (emblic myrobalan), with powder of pippalī (long pepper) and madhuka as the addition.
02.01.53 | A decoction of madanaphala (Randia dumetorum) as vamana (emesis).
02.01.54 | The powder of dantī (probably Baliospermum montanum) and viḍaṅga (Embelia ribes), licked with oil, is the purgation (virecana).
02.01.55 | Āsthāpana (decoction enema) strong or thick (pragāḍha) with a decoction of rājavṛkṣa (Cassia fistula) and madanaphala.
02.01.56 | Oil cooked with madhuka and pippalī (long pepper), as anuvāsana and as uttarabasti. In the Vāta-type fault of ārtava (menstrual blood) also: drinking of ghee cooked with bhārgī (Clerodendrum serratum) and bhadradāru (probably deodar).
02.01.57 | Or milk cooked with kāśmarya, kṣudrasahā and an unclear word, on a conjectural reading.

02.01.60 | A decoction of sarala (probably Pinus roxburghii) and mudgaparṇī as the wash (prakṣālana).
02.01.61 | In the Pitta-type (one) one should drink the decoction of the pair of kākolī and the root of vidārī, the decoction of utpala and padmaka, and the decoction of the flower of madhuka and the fruit of kāśmarya, with vāsa (probably Adhatoda vasica) and sugar.
02.01.62 | Or a decoction of white sandalwood (śveta-candana, probably Santalum album), with honey.
02.01.63 | Or a paste (kalka) of dhava (probably Anogeissus latifolia) and dhātakī (Woodfordia fruticosa).
02.01.64 | Or, with ghee, a paste (kalka) of madhuka, madhurasā and mṛdvīkā (grape or raisin).
02.01.65 | Śamyāka, gavākṣī (probably Citrullus colocynthis) and milk are the purgation (virecana).

02.01.67 | A decoction of gairika (red ochre) and ariṣṭa (probably neem) as the wash (prakṣālana).
02.01.68 | In the Kapha-type (one) one should drink a decoction of kuṭaja (Holarrhena antidysenterica), kaṭukā (probably Picrorhiza kurroa) and aśvagandhā (Withania somnifera).
02.01.69 | Or, with honey (mākṣika), on a conjectural reading a decoction relating to the milk-trees (kṣīrivṛkṣa); the printed word is unclear.
02.01.70 | Or one should lick the powder of just these, with honey and ghee.

02.01.72 | And one should hold just its paste in the yoni (vagina).
02.01.73 | On a conjectural reading, a decoction of the unclear compound rodhārtiduka- as the wash (prakṣālana), or the urine of goat and sheep.
02.01.74 | In kuṇapa-retas (probably semen of a corpse-like smell), one should make him drink ghee cooked with dhātakī flowers, khadira (Acacia catechu), arjuna (Terminalia arjuna) and dāḍima (pomegranate), or, on a conjectural reading, with madana and the like.
02.01.75 | In granthi-retas (semen with knots or lumps): with pāṣāṇabhid (probably Bergenia ligulata), or with the ash of palāśa (Butea monosperma).
02.01.76 | In pūya-retas (semen with pus): with the two (groups) beginning with parūṣaka (phalsa, Grewia asiatica) and beginning with nyagrodha (banyan, Ficus benghalensis).
02.01.77 | In mūtra-purīṣa-retas (probably semen with urine and feces) greatly altered by Vāyu (wind): with hiṅgu, uśīra (probably vetiver, on a conjectural reading of the printed śīra) and citraka (leadwort); or the (one) cooked with citraka, vitunnaka, priyaṅgu, hiṅgu, samaṅgā and mṛṇāla (lotus stalk), with the powder of elā (probably small cardamom) and moca as the addition.
02.01.78 | Likewise, in granthi-ārtava (menstrual blood with knots or lumps): a decoction of pāṭhā (probably Cissampelos pareira), trikaṇṭaka (probably Tribulus terrestris) and vṛkṣaka (probably kuṭaja, Holarrhena antidysenterica).
02.01.79 | In kuṇapa-pūya-ārtava (menstrual blood with a corpse-like smell and pus, probably), one should drink a decoction of candana. And here the paste and the decoction of triphalā (the three myrobalans), the holding (dhāraṇa) and the sipping or rinsing (ācamana).
02.01.80 | And in all faults (doṣa) of semen and ārtava, one should treat the woman and the man again and again with the actions beginning with sneha (oil, fat), especially with uttarabasti injections.
02.01.81 | And in the yoni (vagina), as suited to each, with pastes (kalka) and with picu (cotton-wool wads).
02.01.82 | In this way, the two whose semen and ārtava are purified should follow the procedure (vidhi) stated earlier.
02.01.83 | The ṛtu (season), however, is that with ārtava seen, and it lasts twelve nights.
02.01.84 | According to others, sixteen nights.
02.01.85 | But, according to some, even a month, of (the one) whose yoni, womb (garbhāśaya) and ārtava are pure.
02.01.86 | According to others, in the same way there is also one with ārtava not seen.
02.01.87 | (A woman) with a lean, clear face, and with throbbing hips and breasts,
02.01.88 | One should know that woman to be in her ṛtu (season), a ṛtumatī who has drooping eyes and belly and desire for a man.
02.01.89 | As a lotus comes to contraction when the day has passed, so
02.01.90 | when the ṛtu (season) has passed, that yoni (vagina) does not receive semen (śukra) inside.
02.01.91 | Blood accumulated over a month, again in the ṛtu (season), by the two dhamanīs (vessels),
02.01.92 | slightly dark and ill-smelling, wind (vāyu) would discharge from the mouth of the yoni.
02.01.93 | Then, when the flower is seen (puṣpadarśana: the onset of menstruation), from the first day onward she should remain a chaste woman (brahmacāriṇī), without bathing and ornaments, sleeping on a spread of darbha grass, for three nights.
02.01.94 | And, in one of the two, a leaf-bowl or the palm of the hand, she should eat a little yāvaka (probably a barley preparation) cooked with milk, for the purpose of thinning (karśana).
02.01.95 | She should avoid sharp, hot, sour and salty (ones).
02.01.96 | On the fourth day, rubbed with unguent powder, bathed in cold water, anointed, adorned, wearing white garlands and garments, with auspicious rites and benedictions performed, with her mind on no other, she should look upon a husband of just this kind.
02.01.97 | For then, whatever kind (of one) she sees or thinks of, a (one) of that kind she bears.
02.01.98 | Then, after the bath, again, one desiring a son with good qualities should let four nights pass (on a conjectural reading).
02.01.99 | From the day of the flower (menstruation), seven nights (on a conjectural reading).
02.01.100 | Then, on the eighth, tenth or twelfth night, one desiring a son should lie together.
02.01.101 | On the fifth, seventh or ninth (night), one desiring a daughter.
02.01.102 | In these (nights), progressively, the offspring has the endowment of life-span, health, lordship, good fortune, strength, complexion (varṇa) and the sense faculties.
02.01.103 | But after this there is a progressive decrease of life-span and the rest.
02.01.104 | Among these, in the even nights the ārtava (menstrual blood) becomes small; in the odd (ones) it increases.
02.01.105 | Therefore in these (nights), in order, there is the birth of a son and of a daughter.
02.01.106 | And for this very reason one desiring a son should not approach a woman in whom the sight of the ārtava (menstrual blood) has not ceased, even on odd days.
02.01.107 | But if, owing to conformity to diet, there is an abundance of semen (śukra) in the odd (nights) and a deficiency in the even ones, then a male is born of female form, weak, or deficient in limb.
02.01.108 | And a female of male form, weak, or deficient in limb.
02.01.109 | But on the eleventh and thirteenth (nights) there would be a napuṃsaka (a person neither male nor female; eunuch).
02.01.110 | Then the teacher (upādhyāya) should perform the procedure relating to a son (putrīya).
02.01.111 | But for the purified woman, devoid of mantra.
02.01.112 | And the woman is to be told: 'Think upon the janapadas (regions or their peoples) that have the form, complexion and conduct of whatever kind of son you may wish for.'
02.01.113 | And she should follow the food, way of living (vihāra), practices (upacāra) and accessories (paricchada) of that region.
02.01.114 | And at the end of the rite, the man, chaste for a month, having eaten boiled śāli rice with ghee and milk, not over-fed, happy, wearing a garland, cheerful, with limbs refreshed, in white clothes, in a part of the night approved by the muhūrta-expert (astrologer), thinking of auspicious things, desirous of that, should mount the well-spread bed with the right foot, toward the east.
02.01.115 | And the woman, of that very kind, with limbs made thin and with oil and māṣa (black gram) as the chief food, should first ascend the bed with the left foot, on the right of the man.
02.01.116 | There one should employ the mantra.
02.01.117 | You are the ahi; you are life; you are the support on every side. May Dhātṛ place you; may Vidhātṛ place you. Be with brahma-splendour (brahmavarcas).
02.01.118 | Brahmā, Bṛhaspati, Viṣṇu, Soma, Sūrya, and also the two Aśvins,
02.01.119 | Bhaga, and then Mitra and Varuṇa: may they bestow on me a heroic son.
02.01.120 | Then, having soothed each other with gentle words, they two should lie together, with joy and agreeably.
02.01.121 | And at the end he should suddenly sprinkle her with cold water.
02.01.122 | In this matter, a woman who is over-fed, hungry, thirsty, frightened, dejected, afflicted by grief, angry, very fat, desiring another, or desiring no intercourse, does not bear a foetus (garbha), or bears a defective one.
02.01.123 | Likewise the man also; and he should not stay below.
02.01.124 | For thus a male is born with a woman's behaviour, or a female with a man's behaviour.
02.01.125 | And one should not resort to a woman lying face down or lying on her side.
02.01.126 | In one lying face down Vāta is strong; it presses (afflicts) the yoni (vagina).
02.01.127 | In one lying on the right side, Kapha (śleṣman), pressed and displaced, closes the womb (garbhāśaya).
02.01.128 | In one lying on the left side, similarly Pitta scorches the blood (rakta) and the semen (śukra).
02.01.129 | Therefore, lying on her back, she should receive the seed (bīja; probably semen).
02.01.130 | So here the doṣas remain just in their own places.
02.01.131 | And having known her to have obtained a foetus, on a conjectural reading before the foetus becomes manifest, one should employ the puṃsavana (rite for obtaining a male child) and the like in Puṣya (the lunar mansion Puṣya).
