05.06.1 | Now, therefore, we shall explain the siddhi (success, accomplishment) of the mishaps of basti (enema): the Bastivyāpattisiddhi.
05.06.2 | Vibandha (obstruction, retention), heaviness, ādhmāna (distension), head-pain, vāhana, and (ones) going upward.
05.06.3 | Belly-pain (kukṣiśūla), aṅga-ruci, dhmā, heart-pain, cutting pain (kartana) and flow (srava).
05.06.4 | From non-application (ayoga) and from over-application (atiyoga) of the basti (enema) there are six and six mishaps.
05.06.5 | Applied to one whose body is not oiled and not sweated, and whose koṣṭha (storehouse, belly) is heavy.
05.06.6 | Cold, or with little oil, salt, drug and quantity, or even thick.
05.06.7 | The basti, having agitated that doṣa and not expelling it because of weakness,
05.06.8 | Causes an extremely severe obstruction (vibandha) of wind (vāta), stool and urine.
05.06.9 | Pain in the basti (bladder, lower belly) and navel, ānāha (distension of the belly), hṛllepa (a "smearing" of the heart), and swelling in the anus.
05.06.10 | Itching, gaṇḍa (lumps, swellings), discolouration, restlessness (arati) and softness (mārdava) of the fire (vahni).
05.06.11 | The pair of decoctions prescribed earlier, in a middling doṣa, in one with atisāra (diarrhoea).
05.06.12 | Therefore, in that case the drinking of one, the hot one, is recommended.
05.06.13 | Phalavartis (suppositories), likewise sweatings (sveda), and, having known the time, purgation (virecana).
05.06.14 | Having bilva-root (wood-apple), trivṛt (turpeth), dāru (probably devadāru), yava (barley), kola (probably jujube) and kulattha (horse gram).
05.06.15 | The basti having surā and the others and urine, on a conjectural reading of the damaged word saprākūpeṣyaḥ, should bring it.
05.06.16 | In saṃśeṣa-āma (remaining āma), by a mild nirūha (decoction enema), stirred by the doṣas,
05.06.17 | It stupefies (mūrcchayati) the wind (anila), obstructs the path, and harms the fire (agni).
05.06.18 | Heaviness, fatigue, pain in the heart, burning, bewilderment and twisting (veṣṭana).
05.06.19 | Then one should do; one should treat him with drying (rūkṣa), sweating (sveda) and digestive (pācana) (ones).
05.06.20 | Water filled with pippalī (long pepper), kattṛṇa (a grass, identity uncertain), uśīra (vetiver), dāru (probably devadāru) and sūrvā (identity uncertain).
05.06.21 | One should drink it mixed with sauvarcala (black salt), kindling (dīpana) and cleansing in the heart.
05.06.22 | Or vacā (sweet flag), nāgara (dry ginger) and śaṭhī (identity disputed), mixed with curd-maṇḍa.
05.06.23 | They should be drunk with prasannā (a clear liquor), or with ariṣṭa or with āsava (fermented liquors).
05.06.24 | The first word, printed dārutrikaṭuukam, is damaged and is not translated, on a conjectural reading; pathyā (chebulic myrobalan), palāśa (flame of the forest), citraka (leadwort) and śaṭhī.
05.06.25 | Having ground (them) and kuṣṭha (costus) with urine, one should drink; and the kindling (dīpana) kṣāras.
05.06.26 | And one should administer to him a basti with urine, of the dāśamūla (the ten roots).
05.06.27 | Applied with little potency (vīrya), in one rich in doṣa, in a dry one, or in one with a harsh abode (āśaya).
05.06.28 | The basti, covered by doṣa and with its path blocked, may obstruct the wind.
05.06.29 | That wind, gone the wrong way, would cause distension (ādhmāna) and oppression of the vital parts (marma).
05.06.30 | Burning of the anus and koṣṭha (storehouse, belly), and pain in the testicles and groins.
05.06.31 | It obstructs the heart with pains, and runs here and there.
05.06.32 | In that case, to one whose body is well anointed and sweated, one should apply a varti (suppository).
05.06.33 | And the nirūha (a decoction enema) beginning with bilva should have pīlu (probably the toothbrush tree), sarṣapa (probably mustard) and urine.
05.06.34 | And an anuvāsana (oil enema) prepared with the damaged word saralāmadaruibhyām, on a conjectural reading beginning with sarala.
05.06.35 | In one with much doṣa and strength, with a harsh koṣṭha (storehouse, belly), a basti thin and mild,
05.06.36 | Cold, small and covered by the doṣas, it obstructs the wind.
05.06.37 | That wind (vāyu), moving upward along the body, would cause head-pain (headache).
05.06.38 | Stiffness of the neck, pratiśyāya (nasal catarrh), deafness and disturbance of vision.
05.06.39 | Him, smeared with hot oil and salt, sweated and kneaded.
05.06.40 | With sharp smokes, with pradhamana (powder-blowing), nasya (nasal medicines), and mouth-purgings (āsyavirecana),
05.06.41 | With purgations and bastis, quickly, one should treat (him) with ānulomika (following the natural direction) (ones).
05.06.42 | In (one) who is oiled and sweated, when the doṣa is great, a basti (enema) that is mild and has little drug.
05.06.43 | Having stirred up the doṣa, (it) would remove a little doṣa and would produce pravāhikā (dysentery with straining).
05.06.44 | Swelling (śopha) in the basti (bladder, lower belly) and in the apāna (anus), and sinking (sadana, weakness) in the thighs and shanks.
05.06.45 | The being (jantu) whose wind is obstructed strains repeatedly.
05.06.46 | Sweatings (sveda), anointings (abhyaṅga) and nirūhas (decoction enemas), cleansing (śodhanīya) and ānulomika (following the natural direction) (ones),
05.06.47 | One should prescribe, and, having subjected (him) to laṅghana (lightening, fasting), (prescribe) his regimen (vṛtti) like that of one purged.
05.06.48 | Causing holding back (saṃrodha) of the urge (vega), or pressed by excess quantity,
05.06.49 | Or a basti lacking unctuousness, salt (lavaṇa) and heat, small, with little drug,
05.06.50 | Or mild, thrown upward by the wind, from the mouth and nose,
05.06.51 | (It) comes out, producing fainting (mūrcchā), nausea (hṛllāsa), thirst, burning (dāha) and the others.
05.06.52 | Having seen his disorder (vikāra) of fainting, one should sprinkle the face with cold water.
05.06.53 | One should fan (him) until the destruction of the exhaustion (āklama), and should make (him) do prāṇāyāma (breath control).
05.06.54 | One should knead the back, sides and belly with hot hands, (him) face downward.
05.06.55 | Having pulled (him) by the hair, one should shake (him) and frighten (him) with vyāla (snakes or savage beasts) and fanged (ones).
05.06.56 | With weapons, firebrands (ulkā) and the king's men (rāja-puruṣa); for thus the basti goes downward.
05.06.57 | And one should make pressure (āpīḍa) on the throat with the hands and cloths, as if he were dying.
05.06.58 | For, from the obstruction of prāṇa and udāna, (the one) whose course is the more fully established,
05.06.59 | The apāna wind (pavana) quickly draws that basti away.
05.06.60 | And one should make (him) drink a paste (kalka) of kuṣṭha (costus) and kramuka (probably areca nut), joined with sour (amla).
05.06.61 | From heat, sharpness and flowing nature (saratva), that too would bring the basti into the natural direction (anulomayet).
05.06.62 | Or a paste (kalka) of trivṛt (turpeth) and pathyā (chebulic myrobalan) with cow's urine, a downward anulomana (bringing into the natural direction).
05.06.63 | When (it) is situated in the pakvāśaya (receptacle of the digested, the large intestine), in the sweated (one), a nirūha (decoction enema) of the daśamūla (the ten roots),
05.06.64 | And (it) is to be administered with yava (barley), kola (probably jujube) and kulattha (horse gram) prepared in urine.
05.06.65 | Or a basti with shoots (pallava) of vaṃśa (probably bamboo) together with amṛtā (probably giloy), cooked in cow's urine,
05.06.66 | With the bark and leaf of pūtīkarañja (identity disputed), śaṭhī (identity disputed), devāhva (on a conjectural reading; probably devadāru) and rohiṣa (probably palmarosa grass, Cymbopogon martinii).
05.06.67 | A basti with oil, jaggery and sindhūttha (rock salt), having a paste (kalka) of purgative drugs, cooked with the five roots beginning with bilva (wood-apple), when (it) is situated in the chest.
05.06.68 | When (it) is situated in the head: nāvana (nasal medication), smoke (dhūma); the head is to be covered with sarṣapa (mustard).
05.06.69 | A basti too hot, sharp, sour, thick, or of one excessively sweated,
05.06.70 | In a little doṣa, in a soft koṣṭha (storehouse, belly), or applied again and again,
05.06.71 | Having reached the state of over-application (atiyoga), it would become a cause of belly-pain (kukṣiśūla).
05.06.72 | That, similar in features (ākṛti) and treatment (sādhana) to over-application of purgation (virecana).
05.06.73 | Pṛśniparṇī (pithvan), sthirā (probably śālaparṇī), padma (probably lotus), kāśmarya (gambhārī), and madhūka (mahua) and utpala (probably blue lotus),
05.06.74 | Having ground drākṣā (grape or raisin) and madhūka (mahua), in milk (and) in rice-washing,
05.06.75 | In the clear part (prasāda) of drākṣā, of pakvaloṣṭa (a baked or ripe clod; sense uncertain), or of madhuka (probably liquorice),
05.06.76 | Having added (it), one should apply the basti with ghee in burning (dāha) arising from over-application (atiyoga).
05.06.77 | Without having prepared (him) by oleation (sneha) and sweating (sveda); heavy, sharp, of excessive quantity (one),
05.06.78 | A basti introduced for one badly placed (duḥsthita), or for one lying badly (duḥśayita),
05.06.79 | Not properly set going (avipravṛtta), (it) would provoke the wind (marut); that (basti), gone by a wrong path,
05.06.80 | (It) produces pain of the limbs (aṅga-ruj), yawning (jṛmbhā), stiffness (stambha) and splitting (bheda) of the joints.
05.06.81 | Him, smeared (abhyakta) with oil and salt, sweated with saṃstara and the others,
05.06.82 | With bilva (wood-apple), kola (probably jujube), yava (barley), eraṇḍa (castor), varṣābhū (punarnavā) and the pair of bṛhatī (the two prickly nightshades); kola and bṛhatī are on a conjectural reading of the misprinted words.
05.06.83 | With kulattha (horse gram), boiled mastu (curd-water, whey), with (ones) joined with phala (fruit) and sauvīraka (a sour fermented gruel).
05.06.84 | He should give an āsthāpana (decoction enema) with rock salt (sindhūttha), to (one) who has eaten with jāṅgala (dry-country) juices (rasa).
05.06.85 | On a conjectural reading: he should give an anuvāsana (oil enema) with oil well cooked with the Vāta-conquering (anilajit, lit. wind-conquering) dhanya (unclear).
05.06.86 | In (one) with a soft koṣṭha (belly), in the weak (one), a basti (enema) that is very sharp and over-expelling,
05.06.87 | (it) would cause hiccup (hidhmā). In that (case), whatever is hiccup-destroying and nourishing (bṛṃhaṇa) is beneficial.
05.06.88 | With the juices (rasa) of balā (probably Sida cordifolia), sthirā (identity disputed; perhaps śālaparṇī, Desmodium gangeticum) and the rest, kāśmarya (Gmelina arborea) and triphalā (the three myrobalans), and with rock salts (saindhava),
05.06.89 | Having cooked oil with prasannā (the clear upper layer of surā liquor), āranāla (a sour fermented gruel) and sour (ones), he should give an anuvāsana (oil enema).
05.06.90 | He should give (it) smeared (mixed) with hot water and with pippalī (long pepper), joined with salt.
05.06.91 | With smoke (dhūma), linctus (leha), juice (rasa), milk and fomentation (sveda), and also food that drives away Vāta.
05.06.92 | (A basti) that is very sharp, or with vāta (wind, air), or not properly pressed,
05.06.93 | The basti (enema) would strike the heart. In that (case), with kāśa (Saccharum spontaneum), kuśa (Desmostachya bipinnata) and utkaṭa (unclear),
05.06.94 | It would be with sour (ones), salt, skandha (trunk; unclear here), karīra (disputed: bamboo shoot, or the shrub Capparis decidua) and badarī (jujube) fruits,
05.06.95 | A basti with boiled (ones) is beneficial, siddha (cooked), and with Vāta-destroying anuvāsanas (oil enemas).
05.06.96 | For (one) with a soft koṣṭha (belly) and little doṣa, (a basti that is) dry, sharp and excessive in quantity,
05.06.97 | having quickly destroyed the malas (wastes) of the basti, would aggravate Vāta and Pitta.
05.06.98 | They, indeed, cutting his navel, basti (probably the bladder) and anus again and again,
05.06.99 | On a conjectural reading: discolouration, a little-by-little arising (utthāna); the basti would be scraping (nirlekhana).
05.06.100 | In that (case), with sweet and cool drugs (auṣadha), and milk boiled with sugarcane (ikṣu) and the rest,
05.06.101 | With the pastes (kalka) of yaṣṭyāhva (licorice) and tila (sesame), a basti would be for (one) who eats milk.
05.06.102 | Equal sarja-rasa (probably the resin of the sal tree), yaṣṭyāhva (licorice), jiṅgiṇī (probably Lannea coromandelica), kardama (mud) and añjana (collyrium; kind unclear),
05.06.103 | Having put (these) into milk, a basti would be for (one) who eats a distinctly sour taste (rasa).
05.06.104 | A slimy (picchila) basti is beneficial, and a sneha (oil or fat) boiled with sweet (ones).
05.06.105 | A basti (enema) that is kṣāra, sour, sharp, hot and salty, or (that) of (one) who is paittika (of Pitta),
05.06.106 | Burning, scraping and damaging the anus, it makes a flow (parisrava) for him.
05.06.107 | It discharges blood together with the burnt (vidagdha), with colours, and Pitta, abundantly.
05.06.108 | And many times, with excessive urge (vega), (he) goes into moha (bewilderment, fainting), repeatedly.
05.06.109 | In that (case), on a conjectural reading: goat's milk boiled with crushed, fresh śāmalī (probably śālmalī, the silk-cotton tree) tantra (threads, fibres; unclear),
05.06.110 | Strained, joined with ghee, he should give a basti (enema), and other slimy (ones).
05.06.111 | Thus is the kalpa (preparation) in the sprouts of vaṭa (banyan) and the rest, and in yava (barley) and tila (sesame).
05.06.112 | It is recommended in suvarcalā (identity disputed; perhaps Cleome viscosa) and upodaka (probably Malabar spinach, Basella alba), and in karbudāra (probably Bauhinia variegata; disputed).
05.06.113 | And in the anus, he should make cool and sweet pourings (seka) and coatings (pralepana).
05.06.114 | And here, the procedure (kriyā) that destroys raktapitta (a bleeding disorder) and atisāra (diarrhoea) is commended.
05.06.115 | In dāha (burning) and the rest, he should drink a paste (kalka) of trivṛt (turpeth) with mṛdvīkā water (grape water).
05.06.116 | That, indeed, having destroyed Pitta, faeces and Vāta, would conquer (those) beginning with burning (dāha).
05.06.117 | And, purified, he should drink a cool yavāgū (thin gruel) joined with sugar (śarkarā).
05.06.118 | Or he should employ food for (one) who is over-purged (and) whose stool is depleted.
05.06.119 | Kulmāṣa (probably a preparation of partly cooked grain) with māṣa (black gram) soup (yūṣa); as drink, curd or else surā (an intoxicating drink).
05.06.120 | He who, in pain and with aruci (distaste for food), would pass raw, putrid (kuṇapa) (ones),
05.06.121 | On a conjectural reading: he should drink (them) together with ghana (probably musta), ativiṣā (Aconitum heterophyllum), kuṣṭha (costus), nadāru (unclear) and vacā (sweet flag).
05.06.122 | (He) who is made to flow out faeces, Vāta, blood, Pitta or Kapha,
05.06.123 | Cooked (pakva); in that (case), a basti of its own group (svavargīya) is best, (what is) conquered by the physician (bhiṣagjita).
05.06.124 | Of these six, from the combination of two, there are thirty varieties.
05.06.125 | Together with the single (ones), one should know thirty-six, also with their complications (upadrava).
05.06.126 | Pain (śūla), pravāhikā (straining at stool, tenesmus), distension (ādhmāna), cutting pain (parikarti), loss of appetite (aruci) and fevers (jvara).
05.06.127 | Thirst (tṛṣ), bewilderment (moha), burning (dāha), fainting (mūrcchā) and so on one should know as the complications (upadrava) of these.
05.06.128 | There, in āma (undigested matter), the drink — on a conjectural reading of the unclear word 'tara' — joined with pungent, sour and salty (ones),
05.06.129 | A digestive (pācana) is recommended; in āma, basti (enema) is indeed forbidden.
05.06.130 | A basti (enema) of the group of wind-destroying and binding (grāhin) (ones) is recommended in stool.
05.06.131 | An oil enema (sneha-basti), sweet and sour, with the salt evident, in wind (samīraṇa).
05.06.132 | In blood, with blood, in Pitta-and-blood (pittāsra): astringent, sweet and bitter.
05.06.133 | When Kapha is being made to move (sāryamāṇa), the basti is astringent, pungent and bitter.
05.06.134 | With stool, or with wind, or with āma; in stool, or else with winds.
05.06.135 | In the mixed (case), there should be the drink — on a conjectural reading of the unclear word 'tara' — joined with pungent, sour and salty (ones).
05.06.136 | With Pitta, in āma, with blood, likewise; or again, of those two, with āma.
05.06.137 | Of the two that are mixed, there should be a drink with pungent, sweet and bitter (tastes).
05.06.138 | Likewise, in āma mixed with Kapha, (one) astringent, pungent and bitter.
05.06.139 | But when Kapha is joined with āma: a medicine (auṣadha), astringent and — on a conjectural reading of the doubtful word 'lava' — [salty].
05.06.140 | With Vāta, in stool or in Pitta; in stool, Pitta and blood, likewise in wind,
05.06.141 | In the mixed (case), a basti astringent, sour and sweet would be best.
05.06.142 | With Vāta and in blood, the basti should be done sweet, sour and bitter.
05.06.143 | Of stool and blood, of Pitta and stool, of blood and Pitta,
05.06.144 | In mutual combination, the basti is astringent, sweet and bitter.
05.06.145 | With Kapha, in stool or in Pitta; in Kapha, with stool, Pitta and blood,
05.06.146 | In the mixed (case), a basti pungent, bitter and astringent would be best.
05.06.147 | In Kapha mixed with wind, there should be a basti pungent, bitter and sour.
05.06.148 | But in blood mingled with Kapha: sweet, uṣaṇa (pungent) and bitter.
05.06.149 | In wind mixed with Kapha, it should be pungent, sour and salty.
05.06.150 | By combining three, four, five and six, one should vary (it) in just this way.
05.06.151 | And this method (yukti), stated for atisāra (diarrhoea), is held also in all diseases.
05.06.152 | At once, on a conjectural reading of the unclear word 'sadrusam', in the combination of six, there should be a pācana (digestive).
05.06.153 | But of the five (ones) free of āma, a basti having six tastes is beneficial.
05.06.154 | Unripe fruits (śalāṭu) of udumbara (cluster fig), the barks of jambū (probably jamun), āmra (mango) and udumbara.
05.06.155 | Conch shell (śaṅkha), sarjarasa (resin of the sal tree), lac (lākṣā) and kattṛṇa (a grass, unidentified), and with a pala as the portion.
05.06.156 | Having ground (them), one should cook with these a prastha (a measure) of ghee, doubled with milk.
05.06.157 | In all atisāras (diarrhoeas), this is to be drunk according to strength.
05.06.158 | With kacchurā, dhātakī (woodfordia), bilva (bael), samaṅgā (probably touch-me-not; disputed) and raktamūlin,
05.06.159 | And with masūra (lentil) and aśvattha (sacred fig) tips, boiled in water, there should be a yavāgū (gruel).
05.06.160 | With the sprouts (pallava) of young udumbara, kaṭvaṅga (probably Oroxylum indicum; disputed), samaṅgā and plakṣa (probably a fig, pilkhan),
05.06.161 | And with masūra, dhātakī flowers and balā (country mallow), it should be likewise.
05.06.162 | Mishaps (vyāpad) of various kinds arise from an error (vibhrama) in the basti.
05.06.163 | According to the path, according to the condition, one should carry out the treatment (sādhana) of those.
05.06.164 | Thus the sixth chapter, named Bastivyāpatsiddhi.
