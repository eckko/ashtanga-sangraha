02.03.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the Śārīra (on the body) named Garbhopacaraṇīya (the care to be given to the embryo).
02.03.2 | In the first month the pregnant woman should drink milk that has been prepared, cool, in measure, at the time.
02.03.3 | In that (month) also, for the first twelve nights, she should drink ghee (sarpis) arising from milk, boiled with śāliparṇī (Desmodium gangeticum) and palāśa (probably Butea monosperma), having as after-drink (anupāna) cool water boiled with gold and silver.
02.03.4 | On a conjectural reading, one should have her take as food, in the evening and in the morning, (ones) that are sweet and cool, mostly liquid and suitable (sātmya).
02.03.5 | And she should not resort to oil massage (abhyaṅga) or rubbing with powders (udvartana).
02.03.6 | On a conjectural reading, she should avoid, up to the fifth month, the doṣa-causing (ones) as stated.
02.03.7 | Especially in the second (month), she should drink milk cooked with sweet medicines (madhura-auṣadha).
02.03.8 | In the third (month), the same, with ghee (sarpis) and honey (madhu).
02.03.9 | In the fourth (month), joined with fresh butter (navanīta) in the measure of an akṣa.
02.03.10 | In the fifth (month), milk-ghee (kṣīrasarpis), ghee of milk.
02.03.11 | In the sixth (month), that same, cooked with sweet medicines (madhura-auṣadha).
02.03.12 | And in the seventh (month).
02.03.13 | And in that (month), 'burning (vidāha) of the mother occurs through the arising of the embryo's hair,' so women say; 'not so,' (says) the venerable Ātreya.
02.03.14 | But from the pressure of the embryo, the doṣas, having reached the heart (hṛdaya), burn; then itching arises, (and) kaṇḍūkikisa (kaṇḍū, itching, + kikisa, probably stretch marks; compound unclear).
02.03.15 | In that case, with water of kola (probably jujube), one should give her a palm's measure of fresh butter (navanīta) cooked with sweet medicines.
02.03.16 | And with pastes (kalka) of candana (sandalwood) and mṛṇāla (lotus stalk), she should rub the breast and belly.
02.03.17 | Or with powders (cūrṇa) of śirīṣa (Albizia lebbeck), dhātakī (Woodfordia fruticosa, on a conjectural reading), mustard (sarṣapa) and madhuka (probably licorice).
02.03.18 | Or with pastes (kalka) of the seeds of kuṭaja (Holarrhena antidysenterica) and arjaka (probably a basil), mustā (nut grass, Cyperus rotundus) and haridrā (turmeric); or with pastes of nimba (neem), kola (probably jujube), surasā (probably holy basil) and mañjiṣṭhā (Indian madder).
02.03.19 | Oil massage (abhyaṅga) with water cooked with hare (śaśa), deer (hariṇa), punarnavā (Boerhavia diffusa), mālatī (identity disputed; possibly a jasmine) and madhuka (probably licorice); with triphalā (the three fruits) mixed with the blood of the spotted deer (pṛṣata); and with oil cooked with a paste of karavīra leaves (oleander).
02.03.20 | Pouring (pariṣeka) again with water cooked with mālatī (identity disputed; possibly a jasmine) and madhuka (probably licorice).
02.03.21 | She should avoid scratching.
02.03.22 | When the skin splits, for the purpose of avoiding disfigurement, she should make use of bathing, rubbing with powders (udvartana) and sandalwood (candana).
02.03.23 | And she should eat sweet food, little in quantity, with little oil or fat (sneha) and salt, with little water as after-drink (anupāna).
02.03.24 | In the eighth (month) she should drink milk gruel (yavāgū) containing ghee.
02.03.25 | 'Not so,' ṣaṇḍhakātho (unclear), on a conjectural reading, from fear of the affliction of tawny colour to the embryo.
02.03.26 | There is the affliction of tawny colour; even so, one should act thus.
02.03.27 | She produces a child free of disease, endowed with the excellence of strength, complexion, sattva and body-build (saṃhanana), reckoned foremost among his kinsmen: so (says) the venerable Ātreya.
02.03.28 | One should administer an āsthāpana (decoction enema) with jujube (badara) water joined with palala (identity disputed; possibly sesame paste), milk, curd, whey (mastu), oil, salt, fruit, ghee and honey.
02.03.29 | On a conjectural reading, for clearing the old stool, and with oil cooked with sweet medicines beginning with madhuka (probably licorice), one should administer an anuvāsana (oil enema), for the downward movement (anulomana) of the wind (vāyu).
02.03.30 | For when the wind is moving downward, she gives birth easily, free of complications (upadrava).
02.03.31 | But to the pregnant woman, face down (nyubja), one should administer either an āsthāpana (decoction enema) or an anuvāsana (oil enema).
02.03.32 | In that way, because of the openness of her passage, the medicine (auṣadha) enters properly.
02.03.33 | After this, until the time of delivery, one should treat (her) with unctuous gruels (yavāgū) and with the juices (broths) of jāṅgala (dry-land) animals: so (says) the venerable Dhanvantari.
02.03.34 | In the ninth (month), however, from that same anuvāsana oil, a picu (cotton swab) should be inserted into the yoni (vagina), for the oiling of the passage of the embryo and the receptacle (āśaya, probably the uterus).
02.03.35 | By this, beginning from the first month, step by step, at the time of delivery the belly (kukṣi) that bears the embryo, the waist (kaṭī), the flank (pārśva) and the back (pṛṣṭha) of the pregnant woman become soft.
02.03.36 | And the wind (vāyu) becomes anuloma (moving in the proper, downward direction).
02.03.37 | And urine and stool become natural, and the jarāyu (afterbirth: placenta with membranes) goes to the passage.
02.03.38 | And the strong woman gives birth at the time, easily, to a desired son, healthy, long-lived and happy.
02.03.39 | On a conjectural reading, one should prescribe daily bathing for the pregnant woman with a cooled decoction (kvātha) of bilva (bael), kārpāsī (cotton), phaṃpheṇā (unclear), pāṭalī (Stereospermum suaveolens), picumanda (neem), agnimantha (probably Premna), māṃsī (probably spikenard), vardhamānaka (identity disputed) and patrabhaṅga (unclear), or with water of all fragrant substances (sarvagandha).
02.03.40 | And even before her ninth month, on a conjectural reading, one should have a delivery chamber (sūtikāgāra) made in an auspicious place free of bones, gravel and potsherds, approved by the science of building sites (vāstu-vidyā), comfortable in all seasons, furnished with all equipment, with fire kept at hand, with a door to the east or to the north.
02.03.41 | On a conjectural reading: there, when the stars and the rest are favourable, with the utterance of puṇyāha (auspicious-day formula), but circling clockwise round the cows, brahmins and elders, having entered, surrounded by (ones) who have given birth many times, anuktābhiḥ and avipādābhiḥ (unclear), able to bear hardship, devoted to auspicious rites (svastyayana), with wind, urine and stool made to move properly by regulating food and conduct, she should await the time of delivery.
02.03.42 | And even in a slight obstruction (vibandha) of stool and urine, one should insert phalavartis (suppositories).
02.03.43 | Then, in her whose delivery is near, there occur exhaustion (klama), weariness (glāni), the two eyes as if with released fastenings, abundance of spitting, urine and stool, looseness of the belly, heaviness below, lack of desire for food, pain (vedanā) in the belly (udara), heart (hṛdaya), waist, bladder (basti) and groin; and pricking (toda), tearing (bheda), pain (śūla), throbbing (sphuraṇa) and flows (sravaṇa) of the yoni occur.
02.03.44 | After that, the appearance of the āvīs (probably labour pains) and the flow of the water of the embryo (garbhodaka: amniotic fluid).
02.03.45 | Then one should make her drink gruel (yavāgū) with ghee, her to whom the embryo has come forward, who has performed the kautuka and auspicious rites, with a male-named fruit (puṃnāma-phala, unidentified) in her hand, well anointed with oil and sprinkled with hot water.
02.03.46 | Then women with new (unworn) clothes, not to be suspected, pleasant to look at, advanced in age, skilled in delivery, bold, with cut nails, should attend upon the sūtikā (woman in labour) with comforting, pleasant and truthful words, her lying on a soft bed on the ground covered with a very red bull's hide, face upward, with bent thighs and back.
02.03.47 | On a conjectural reading: then one should give her, to smell again and again, a powder of kuṣṭha (costus), elā (small cardamom), lāṅgalī (glory lily), vacā (sweet flag), cyanya (unclear), citraka (leadwort) and cirabilva (probably the Indian elm).
02.03.48 | Likewise, the smoke of any one of birch bark (bhūrjapatra), śiśipā (sissoo) or the sap (rasa) of sarja (sāl), and at intervals.
02.03.49 | Having anointed the regions of her sides, back, waist and thighs with lukewarm oil, one should knead them comfortably.
02.03.50 | Thus the fetus (garbha) turns around, downward.
02.03.51 | Its sign (lakṣaṇa).
02.03.52 | Having left the heart (hṛdaya), it enters the belly (udara) and takes hold of the head of the bladder (basti, probably).
02.03.53 | On a conjectural reading: they urge her on, āḍhyaḥ (unclear). If it does not turn in this way, then she should be made to do yawning and walking about again and again, and the procedure stated just before.
02.03.54 | Others, however, say that a mortar filled with grain is to be struck with a pestle.
02.03.55 | That, however, is not right.
02.03.56 | For the avoidance of severe exertion is constantly prescribed for a pregnant woman.
02.03.57 | On a conjectural reading: and especially at the time of delivery, in her whose dhātus and doṣas have all been set in motion, because of delicacy (trāryā, unclear), the wind (vāyu), stirred up by muśalyāyāma (unclear), having found an opening, might injure the vital breaths (prāṇa).
02.03.58 | Then a skilled woman, having placed her whose fetus has turned on a couch (paryaṅka), and, from the foot-side of her as she sits, having anointed the yoni (vagina) comfortably in the natural direction (anuloma), should press the two buttocks (sphij) with the two feet.
02.03.59 | And she should stretch out the yoni (vagina) again and again.
02.03.60 | On a conjectural reading: and she should say, "O fortunate one, bear down (pravāhaṇa) gently, gently; the colour of your face is very clear; you will bear a son."
02.03.61 | Another (woman), however, should mutter this mantra in her left ear.
02.03.62 | Earth, water, sky (viyat), fire (tejas), wind, Viṣṇu, Prajāpati.
02.03.63 | May (it) always protect you, together with the fetus, or also grant freedom from the dart (śalya).
02.03.64 | Give birth, you who are unharmed, to the unharmed (one), O fair-faced one.
02.03.65 | A son having the splendour of Kārtikeya, protected by Kārtikeya.
02.03.66 | Likewise.
02.03.67 | Here: nectar (amṛta), and Soma, and Citrabhānu, O lady.
02.03.68 | And Uccaiḥśravas, the horse: may (they) dwell in your house.
02.03.69 | This nectar (amṛta), indeed drawn up from the waters: may it quickly release this fetus of yours, O woman.
02.03.70 | On a conjectural reading: therefore may fire (anala), wind (pavana), sun (arka) and Indra (Vāsava), together with the bearers of salt water, grant you peace (śānti).
02.03.71 | Likewise another (woman) should instruct (her).
02.03.72 | When the pain (vedanā) has not come, do not bear down.
02.03.73 | For bearing down at the wrong time (akāla-pravāhaṇa) is like forcing out the urges (vega) of feces, urine and the rest; it is useless and unwholesome for the fetus, because it causes breathlessness (śvāsa), cough (kāsa), wasting (śoṣa), hunchedness (kubjatā) and so on.
02.03.74 | Likewise, not bearing down when there is pain (vedanā), like the holding back of those very (urges), is for injury.
02.03.75 | Then do as stated, gently, gently, and at first bear down.
02.03.76 | Strongly at the exit of the fetus; more strongly on arrival at the mouth of the yoni, until delivery.
02.03.77 | For that woman who is bearing down, they should raise a cry: "She has given birth, she has given birth, to a blessed, blessed son!"
02.03.78 | Thus her vital breaths (prāṇa) are nourished by joy.
02.03.79 | In garbha-saṅga (retention of the fetus; probably obstructed labour), one should fumigate the yoni (vagina) with the sloughed skin of a black snake, or with piṇḍītaka (probably the emetic nut).
02.03.80 | And one should tie the root of hiraṇyapuṣpī (unidentified) on the hand and foot.
02.03.81 | And one should hold suvarcalā (identity disputed) or viśalyā (probably the same as lāṅgalī).
02.03.82 | Now, if the placenta (aparā) of one who has delivered does not fall out, then, pressing firmly with the right hand from above the navel and gripping from behind, one should shake.
02.03.83 | Or one should strike the hip (śroṇi) with the heel.
02.03.84 | Or, having gripped the two buttocks (sphij), one should press them thoroughly.
02.03.85 | With a braid, or with a finger wound round with hair, one should touch the throat (kaṇṭha) and palate (tālu).
02.03.86 | And one should fumigate the yoni (vagina) with birch bark (bhūrjapatra), kāca-maṇi (literally 'glass-gem'; sense doubtful) and snake slough, or with bhūrja and guggulu (Indian bdellium).
02.03.87 | Or, having anointed the yoni (vagina) with ghee cooked with the root of śāli (a variety of rice), one should fumigate with probably bitter bottle gourd (kaṭukālābu), jālinī (a Luffa; disputed), nimba (neem) and snake slough.
02.03.88 | Or, for one not anointed, one should smear with (ones) mixed with kaṭu oil (probably mustard oil), made into a paste (kalka), or smeared with oil.
02.03.89 | Or with a paste (kalka) of jaggery (guḍa) and dry ginger (nāgara).
02.03.90 | Or one should eat that very (paste).
02.03.91 | Or with a paste (kalka) of lāṅgalī (glory lily) root one should smear the hand and the belly (udara).
02.03.92 | Or one should pour the milk (kṣīra) of mahāvṛkṣa (disputed; probably snuhī, a spurge) over the head.
02.03.93 | Kuṣṭha (costus) and tālī (unclear), with paste (kalka), should be given to drink with any one of bilva (bael), bilvaja (unclear), soup (yūṣa), maireya, āsava, surā and surā-maṇḍa; or with a sour (one) that is sharp and hot with vyoṣa (the three pungents); or with horse-gram (kulattha) juice; or with a cooking together of madhuka (probably licorice) and pippalī (long pepper).
02.03.94 | Or a paste (kalka) of pippalī (long pepper), elā (probably cardamom), kola (probably jujube fruit), viḍa (drug name), cavya (probably Piper chaba), citraka (leadwort) and upakuñcikā (probably nigella; identity disputed).
02.03.95 | Having taken out the right ear of a living donkey or bull, and having put it into any one of the dips (āplavana) beginning with bilva, bilvaja and yūṣa, and having taken it out after it has stayed a muhūrta, one should give that dip to drink.
02.03.96 | Or a paste (kalka) of kuṣṭha (costus) and elā (probably cardamom) with surā (grain liquor).
02.03.97 | Or a decoction of arka (calotrope), ala (unclear) and arka, mixed with surā.
02.03.98 | Or a paste (kalka) of kuṣṭha (costus) and the root of lāṅgalī (probably gloriosa lily), with either madya (an intoxicating drink) or urine.
02.03.99 | Or the powder of the group beginning with vatsaka, with madya (an intoxicating drink).
02.03.100 | And a picu (cotton wad) of oil cooked with śatapuṣpā (probably dill), kuṣṭha, madana (probably emetic nut) and hiṅgu (asafoetida) should be made to be held.
02.03.101 | And then, at the time of anuvāsana (oil enema), one should give her an anuvāsana.
02.03.102 | And with the dips (āplavana) beginning with bilva, bilvaja and yūṣa (soup).
02.03.103 | One should give an āsthāpana (decoction enema) with (ones) furnished with phala, jīmūtaka (probably a Luffa gourd), ikṣvāku (probably bitter bottle gourd), dhāmārgava (identity disputed), kuṭaja, ākai (unclear), kṛtavedhana (probably ridge gourd) and hastiparṇī (unidentified).
02.03.104 | With (one) mixed with siddhārthaka (probably white mustard), kuṣṭha, lāṅgalī and the milk (kṣīra) of mahāvṛkṣa (identity disputed), or with surā-maṇḍa; the stalks of śālmalī (silk-cotton tree) or striñcāni (unclear) having been pounded, one should crush (them) in a decoction of the five roots (pañcamūla).
02.03.105 | And with that, strained, oily, joined with phala and the rest, with pungent oil (kaṭutaila; probably mustard oil) cooked with the substances of the āsthāpana as stated, one should give an uttarabasti (urethral or uterine injection).
02.03.106 | Or one should give an anuvāsana (oil enema) with pungent oil (kaṭutaila; probably mustard oil) cooked with the paste of śigru (drumstick), sumukha (probably a basil), marica (black pepper), ajājī (probably cumin) and citraka, with milk and with cow's urine.
02.03.107 | Or, having filled the yoni (female genital passage) with picchā of umā (probably flax) and śālmalī together with ghee, one should shake.
02.03.108 | Or with an oiled hand with the nails cut, one should remove, following the mālā (garland or row; sense unclear).
02.03.109 | If, in one who has given birth, there is a pain called makkala in the basti (bladder) and muddhodareṣu (unclear).
02.03.110 | In that case one should drink the powder of yavakṣāra (kṣāra of barley; probably impure potassium carbonate) with ghee, or with pleasantly warm water.
02.03.111 | Or the group beginning with varaṇa, together with pañcakola (long pepper, its root, cavya, citraka and dry ginger), elā (probably cardamom) and atīvāpa (unclear).
02.03.112 | Or else (one) having as pratīvāpa (added substance) the groups beginning with vīratara and beginning with mūṣaka.
02.03.113 | Or salt powder with a decoction (niryūha) of the group beginning with vatsaka.
02.03.114 | Or the group beginning with vatsaka, with madirā (an intoxicating drink).
02.03.115 | Or with a decoction of the aṃśumatī pair (probably two plants, śālaparṇī and pṛśniparṇī; identity disputed), or a paste (kalka) of marica (black pepper) and bhadradāru (deodar).
02.03.116 | Or a paste (kalka) of bhāīīṃ (unclear), nāgara (dry ginger) and devadāru (deodar), with warm water.
02.03.117 | Or one should eat old jaggery (guḍa) mixed with the powder of trikaṭu, trijātaka and dhanikā (probably coriander).
02.03.118 | If her yoni (female genital passage) should slip from its own place.
02.03.119 | Then, having anointed the yoni with the blood of anyone whatever, a skilled woman should place it back in its proper place.
02.03.120 | And the placed (one) should be fomented many times with oil well cooked with a paste of aśoka, rohiṇī (identity disputed), barhiṣa (unidentified), uśīra, priyaṅgu (probably Callicarpa macrophylla; identity disputed) and devadāru, and filled.
02.03.121 | And one should say: "Go, O fortunate one, to your own place."
02.03.122 | In the same way any one woman should bring forth (on a conjectural reading) the child just born, by the procedure for the care of a child.
02.03.123 | But that will be taught in the later (ones).
02.03.124 | Then one should anoint the sūtikā (woman who has recently given birth) with balā-oil (balā: probably country mallow).
02.03.125 | And a dose (mātrā) of sneha (oil or fat), joined with pañcakola powder, or with the powder of yavānī (ajwain), upakuñcikā (probably nigella; identity disputed), cavya, citraka, vyoṣa and saindhava (rock salt), digesting within the day, should be given to the hungry (one) to drink, according to suitability (sātmya).
02.03.126 | For one unfit for sneha (oil or fat), a decoction of vāta-relieving medicines, or a decoction of the lesser pañcamūla (the five small roots: śālaparṇī, pṛśniparṇī, bṛhatī, kaṇṭakārī and gokṣura); and for one who has drunk, having anointed with yamaka (probably a mixture of oil and ghee), one should wrap the belly with cloth.
02.03.127 | Thus wind (vāyu) would not produce an alteration of the belly (udara), because of the absence of space.
02.03.128 | But when the sneha has been digested, a yavāgū (gruel) cooked with the very same earlier medicines, or with a decoction of the group beginning with vidārī, or with milk, well boiled and liquid, should be given to drink in measure.
02.03.129 | And before the drinking of sneha and of yavāgū, at both times, one should sprinkle with hot water.
02.03.130 | Having kept this up for three nights, five nights, or samarātra (unclear), one should then nourish (her) gradually with a soup (yūṣa) of barley, jujube (kola) and horse gram (kulattha), with light food and drink, and after twelve nights with jāṅgala broths and the like, having regard to the digestive fire (agni), strength and so on.
02.03.131 | And water that has been boiled and cooled should be given to drink.
02.03.132 | Likewise one should attend (to her) with (ones) cooked with life-promoting (jīvanīya), nourishing (bṛṃhaṇīya), sweet and vāta-relieving (ones), through anointing (abhyaṅga), ucchādana (unclear), sprinkling (pariṣeka) and immersion (avagāha), and with agreeable foods and drinks.
02.03.133 | For thus, on a conjectural reading, from the body whose dhātus of the whole body have been wasted (kṣapita) and made slack (śithila) by the growth of the foetus, and which is emptied of pravāhuṇa (unclear), pain, moisture (kleda), the outflow of blood, in particular, (she) becomes new again. And for this very reason the disease of the sūtikā becomes hard to cure or incurable. Therefore she should be treated with effort by the procedure as stated.
02.03.134 | Thus, treated for one and a half months and gradually released from the restraint of food and conduct, she would be one from whom the name sūtikā has gone.
02.03.135 | From the reappearance of the ārtava (menses), so some (say).
02.03.136 | Thus, taking the first month, as long as (she) is again capable of a foetus (garbha).
02.03.137 | The woman would be (so); until then the whole procedure has been shown well in every way.
02.03.138 | Thus the third chapter, named Garbhopacaraṇīya.
