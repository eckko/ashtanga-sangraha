02.09.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter on the knowledge of abnormal body parts (vikṛtāṅgavijñānīya).
02.09.2 | The doṣas (humours) that have gone beyond the range of medicine and pervade the whole body, in men, whether healthy or afflicted, who desire another body, indicating the decay of life as clouds (indicate) rain, suddenly produce a reversal of natural state.
02.09.3 | In brief, they call that riṣṭa (an ill-omen, a sign of approaching death).
02.09.4 | Among these: fair or not fair, dusky or not dusky, black or not black: these are the colours of the body's natural state.
02.09.5 | And its varieties are lotus-fair (padmagaura) and the like.
02.09.6 | But blue, dusky, copper-coloured, turmeric-yellow, white and the like are abnormalities. Among these, the natural-state colour and the abnormal colour, unclear (damaged print: yugapadgaṃdhegaikadeśe, followed by vā 'or'), are for death.
02.09.7 | By this, the being otherwise without cause of fading, growth (on a conjectural reading), grief, joy, dryness, unctuousness and the like, and likewise of all the states stated in the (passage) on varieties of prakṛti (large, plump, fine, long) and determined by the division of doṣas etc., is stated.
02.09.8 | As for the chāyās (shades or lustres), there are five in the body.
02.09.9 | Among these, unclear (damaged print: khādamlakhaṅgākāśanīlanirmalā); unctuous, as if with lustre (prabhā).
02.09.10 | From wind (vāta): dry as dust and ash, dust-reddish, dark brown, lacking lustre, smelling of sweat-waste (sveda-mala).
02.09.11 | From tejas (fire): unclear (damaged print: hutahutāśavidyutasatapanīyaraktā, ending in raktā 'red'); dṛṣṭimukhā (with eye and face).
02.09.12 | From water (ambhas): clear as vaidūrya (beryl or cat's-eye; identity disputed) and, on a conjectural reading, indranīla (probably sapphire); dense, very unctuous.
02.09.13 | From earth: of pleasing lustre, dense, steady, unctuous, on a conjectural reading pure, dusky and white.
02.09.14 | Of these, the one of vāyu (wind) is for destruction and for very great affliction; the remaining ones, however, are those with a rise of happiness (sukhodaya).
02.09.15 | There are seven prabhās (radiances): red, yellow, pale, white, black, dark brown and green.
02.09.16 | Those (that are) unctuous, spreading and stainless are auspicious; the opposite (ones) are inauspicious.
02.09.17 | The distinction between the two.
02.09.18 | The chāyā overspreads the colour; on a conjectural reading it is perceived as near; and it consists of the five elements (bhūta).
02.09.19 | The prabhā, however, illuminates the colour; it is perceived from afar; and it is just the radiance of tejas (fire).
02.09.20 | He in whose eye the little girl (kumārikā, probably the small figure seen in the pupil) made of reflection is not seen is parāsu (one whose life-breath has gone).
02.09.21 | He whose reflection (praticchāyā), in sunlight, on a conjectural reading mirror, and the like, is suddenly seen cut, split, deficient, in excess, confused, lost, on a conjectural reading crooked, with head downward, headless, two-headed, spread out, otherwise, or distorted.
02.09.22 | He whose forehead does not sweat.
02.09.23 | He whose head-hair and body-hair, unanointed, are seen as if anointed.
02.09.24 | And the two eyes: sunk in too deep, protruding, widened, contracted; with contracted brows, with bent-down brows; on a conjectural reading crooked, uneven; with ceased sight, with deficient sight; resembling a mongoose (nakula), resembling a pigeon (kapota), of the colour of a firebrand, of abnormal colour, blue and the like; very unsteady, stiff and drooping, discharging, excessively open, excessively shut, or with lost eyelashes.
02.09.25 | Or the nose too wide open, too closed, crooked, cracked, covered with piṭikās (small boils or pustules) or dry: he is gatāyu (one whose life-span has gone).
02.09.26 | He whose lower lip goes downward, and upper lip, or the two (lips) facing upward, or blue like the ripe jambū fruit (Syzygium cumini, Indian blackberry).
02.09.27 | Or the teeth very dry, copper-coloured, dusky, muddy, flowered, with grit (śarkarā), or they suddenly fall out.
02.09.28 | Or the tongue crooked, stiff, numb, coated, heavy, dark brown, swollen, dry, spreading, or as if covered with thorns.
02.09.29 | He whose attachments of cheek, forehead and jaw are slack as if fallen from their place; on a conjectural reading, whose head, neck or back does not bear its own weight.
02.09.30 | Or the two jaws, a lump staying in the mouth.
02.09.31 | He whose penis (meḍhra) is swollen, or whose two testicles (vṛṣaṇa) have gone out too far, or the reverse of this.
02.09.32 | He whose urine is very fierce (strong).
02.09.33 | He from whose orifices (kha), together with the hair-pores, blood flows out without the fault of poison.
02.09.34 | He whose limbs would be, without cause, too light or too heavy: he is mumūrṣu (one about to die; literally 'wishing to die').
02.09.35 | He whose voice, single or manifold, or stammering, indistinct (on a conjectural reading), thin or piteous, is suddenly perceived.
02.09.36 | Or he who, wishing to speak, becomes bewildered.
02.09.37 | He whose chest, while he speaks, aches greatly upward
02.09.38 | He whose exhalation (ucchvāsa) is too long, too short, foul-smelling or sweet-smelling
02.09.39 | He whose fingers, when stretched, would not crack, and whose sound, even 'other and other' (reading doubtful), in sneezing, going, urinating and the like, is beyond its natural state
02.09.40 | He in whose body, mouth, waste (mala), wound, clothes and the like there is a smell, pleasant or unpleasant, of garlands, fruits, sandalwood, corpses and the like
02.09.41 | He whose body, because of deformity (vairūpya) or good flavour (saurasya), lice, flies and the like greatly abandon or resort to
02.09.42 | He in whom there is coldness in the limbs and parts that are constantly warm, or, in those that are (already) cold, excessive coldness resembling kumuda (probably white water lily) and utpala (probably blue water lily), excessive sweating, excessive stiffness, and slackness as if in (parts) whose flesh and blood have gone
02.09.43 | And he in whose body a conjunction (yoga) or excessive conjunction (atiyoga) of the great elements (mahābhūta) is seen at one time: all those are ripened by time (kālapakva)
02.09.44 | And on this point there is
02.09.45 | He whose vein-lines (sirā, vein) are unprecedented, or even like the young moon
02.09.46 | On the forehead, or at the head of the bladder (basti), he does not live six months
02.09.47 | Water, as with a lotus (padminī) leaf, in the body of whichever embodied being
02.09.48 | It floats; of one that floats, his life is six months
02.09.49 | He whose veins (sirā) are of greenish appearance and whose hair pores are closed
02.09.50 | That man, craving the sour (amla), attains death from Pitta
02.09.51 | He on whose head, or even on the face, there is powder resembling powder of cow dung
02.09.52 | Or smoke with oiliness on the head; his life is up to the end of a month
02.09.53 | On the head or on the two eyebrows, new partings and whorls (sīmantāvartaka) make
02.09.54 | Death, for a healthy person after six nights, but for a sick person after three nights
02.09.55 | He whose chest, when he is bathed and anointed, dries up first and greatly
02.09.56 | On a conjectural reading: while all the limbs are wet, he does not live half a month
02.09.57 | He in whose chest there may be heat, and in the belly (jaṭhara) excessive coldness
02.09.58 | Broken stool (bhinna; probably loose) and thirst; as a departed one (preta), just so is he
02.09.59 | Urine, stool, spittle (niṣṭhyūta) and semen (śukra) sink in water
02.09.60 | Or he whose spittle (niṣṭhyūta) is of many colours perishes in months
02.09.61 | The weakening of the voice and the loss of strength and colour (varṇa)
02.09.62 | And having seen the increase of disease without reason (ayuktyā), one should foretell death
02.09.63 | One speaking in a faulty voice (apasvara), his own death having arrived
02.09.64 | And one should avoid from afar the hearer of his sound
02.09.65 | One with upward breath (ūrdhvaśvāsa), whose warmth has gone, whose groins are afflicted by pain
02.09.66 | And one finding no comfort (śarma): the intelligent one should avoid
02.09.67 | On a conjectural reading: he in whom cold pustules (piṭaka) have arisen, or who is cold-limbed, and who burns
02.09.68 | Hating heat and afflicted by cold, he is within the reach of the lord of the departed (probably Yama)
02.09.69 | He on whose neck a shadow like a rope is seen attached
02.09.70 | That living being is surely dragged away soon by the servants of Yama
02.09.71 | Thus the ninth chapter, named Vikṛtāṅgavijñānīya
