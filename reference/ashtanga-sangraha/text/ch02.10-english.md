02.10.1 | Now, therefore, we shall explain the Śārīra (the section on the body) named Vikṛtehāvijñānīya, the knowledge of abnormal activity (vikṛta-īhā).
02.10.2 | He who — the sky as if it had become solid, and the solid as if it were the sky —
02.10.3 | The formless as if formed, and the formed; or the formed standing in a formless state —
02.10.4 | The bright (as) lightless, and likewise; the white (as) black, and the unreal (as) real
02.10.5 | One free from eye disease, and the moon of many forms, without its mark —
02.10.6 | While awake, demons (rakṣas), gandharvas, pretas (spirits of the dead), and others of that kind —
02.10.7 | A form, an altered shape (vyākṛti), and likewise — he who sees (these) perishes.
02.10.8 | He who does not see Arundhatī (probably the star Alcor), standing near the great sages,
02.10.9 | or Dhruva (probably the pole star), or the Ākāśagaṅgā (the Milky Way) — he does not see that year (samā).
02.10.10 | (ones) born of the roar of cloud and flood-water, of the vīṇā (lute), the paṇava (probably a small drum) and the flute,
02.10.11 | and who hears other sounds, non-existent ones, or not even existing ones —
02.10.12 | who, having pressed both ears, would not hear the sound dhukadhukā —
02.10.13 | Likewise, he who takes smells, tastes and touches by reversal (viparyaya, the contrary way),
02.10.14 | or who does not at all, and who does not smell the odour of a lamp —
02.10.15 | He for whom, by rule (vidhi), for disorder (doṣa), ... the tastes (rasa); the middle word is damaged in print and unclear, so this line is rendered on a conjectural reading.
02.10.16 | he whose limbs are strewn as if with dust (on a conjectural reading), or who is not aware of a blow on his limb —
02.10.17 | Without severe austerity (tapas), or yoga done by rule (vidhi-pūrvaka, preceded by the rule),
02.10.18 | and who knows what is beyond the senses (atīndriya) — of them one should foretell death.
02.10.19 | The tongue dusky (śyāva), the mouth putrid, the left eye sinks in —
02.10.20 | or the one on whose head birds settle — him one should avoid.
02.10.21 | who creeps about with drooping shoulders, as if rubbing his two feet —
02.10.22 | who, eating much wholesome (hita) food, continually declines in strength —
02.10.23 | who eats little and has much faeces and urine, and who eats much and has little urine and faeces —
02.10.24 | or who, eating little and afflicted by Kapha, breathes long and stirs —
02.10.25 | who, having breathed in long and breathed out short, becomes exhausted —
02.10.26 | and who breathes short and throbs violently, jerked about (vyāviddha) —
02.10.27 | who with difficulty tosses his head about ...; the rest of the line is damaged in print and unclear, so this is rendered on a conjectural reading.
02.10.28 | whose sweat trickles from the forehead, and whose joinings (sandhāna) and bindings (bandhana) are slack —
02.10.29 | who, when being raised up, would become bewildered (probably faint), whether strong or even weak —
02.10.30 | who sleeps only on his back (uttāna, face up) and distorts his two feet —
02.10.31 | who tries to grasp only what is non-existent, on bed, seat, wall and so on —
02.10.32 | who laughs at what is not to be laughed at, growing bewildered, and licks his two lips (daśana-cchada, lit. tooth-covers) —
02.10.33 | who licks around his upper lip and makes belches (on a conjectural reading) —
02.10.34 | whom a shadow rushes at, black, yellow, or even reddish —
02.10.35 | and those who hate the physician, medicine, drink, food, elders (guru) and friends —
02.10.36 | All these are to be known as gone under the sway of Samavartin (Yama).
02.10.37 | He whose neck, forehead and heart sweat while cold
02.10.38 | And the other region is hot; his refuge is the deities.
02.10.39 | He who is of small light (aṇujyotis), not one-pointed in mind (anekāgra), ill-lustred or ill-complexioned (duśchāya), and always low-spirited.
02.10.40 | He whose set-out offering (bali) the bali-eaters (balibhuj, probably crows) do not eat.
02.10.41 | And he who, without cause, intellect (medhā), beauty (śobhā), growth (upacaya), prosperity (śrī)
02.10.42 | obtains, or from these a falling away (vibhraṃśa); he reaches the abode of Yama.
02.10.43 | Whose (one) made up of guṇa (quality) and doṣa, of a healthy or of an ill person
02.10.44 | The natural state (prakṛti) goes into change; on a conjectural reading, he does not live six months.
02.10.45 | Devotion (bhakti), character (śīla), memory (smṛti), giving up (tyāga), intellect (buddhi) and strength (bala), without cause.
02.10.46 | These six turn away from one who will die in six months.
02.10.47 | On a conjectural reading: gait, speech, trembling and bewilderment like those of a drunk man, in one who will die in a month.
02.10.48 | On a conjectural reading: is lost; the word 'ajñānanūṣaḍhāt' is unclear; the pain of hair-plucking.
02.10.49 | And he whose food does not go to the throat (kaṇṭha), except for a throat ailment.
02.10.50 | Servants go contrarily, and the form of a preta (departed spirit, ghost) arises.
02.10.51 | He whose sleep is constant, or not at all, does not live.
02.10.52 | On a conjectural reading: the face is filled with tears, and both feet sweat greatly.
02.10.53 | And the eye of one who will go to the realm of Yama falls into agitation (ākulatā).
02.10.54 | Displeased (arati) with those things (bhāva) in which he formerly delighted, he does not live.
02.10.55 | And the physician, even when asked, should tell the death that is hard to hear.
02.10.56 | On a conjectural reading: of one whose life-breath has gone (gatāsu), of kinsmen and friends; and one should not wish to treat him.
02.10.57 | since one whose life-breath has departed (parāsu) is attended by those beginning with Yama's messengers and piśācas,
02.10.58 | by those who destroy the potencies (vīrya) of medicines; therefore one should avoid him.
02.10.59 | There is no death without riṣṭa (sign of death), and there is life in which riṣṭa has been seen.
02.10.60 | In the absence of riṣṭa, knowledge of riṣṭa; and not in riṣṭas, from lack of skill.
02.10.61 | This is the statement of Caraka; but by Suśruta it is recited.
02.10.62 | For death in riṣṭa is certain; that, it is said, by stainless brāhmaṇas
02.10.63 | and is warded off by those intent on rasāyana (rejuvenation therapy), austerity (tapas) and giving (dāna).
02.10.64 | On a conjectural reading of the name: Kṛṣṇātreya, riṣṭa twofold, by the division into stable and unstable.
02.10.65 | Even from an abundance of the doṣas, a semblance of riṣṭa (riṣṭābhāsa) may arise.
02.10.66 | On the subsiding of those doṣas it would subside; but the lasting (one) is certainly for death.
02.10.67 | The whole fruit of Āyurveda (the science of life), which is established in one who knows lifespan (āyurjña).
02.10.68 | Therefore the physician should always be attentive to the knowledge of riṣṭa.
02.10.69 | Thus the tenth chapter, named Vikṛtehāvijñānīya.
