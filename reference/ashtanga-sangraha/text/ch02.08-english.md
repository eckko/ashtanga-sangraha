02.08.1 | Now, therefore, we shall explain the Śārīra (that which relates to the body) concerning the kinds of constitution (prakṛti).
02.08.2 | There are seven constitutions (prakṛti): from the doṣas singly, from combination, and from equality.
02.08.3 | There, whichever doṣa, one or more, becomes predominant in the semen (śukra), the menstrual fluid (ārtava), the womb (garbhāśaya) and the time, and in the mother's food and conduct, by that, until death (on a conjectural reading), the constitution (prakṛti) arises, as for a worm being born in a poison-creeper, by the poison; like a poisonous worm, a scorpion and so on, or like a snake, by its own poison.
02.08.4 | Therefore, because of being made of that, because the body is soaked (bhāvita) by the semen (śukra) and the menstrual fluid (ārtava), and because of being natural (prākṛta), it is not harmed to that extent by that doṣa.
02.08.5 | And his body is furnished (upaskriyate) by the qualities of that (doṣa) alone.
02.08.6 | And therefore he is not suddenly afflicted by the opposites of its qualities.
02.08.7 | But in that state, when the doṣa is abnormal (vikṛtimat) and excessive (ulbaṇa), the embryo (garbha) does not come into being at all.
02.08.8 | Even at conception it perishes (vipadyate), or it becomes abnormal (vikṛta).
02.08.9 | And so some say.
02.08.10 | Vāta and the others are of two kinds: natural (prākṛta) and abnormal (vaikṛta).
02.08.11 | Among these, the natural (prākṛta) ones are the causes of the sevenfold constitution (prakṛti), having one birth with the body.
02.08.12 | They have the name dhātu from supporting the body; of the unclear (doṣārvyānām) abnormal (vikṛta) ones, being the seed (bīja), in one about to die they move away from their own nature (svarūpa).
02.08.13 | Even when all are present in the body, in the matter of constitution (prakṛti) the designation is by the prominent (ulbaṇa) one.
02.08.14 | But the abnormal (vaikṛta) ones, arising from the foods of one who has come out of the womb, [unclear: asāt], grow only upon the natural (prākṛta) ones; by the force of time and so on, through [unclear: svapnamāṇavṛddhikṣayayogāt], they favour the body and also vitiate it. Likewise ojas too, supreme, situated in the heart, of the measure of eight drops, is the support; [unclear: prākṛtikalya]; of ojas, whose nature is rasa, sharing in decrease and increase.
02.08.15 | Now, in accordance with the qualities of his own doṣa, one of Vāta constitution (vātaprakṛti) is thin and dry, with thin and scanty limbs, teeth, nails, hair, eyes and voice; a hater of cold, with tightly bound (udbaddha) calves; going with noisy joints; quick to begin, to be agitated, to grasp and to forget; unsteady in fortitude, mind, gait, gaze, disposition and friendship; a thief, ignoble; envious, with his senses unconquered; fond of music (gāndharva), legends (itihāsa), laughter, play, quarrel, hunting, gardens and journeys; and with a desire for, and suitability to, unctuous, hot, sweet, sour (on a conjectural reading) and salty food and drink.
02.08.16 | And further.
02.08.17 | With little wealth, strength, life and sleep; with weak (kṣāma) speech and a body spread over with vessels (dhamanī, probably the visible veins).
02.08.18 | Unfortunate, eating very much, talkative, an unbeliever (nāstika), with split hair, hands and feet.
02.08.19 | With eyes slightly open and the mouth very slack (susrasta); he moans (stanyate), [unclear: kthati], and grinds his teeth.
02.08.20 | He goes in dry, rough and uneven rivers, in the sky and on mountain peaks.
02.08.21 | One of Pitta constitution (pittaprakṛti) is warm and fair-bodied, with copper-coloured nails, eyes, tongue, lips, palms and soles; with loose joint-ligaments and flesh; with hair of the head and body like a karabha (camel or young elephant), tawny, sparse and soft; of middling strength and life, with little semen, sexual intercourse and offspring; brave, proud; with early wrinkles (valī), baldness (khalita), grey hair (palita), pimples (piṭka) and blemishes (vyaṅga) and, on a conjectural reading, hunger and thirst; intelligent, unfortunate; and with desire for, and suitability to, sweet, bitter, astringent (kaṣāya) and cold (ones).
02.08.22 | And further.
02.08.23 | One to whom garlands, unguents and adornment are dear; well-behaved, pure, affectionate to his dependents.
02.08.24 | He is endowed with wealth (vibhava), daring, intellect and strength; in fears he is a refuge even for the lords of the quarters (dikpati).
02.08.25 | A hater of heat, sweating, foul-smelling (on a conjectural reading), with much stool, anger, drinking, eating and jealousy.
02.08.26 | Asleep, he would see karṇikāra trees (probably the kanak-champa tree), palāśa trees (the dhak or flame-of-the-forest), the burning of the quarters (digdāha), meteors, lightning, the sun and fires.
02.08.27 | But one of Kapha constitution (kaphaprakṛti) has the colour of any one of dūrvā grass (Bermuda grass), indīvara (probably the blue lotus) or the stalk of the śara reed (probably a reed grass); even, well-proportioned (on a conjectural reading), unctuous, firm, delicate, smooth, with joint-ligaments well-fitted (on a conjectural reading); with a full, handsome body; with a large forehead, thighs and arms; with eyes in which the white and the dark are distinct, clear, long, wide and lashed; with a sound like a lion, a mṛdaṅga drum and a cloud; enduring hunger, thirst and heat; with much (on a conjectural reading) ojas, strength, semen, sexual intercourse and offspring; [unclear: sthiraśoṣa], garlands and unguents; with firm, hidden enmity; gentle, truthful; possessing memory and steadiness, not greedy, not crying even in childhood; and with desire for, and suitability to, pungent (kaṭu), bitter, astringent (kaṣāya), hot and dry (ones).
02.08.28 | And further.
02.08.29 | With little speech, anger, intellect (medhā), eating and effort; with abundant life and wealth; far-seeing, generous.
02.08.30 | Faithful (śrāddha), deep, of large aim (sthūlalakṣa), forbearing, noble, prone to sleep, slow-acting (dīrghasūtrī), grateful.
02.08.31 | Upright, learned, fortunate, modest, devoted to his elders (guru), of firm friendship.
02.08.32 | In a dream he sees water-reservoirs (toyāśaya) with lotuses (padma) and with rows of birds, and clouds (toyada, giver of water).
02.08.33 | But when the marks of both are mixed, [it is] the one made by combination (saṃsarga).
02.08.34 | One endowed with all the qualities is of the equal-doṣa constitution (samadoṣaprakṛti).
02.08.35 | Among these, the first three are always ailing (nitya-ātura), because of [unclear: doṣānupraśayitaprakṛtitvāt].
02.08.36 | And especially those of dual constitution (dvandvaprakṛti).
02.08.37 | For in these, which produce disorders of mixed qualities, in hunger, thirst and so on, there is, on a conjectural reading, the designation of ill-health (anārogya).
02.08.38 | Therefore one should treat them with a method of opposite qualities, as stated in the Doṣopakramaṇīya (the treatment of the doṣas).
02.08.39 | On a conjectural reading: the balanced (one) with all tastes (rasa) and with what is suited (sātmya); and the others also with what is prescribed according to the season.
02.08.40 | On a conjectural reading: like the doṣas, and also by the qualities beginning with sattva, there are seven constitutions (prakṛti).
02.08.41 | One should know them by cleanliness (śauca) and the like, as stated, arisen only from them.
02.08.42 | On a conjectural reading: likewise, again, the seven constitutions (prakṛti) are based on birth-class (jāti), family, region, time, age, strength, and each individual.
02.08.43 | For among men are seen those various particular states (bhāva) determined by birth-class (jāti) and the rest; and by the more-and-most degrees of combination of the seven and others, by differences of body, and because of conformity to another, the total extent of the varieties becomes countless.
02.08.44 | The body also conforms to sattva (mind) and the rest, and sattva and the rest also conform to the body; and, on a conjectural reading, by the influence of the countless varieties of sattva and the rest, the imitation of form, voice and conduct as well, which is expressible by many words, becomes of countless varieties.
02.08.45 | But the entry into that (tad-āveśa) is produced by the latent impression (vāsanā) of practice in the immediately preceding birth. For that very reason one should examine, accordingly, the anūka (meaning uncertain) of the person, of humans, animals (tiryak), departed spirits (preta) and hell-beings (nāraka), and of their various particular kinds.
02.08.46 | Age (vayas) is threefold: young (bāla), middle (madhya) and old (vṛddha).
02.08.47 | Of these, up to the sixteenth year is the young (age).
02.08.48 | Up to sixty, the middle (age); after that, the old (age).
02.08.49 | Among these also, the young (age) is threefold by mode of living: on breast-milk, on food, and on both.
02.08.50 | In it: growth in the size of the body, and predominance of śleṣman (phlegm, Kapha).
02.08.51 | Because of that, in the young (age) there are unctuousness (sneha), softness, delicacy, little anger and good fortune (saubhāgya).
02.08.52 | The middle (age) also is threefold: youth (yauvana), fullness, and non-decline.
02.08.53 | In it: predominance of Pitta; because of that, a kindled state of the digestive fire (agni), abundance of understanding (prajñā) and ripening, and determination (vyavasāya).
02.08.54 | Of these, up to thirty, youth; up to forty, fullness in the qualities of all the dhātus, the sense-organs, strength, vigour (vīrya), manliness (pauruṣa), memory, speech, knowledge (vijñāna) and, on a conjectural reading, humility (praśraya); after this, non-decline.
02.08.55 | The old (age), however, with the qualities of the dhātus, sense-organs and the rest wasting day after day, overcome by wrinkles, probably baldness (khalita), cough, breathlessness (śvāsa), weakness of the digestive fire (agni) and the like, sinks down like an old house rained upon.
02.08.56 | In it: predominance of māruta (wind; Vāta). Because of that: looseness and lack of essence in the flesh, joints and bones, roughness of the skin, stooping of the body, trembling, cough, breathlessness (śvāsa), the bringing up of phlegm (śleṣman) and nasal mucus (siṃghāṇaka), and wasting of the dhātus.
02.08.57 | Others, however, say:
02.08.58 | In childhood, growth; lustre, intelligence, skin, semen (śukra), eye and the faculty of hearing.
02.08.59 | In the decades, in order, go the mind and all the sense-organs.
02.08.60 | Thus a hundred years is the measure of life.
02.08.61 | In this age, however, there are men who, because of a particular action (karma), live no more than a hundred years. On a conjectural reading, having ascertained the measure of their life by the particular marks of constitution (prakṛti) as stated, one should divide the age (vayas) into three.
02.08.62 | And further.
02.08.63 | For men, each time a hundred has passed, a year goes to decay.
02.08.64 | Of life, owing to abundance of merit (puṇya), the measure that is accepted.
02.08.65 | Strength (bala) also is threefold.
02.08.66 | Innate (sahaja), made by time (kāla), and made by method (yukti).
02.08.67 | Of these, the innate (strength) is the natural (prākṛta) one of the body and of the mind (sattva).
02.08.68 | The time-made (strength) is born of the divisions of the seasons (ṛtu) and made by age.
02.08.69 | The method-made (strength), again, is that arising from food and way of life (vihāra), the vigour-giving, and that born of yoga.
02.08.70 | But these conditions (bhāva), too, are producers of its increase.
02.08.71 | Namely:
02.08.72 | Birth in a region with strong men, and in a time of that kind; a pleasant conjunction of time (kāla-yoga); the excellences of the seed (bīja, probably semen) and field (kṣetra, probably the womb) with their qualities, of the body, of food and of the mind (sattva); natural disposition (svabhāva), youth, exercise and joy.
02.08.73 | Likewise, eight sāras (essences, best qualities) consisting of skin, blood, flesh, fat (medas), bone, marrow (majjā), semen (śukra) and mind (sattva), on a conjectural reading each better than the one before, are taught for knowing the measure of strength.
02.08.74 | And further.
02.08.75 | On a conjectural reading, a physician should not be deluded merely by seeing the body alone, thinking: 'This one is strong because of bulk (upacaya) or because of a large body; this one is of little strength because of thinness or because of a small body.'
02.08.76 | And both of these are also seen to be otherwise in the elephant and the lion.
02.08.77 | There, one endowed with all the essences (sāra) is exceedingly strong, endowed with great heaviness (gaurava), able to bear hardship, having hope in himself in all undertakings, devoted to the good, steady in body, of well-composed gait, with a resonant, smooth, deep and great voice, with the enjoyment of comfort, lordship and wealth, receiving honour, with slow old age and mild illness, usually with widely extended offspring of equal qualities, and long-lived.
02.08.78 | The opposite of that, however, is without essence (asāra); the middle one has middling qualities.
02.08.79 | The measure, moreover, is by one's own aṅgulas (finger-breadths).
02.08.80 | The big toe and the second toe (pradeśinī) of the foot are two aṅgulas long; the other three, in order, each successive one, are less by a fifth part, or less by their nail.
02.08.81 | The prapada (front of the foot), the sole (pādatala) and the heel (pāṣṇi) are separately four aṅgulas long, and are spread six, five and four aṅgulas broad; the foot is exactly fourteen in length and exactly fourteen in girth.
02.08.82 | Likewise the two ankles (gulpha) and the middle of the shank (jaṅghā).
02.08.83 | The foot is four aṅgulas in height.
02.08.84 | The shank (jaṅghā) and the thigh (ūru) are eighteen in length.
02.08.85 | The knee (jānu) is of four aṅgulas.
02.08.86 | The thigh is thirty aṅgulas in girth.
02.08.87 | The muṣka (scrotum or testicles) and the meḍhra (penis) are both six in length, and eight and five in girth.
02.08.88 | The kaṭī (hip or waist region) is sixteen in breadth and fifty in girth.
02.08.89 | The basti-śiras ('head of the bladder'; basti = bladder) is ten aṅgulas.
02.08.90 | The belly (udara) is twelve aṅgulas.
02.08.91 | The two flanks (pārśva) are ten in breadth and twelve in length.
02.08.92 | The trika (probably the sacral region of the lower back) is twelve in height.
02.08.93 | The back (pṛṣṭha) is eighteen in height.
02.08.94 | The interval between the breasts (stanāntara) is a set of twelve.
02.08.95 | The limit (paryanta) of the breast is two aṅgulas.
02.08.96 | The chest (uras) is twenty-four aṅgulas broad and twelve in height.
02.08.97 | The heart (hṛdaya) is of two aṅgulas.
02.08.98 | The two shoulders (skandha) are of eight, and the two armpits.
02.08.99 | On a conjectural reading, the two aṃsa (probably the shoulder-joints) are of six.
02.08.100 | The two prabāhu (a segment of the arm; identity disputed) are of sixteen.
02.08.101 | The two prapāṇi (a part of the hand or arm; identity disputed) are of fifteen.
02.08.102 | The two hands (pāṇi) are twelve aṅgulas; in them as well, the middle finger is five aṅgulas.
02.08.103 | Less than that by half an aṅgula are the index finger (pradeśinī) and the ring finger (anāmikā).
02.08.104 | The little finger (kaniṣṭhikā) and the thumb (aṅguṣṭha) are three and a half aṅgulas.
02.08.105 | The neck (śirodharā) is four in height and twenty-two in girth.
02.08.106 | The face (ānana) is twelve in height and twenty-four in girth.
02.08.107 | The mouth (āsya) is of five aṅgulas.
02.08.108 | Separately, four aṅgulas: the chin (cibuka), the lip (oṣṭha), the nose (nāsā), the interval between the eyes (dṛṣṭy-antara, probably), the ear (karṇa) and the forehead (lalāṭa).
02.08.109 | The border (maryādā) of the nostril (nāsāpuṭa) is a third part of an aṅgula in breadth.
02.08.110 | The eye (netra) is two aṅgulas long and broad by the belly (udara) of the thumb.
02.08.111 | In it, the black (part) is a third portion of the white; the dṛṣṭi (the seeing part, probably the pupil) is a ninth portion of the black, of the size of a masūra-dala (half of a lentil, or a lentil leaf).
02.08.112 | Six finger-breadths (aṅgula) in height and thirty-two in girth: the head.
02.08.113 | Again, the whole body is eighty-four finger-breadths (aṅgula).
02.08.114 | What is equal in that length and breadth is called sama (even, symmetrical).
02.08.115 | Therein, the measure as stated is desirable (iṣṭa); deficient or excessive is undesirable (aniṣṭa).
02.08.116 | Thus indeed:
02.08.117 | Short, tall, fair, dark, hairy, hairless, stout and lean.
02.08.118 | Desirable, moreover, (is one) with: large head, teeth, face, jaws (hanu), breast-tips, back and voice; well-developed thighs, buttocks, waist, chest, neck and palate; hands, feet, chin, ears and forehead having both qualities; fine bones, teeth, nails, finger-joints and hair; long fingers, thighs (sakthi), arms, breath (on a conjectural reading), gaze, eyes and tongue; broad mouth, lower lip, tongue, forehead, ear-seat and chest, and small penis (meḍhra), neck and hair; smooth, (unclear: the printed 'tvakacakarajana'), eyes, teeth and voice; steady sense-organs (indriya), skin, nails, hair and sides; deep navel, joints and voice; hidden jatru (probably the collarbone region), spine (pṛṣṭhavaṃśa) and tooth-roots; red palate, lips, nails and eye-corners, and reddish tongue, palms and soles; black hair, eyebrows, eyes, eyelashes and pupil; (unclear: the printed 'śuklaśukaṃmaṃḍaladaśanacaritam'); raised mind (citta, as printed), shoulders, forehead, belly, face, nails and feet; low-set ears, breasts, nose-tip and gait; dense shoulders and neck; (unclear: the printed 'vaṃtāṃguli'); skin and friendliness (sauhārda).
02.08.119 | (One) with jaw (hanu), (unclear: the printed 'navamadya') and tongue; round buttocks, neck and nails; even back, teeth, forehead and gaze.
02.08.120 | Likewise, the two thighs (ūru) rounded in due succession.
02.08.121 | The navel (nābhi) is right-turning (dakṣiṇāvarta).
02.08.122 | On a conjectural reading, the two breasts and the two eyebrows having a wide interval.
02.08.123 | On a conjectural reading, tortoise-like elevations in the nails and the two feet.
02.08.124 | Straight mouth (face) and nose-bridge (nāsāvaṃśa).
02.08.125 | The two jaws (hanu) not prominent, and the dress (veṣa) too.
02.08.126 | The palate (tālu) smooth, and the tongue too.
02.08.127 | The two lips not very stout or lean, and the structure of the body (deha-bandha) too.
02.08.128 | The heart (hṛdaya) with throbbing, and the two temples (śaṅkha) and the two manyā (probably the vessels at the sides of the neck, the carotids).
02.08.129 | Warm palate, feet and belly, and the outbreath (niḥśvāsa) too.
02.08.130 | The two ears bent down at the back, and hairy.
02.08.131 | The two eyebrows slightly drooping (pralambin).
02.08.132 | (Unclear: the printed 'valibham'); a forehead (lalāṭa) with well-joined joints (sandhi) and the shape of a half-moon; the head like an umbrella.
02.08.133 | The hairs (keśa) not single-rooted, soft.
02.08.134 | Firm, hidden: the veins (śirā), bones and joints.
02.08.135 | Wind (vāta, flatus), urine, dung (purīṣa) and private parts or matters (guhya), released naturally; laughing, weeping, (unclear: the printed 'ārdrani') and activities.
02.08.136 | Likewise, desirable is the body (śarīra), bathed and anointed, drying in the order of the downward spread.
02.08.137 | And on this matter there is:
02.08.138 | (One) with a field (kṣetra) better and better, free from disease from the garbha (embryo; or womb) onward.
02.08.139 | Auspicious, growing slowly by length (āyāma), knowledge (jñāna) and specialised knowledge (vijñāna).
02.08.140 | Thus, in a body endowed with all qualities, a hundred autumns (years).
02.08.141 | Life-span (āyus), lordship (aiśvarya) and all desired states (bhāva) are established.
02.08.142 | (One) experiences pleasure and pain without arrogance and without dejection.
02.08.143 | But sattvān, being heated (tapyamāna): rājasa, not at all tāmasa (on a conjectural reading).
02.08.144 | Giving (dāna), conduct (śīla), compassion (dayā), truth (on a conjectural reading), continence (brahmacarya) and gratitude (kṛtajñatā).
02.08.145 | Rasāyanas (rejuvenation measures), friendliness (maitrī), and the quality (guṇa) that is the cause of increase of merit and life-span.
02.08.146 | Thus the eighth chapter, named Prakṛtibhedīya (concerning the divisions of prakṛti).
