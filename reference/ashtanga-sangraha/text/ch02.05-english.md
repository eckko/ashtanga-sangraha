02.05.1 | Now, therefore, we shall explain the bodily (śārīra) division of the parts (aṅga).
02.05.2 | Even the whole is called 'body' (śarīra).
02.05.3 | And that again has six parts (aṅga): the head, the trunk (antarādhi), the two arms and the two lower limbs (sakthi).
02.05.4 | But the minor parts (pratyaṅga) of this are the eyes, the navel, the nose, the feet and so on.
02.05.5 | Origination is from the great elements (mahābhūta), those consisting of the great guṇas and, on a conjectural reading, constituted of space (kha), wind (pavana), fire (tejas), water (jala) and earth (bhūmi), and presided over by consciousness (cetanā).
02.05.6 | And from the special arrangement of the elements (bhūta) themselves, of many forms, through the power of various actions, seen and unseen, there comes about similarity or dissimilarity of form, size, unctuousness, lustre, voice and so on, differentiated by gradations of fine and gross, of very many kinds.
02.05.7 | Among these, space (ākāśa) is abundant in sattva.
02.05.8 | Wind (vāyu) is abundant in rajas.
02.05.9 | Fire (agni) is abundant in both.
02.05.10 | Water (ambu) is abundant in sattva and tamas.
02.05.11 | Earth (bhū) is abundant in tamas.
02.05.12 | Their marks, in order, are unobstructedness (apratighāta), mobility, heat, fluidity and hardness.
02.05.13 | And in particular, abiding in the sense organs (indriya) beginning with the ear (śrotra), and association with sound, touch, form, taste and smell, increased one by one.
02.05.14 | Among these, those born of space (ākāśa) are the ear (śrotra), sound, the channels (srotas) and separateness (viviktatā).
02.05.15 | Those belonging to wind (vāyu) are the organ of touch (sparśana), touch (sparśa), the movements (parispandana) beginning with the two breaths (praśvāsa and ucchvāsa), and lightness (lāghava).
02.05.16 | Those belonging to fire (agni) are the organ of sight (darśana), form (rūpa), Pitta, heat (ūṣman), digestion (pakti), burning heat (saṃtāpa), intelligence (medhā), colour (varṇa), radiance (bhā), brilliance (tejas) and valour (śaurya).
02.05.17 | Those belonging to water (ambhas) are the tongue (rasana), taste (rasa), the mass of fluids: sweat, moisture (kleda), probably muscle-fat (vasā), rasa (the nutritive fluid), blood (asṛj), semen (śukra), urine (mūtra) and so on, looseness (śaithilya) and unctuousness (sneha).
02.05.18 | Those belonging to earth (pṛthivī) are the nose (ghrāṇa), smell (gandha), the mass of solid forms (mūrti) beginning with hair, nails and bones, steadiness (dhairya) and heaviness (gaurava).
02.05.19 | Among these, those born of the mother (mātṛja) are soft: skin, blood, flesh, fat, marrow (majjā), navel, heart (hṛdaya), stomach (āmāśaya), uterus (garbhāśaya), liver (yakṛt), spleen (plīhan), kloma (identity disputed), intestines (āntra), rectum (guda) and so on.
02.05.20 | Those born of the father (pitṛja) are firm: hair, nails, bones, semen (śukra), sirā (probably veins), dhamanī (probably arteries) and so on.
02.05.21 | Those born of the self (ātman) are: birth in this and that womb (yoni), mind (manas), consciousness (cetanā), the sense organs (indriya), prāṇa and apāna, sustaining (dhāraṇa), the distinctions of form, voice and colour, desire (kāma), anger, greed, fear, joy, the disposition towards dharma and adharma, memory, intellect (buddhi), wish (icchā), aversion, effort, ego (ahaṅkāra), pleasure, pain, lifespan (āyus) and knowledge of the self (ātmajñāna).
02.05.22 | Those born of compatibility (sātmya) are: lifespan (āyus), health (ārogya), absence of laziness, absence of greed, clarity of the sense organs, voice, colour, seed (bīja, probably semen) and vital essence (ojas), an unclear word (printed saṃpadyaśastanatāḥ), abundance of joy, intelligence (medhā) and strength (bala).
02.05.23 | Those born of rasa (the nutritive fluid) are, of the whole body: its coming into being, maintenance (vṛtti), growth, satisfaction (tṛpti), an unclear word (printed aulyaṃ), nourishment (puṣṭi) and vigour (utsāha).
02.05.24 | Those born of pure sattva are: purity (śauca), belief (āstikatva), gratitude, courtesy, resolve, valour, depth, intellect (buddhi), intelligence (medhā), memory, liking for bright dharma (śukla-dharma), devotion (bhakti), non-attachment, and the contrary of the guṇa tamas.
02.05.25 | Those belonging to rajas (rājasa) are: an unclear word (printed durupaghāratāśauryyaṃ), envy (mātsarya), unmeasured talkativeness, egoism (ahaṅkāra), greed, hypocrisy (dambha), pride (māna), anger and joylessness (krodha, aharṣa, as printed) and desire (kāma).
02.05.26 | Those belonging to tamas (tāmasa) are: ignorance, dejection (viṣāda), heedlessness (pramāda), sleep, laziness, hunger and thirst, grief, envy (mātsarya), a wrong notion (vipratipatti), scheming against others and the contrary of the guṇa sattva.
02.05.27 | Among these, sattva is the mind (manas).
02.05.28 | Its disturbance (upaplava) is rajas and tamas.
02.05.29 | Thus they call the body consisting of the elements (bhūtātmaka).
02.05.30 | Again, its division (pravibhāga).
02.05.31 | The skins (tvac), membranes (kalā, probably), doṣas, dhātus, malas, the sense organs of knowledge (buddhīndriya) and their seats, the organs of action (karmendriya), the supra-sensory (atīndriya), the receptacles (āśaya), the abodes of the life-breath (prāṇa), tendons (kaṇḍarā, probably), nets (jāla), bundles (kūrca), cords and sutures (rajjuka, sīvanī), the bone-groupings (asthisaṃghāta), sutures (sīmanta), bones, bone-joints, sinews (snāyu, probably ligaments), muscles (peśī), sirā (probably veins), dhamanī (probably arteries), channels (srotas), on a conjectural reading the digestive fires (agni) and heats (ūṣman), vital spots (marma), hair of the head, beard and body hair, and the divisions of natural state (prakṛti) and deviation (vikṛti).
02.05.32 | Among these, there come into being six skins (tvac) from blood (asṛj) being cooked, like the films (santānikā) of milk.
02.05.33 | From the clarity (prasāda) of the skin, the clarity of the blood.
02.05.34 | And of these, the first is udakadharā ('water-bearing').
02.05.35 | The second is asṛgdharā ('blood-bearing').
02.05.36 | The third is the seat of sidhma (a patchy skin disease, probably pityriasis versicolor; identity disputed) and kilāsa (probably vitiligo).
02.05.37 | The fourth is the seat of all kuṣṭha (the group of chronic skin diseases, which includes leprosy).
02.05.38 | The fifth, again, is the seat of alajī (a skin eruption; identity uncertain) and vidradhi (abscess).
02.05.39 | The sixth, however, is prāṇadharā ('life-bearing').
02.05.40 | When it is cut, one faints and, as it were, enters blinding darkness; and resting on it, sores (arus) arise at the joints, black-red, thick-rooted and most difficult to treat.
02.05.41 | Others, however, speak of seven skins (tvac).
02.05.42 | Among these, the first is named Bhāsinī, which makes manifest the colours (varṇa) and the five shadows (chāyā) beginning with space (kha); it is of the measure of an eighteenth part of a vrīhi (rice grain).
02.05.43 | The second is named Lohinī, of the measure of a sixteenth part.
02.05.44 | The third is named Śvetā; its measure is a twelfth part.
02.05.45 | The fourth is named Tāmrā; its measure is an eighth part.
02.05.46 | The fifth is named Vedinī; its measure is a fifth part.
02.05.47 | The sixth is named Rohiṇī; its measure is that of a vrīhi (rice grain).
02.05.48 | The seventh is named Māṃsadharā ("flesh-bearing"); its measure is that of two vrīhi (rice grains).
02.05.49 | The moisture (kleda) that remains in the spaces between the dhātu-seats (āśaya), well cooked by the respective heats, covered by ligament (snāyu), Kapha (śleṣman) and (probably) membrane (jarāyu), is the essence of the dhātus, like the heartwood (sāra) of wood; having rasa as its remainder on account of its smallness, it is named kalā.
02.05.50 | Those are the seven kalās, the boundaries of the spaces between the dhātu-seats.
02.05.51 | Of these, the first is named Māṃsadharā, in which, in the flesh, there are the spreading networks of the sirā (probably veins), snāyu (ligaments), dhamanī (probably arteries) and srotas (channels), as there are those of lotus-fibres (bisa) and lotus-stalks (mṛṇāla) in muddy water on the ground.
02.05.52 | The second is named Raktadharā ("blood-bearing"); it lies inside the flesh. In it is blood (śoṇita), and especially in the sirā (probably veins), spleen (plīhan) and liver (yakṛt). And from the cut flesh the blood (kṣataja, "wound-born") flows, as milk flows from a milk-bearing tree.
02.05.53 | The third is named Medodharā ("fat-bearing"). Fat (medas) is in it, in the belly and in the small bones, together with blood. And that same (fat), in the head, covered by the skull (kapāla), is called mastiṣka (brain), and, on a conjectural reading, mastuluṅga; but in the large bones (it is) majjā (marrow).
02.05.54 | The fourth is named Śleṣmadharā ("Kapha-bearing"). Joined by the śleṣman (Kapha) situated in it, all the joints become firm, and, on a conjectural reading, together with their subsidiary parts.
02.05.55 | The fifth is named Purīṣadharā ("faeces-bearing"). Resting on the intestine (antra) and on the seats of the raw and the digested (āma and pakva), it separates the waste (mala) lying within the koṣṭha (storehouse), in the unduka (probably the caecum).
02.05.56 | The sixth is named Pittadharā ("Pitta-bearing"), situated in the middle between the seats of the digested and the raw (pakva and āmāśaya). Being the seat of the inner fire (antaragni), it holds by force the food that faces from the āmāśaya (probably the stomach) toward the pakvāśaya (probably the large intestine), and, drying it by the heat of Pitta, cooks it, and releases what is cooked.
02.05.57 | But when occupied by the doṣas, through weakness, only āma (the raw, undigested). Hence, from its taking up of food, it again has the name grahaṇī. And its strength is Pitta itself, which has the name agni (fire). Therefore, supported by the fire, strengthened, having a single welfare (yogakṣema), it keeps the body going.
02.05.58 | The seventh is named Śukradharā ("semen-bearing"). At two aṅgulas (finger-breadths), on the right side, and below the opening of the bladder (basti), resting on the urinary passage and pervading the whole body, it sets śukra (semen) in motion.
02.05.59 | The doṣas, dhātus and malas have been stated before.
02.05.60 | The five sense-organs of cognition (buddhīndriya) are: hearing (śrotra), touch (sparśana), sight (darśana), taste (rasana) and smell (ghrāṇa).
02.05.61 | Of these, by way of equal shares, the objects in order are sound, touch, form, taste and smell.
02.05.62 | The five seats of the sense-organs of cognition are: the two ears, the skin, the two eyes, the tongue and the two nostrils.
02.05.63 | The five organs of action (karmendriya) are those named speech (vāk), anus (apāyu), genital organ (upastha), hand and foot.
02.05.64 | And these too are for the purposes of speaking, discharge, delight, taking and going.
02.05.65 | But the mind (manas) is beyond the sense-organs, because of its association with all objects.
02.05.66 | And it is through union with it (the mind) that the sense-organs engage with their objects.
02.05.67 | And because of having the nature of both the sense-organs of cognition and the organs of action.
02.05.68 | The seven seats (āśaya), on a conjectural reading, are in order the receptacles of blood, Kapha, āma (the raw), Pitta, pakva (the digested), Vāyu and urine.
02.05.69 | In women, between the Pitta seat and the pakva seat (pakvāśaya), the eighth seat is the garbhāśaya (womb, uterus).
02.05.70 | Attached to these (seats) are the members of the koṣṭha (trunk): the heart (hṛdaya), liver (yakṛt), spleen (plīhan), lung (phupphusa), unduka (probably the caecum), kidney (vṛkka), intestine (antra) and so on.
02.05.71 | There, from blood (śoṇita) being inflated by samāna (the Vāyu so named) and cooked by the body's heat, from its unclear word acchataḥ (on a conjectural reading, "from its clear part"), the liver (yakṛt) and spleen (plīhan) arise.
02.05.72 | From the froth of blood, the lung (phupphusa).
02.05.73 | From the dregs (kiṭṭa) of blood, the unduka (probably the caecum).
02.05.74 | From the clear essence (prasāda) of blood and fat (medas), the two kidneys (vṛkka).
02.05.75 | From the clear essence (prasāda) of blood and flesh, the intestines (antra).
02.05.76 | Those (intestines) are three and a half vyāmas (fathoms, arm-spans) in men.
02.05.77 | In women, three vyāmas.
02.05.78 | From the union of blood with Vāyu (wind, anila), the unclear word kolīyam.
02.05.79 | The heart (hṛdaya), moreover, arises from the clear essence (prasāda) of Kapha and blood; it resembles a lotus bud (padmakośa), is hollow, and has its face downward.
02.05.80 | That (the heart) is especially the seat of consciousness (cetanā), and of all the states (bhāva) that follow consciousness.
02.05.81 | On its left side are the spleen (plīhan) and the lung (phupphusa), and on the right the liver (yakṛt) and the kloman.
02.05.82 | Likewise, from the clear essence (prasāda) of the srotas (channels) that carry Kapha and blood, and of the great elements (mahābhūta), the sense-organs (indriya).
02.05.83 | Among these too, in the eye, from the clear essence of Kapha (śleṣman), the white circle (śukla-maṇḍala, probably the white of the eye); that is born from the father.
02.05.84 | From blood, the black circle (kṛṣṇa-maṇḍala, probably the dark part of the eye); that is born from the mother. In the middle, the circle of sight (dṛṣṭi-maṇḍala, probably the pupil region); that has the nature of both.
02.05.85 | The five circles (maṇḍala), on a conjectural reading, are those named eyelashes (pakṣma), eyelid (vartma), white (śukla), black (kṛṣṇa) and sight (dṛṣṭi).
02.05.86 | Their junctions (sandhi) are four.
02.05.87 | And two, at the apāṅga (outer corner of the eye) and the kanīna (probably the inner corner of the eye).
02.05.88 | Thus these are six.
02.05.89 | And six paṭalas (layers).
02.05.90 | Two vartmas (eyelids).
02.05.91 | The outer one, and one resting on fire (agni) and water; the second, flesh; the third, fat (medas); the fourth, bone; their thickness is by a fifth part of sight (dṛṣṭi).
02.05.92 | The binding strands (bandhana-guṇa), however, are four, having the nature of sirā (vein, vessel), kaṇḍarā (large tendon), fat (medas) and Kapha.
02.05.93 | Śleṣman (Kapha), however, is the highest binder of all joints; thus it was said before.
02.05.94 | The tejas (fire, radiance) resting in the eye becomes fit for its work through union with the outer tejas, its own source, as a weapon does with a stone; through excessive union it is harmed.
02.05.95 | And that (tejas), like lightning and like the vaḍavāmukha (the submarine fire), even when situated in the midst of water, does not abandon its fiery nature (tejastva) because of the excellence of its vīrya (potency).
02.05.96 | From the clear essence (prasāda) of flesh, blood and Kapha, however, the tongue (jihvā) arises.
02.05.97 | From the clear essence (prasāda) of flesh, blood, Kapha and fat, the two testes (vṛṣaṇa).
02.05.98 | There are ten seats of life (prāṇāyatana): the head (mūrdhan), the jihvābandhana (tongue-binding), the throat, the heart (hṛdaya), the navel, the bladder (basti), the gūda (anus, rectum), śukra (semen), ojas and blood.
02.05.99 | Of these, the first seven, again, are named mahāmarma (great marma, vital point).
02.05.100 | There are sixteen kaṇḍarās (large tendons); of them, two each in the hands and feet, four, separately, in the neck and the back.
02.05.101 | There are sixteen jālas (nets); of them, those born of flesh, vessel, snāyu (ligament) and bone, four in each single place, at the ankle (gulpha) and the wrist (maṇibandha), are situated mutually latticed.
02.05.102 | Six kūrcas (bundles, sense uncertain): in the hands, feet, neck and penis (meḍhra).
02.05.103 | There are four flesh-cords (māṃsarajju) on both sides of the spine (pṛṣṭhavaṃśa), for the binding of the peśīs (muscles); of them two are outer and two are situated inside.
02.05.104 | There are seven sīvanīs (seams, probably sutures); of them five are in the head, one each in the tongue and the penis (mehana).
02.05.105 | And to be avoided with the śastra (blade, surgical instrument).
02.05.106 | There are fourteen asthi-saṃghātas (bone assemblages); of them one each in the ankle, knee, groin, wrist, elbow and armpit, and, on a conjectural reading, in the trika (probably the sacral region) and the head.
02.05.107 | Likewise the sīmantas (probably sutures of bones); they, however, are five in the head: thus eighteen.
02.05.108 | Three hundred bones, in excess by sixty (that is, three hundred and sixty).
02.05.109 | And of them, a hundred and forty in the limbs (śākhā), a hundred and twenty in the antarādhi (probably the trunk), a hundred in the head.
02.05.110 | There, in each single lower limb (sakthi): five foot-nails (pādanakha); in each toe three bones; those are fifteen.
02.05.111 | Five foot-rods (pādaśalākā); one binder of them; two each in the kūrca, ankle (gulpha) and shank (jaṅghā); and, on a conjectural reading, one each in the heel, knee (jānu) and thigh (ūru).
02.05.112 | And all, beginning with the nail-bones, as in the lower limb, also in the two arms.
02.05.113 | Twenty-four parśukās (ribs).
02.05.114 | Just as many are their sthālakas (small dishes) and arbudas (lumps).
02.05.115 | Thirty in the back.
02.05.116 | Eight in the chest.
02.05.117 | One each in the bhaga (probably the pubic region) and the trika (probably the sacral region).
02.05.118 | And two in the two nitambas (hips).
02.05.119 | Likewise, on a conjectural reading, in the akṣaka (probably the collarbone), the shoulder (aṃsa) and the shoulder-blades (aṃsaphalaka).
02.05.120 | Likewise in the cheeks (gaṇḍa), ears and temples (śaṅkha), and in the jatru (probably the region of the collarbone) and the palate (tālu).
02.05.121 | Thirteen in the neck; four in the kaṇṭhanāḍī (probably the windpipe), two in the jaw-joints (hanubandhana); thirty-two teeth, and likewise ulūkhalas (probably tooth-sockets); three in the nose, six in the head.
02.05.122 | Those in the knees, hips, shoulders, cheeks, palate, temples, groin, middle and heads are named kapāla (plates).
02.05.123 | The teeth (daśana), however, are rucakas.
02.05.124 | In the nose, ears, neck and eye-sockets (akṣikoṣa) are the taruṇas (tender ones, usually taken as cartilage, probable).
02.05.125 | In the hands, feet, sides and back are the valayas (rings).
02.05.126 | The remaining ones are nalakas (tubes).
02.05.127 | These are five kinds of bones, following their names, on a conjectural reading.
02.05.128 | For in them the fleshes (masses of flesh) are bound by vessels (sirā) and snāyus (ligaments).
02.05.129 | Two hundred, in excess by ten (that is, two hundred and ten), of the joints of bones (asthi-sandhi).
02.05.130 | Of them, sixty-eight in the limbs (śākhā).
02.05.131 | Fifty-nine in the trunk (antarādhi).
02.05.132 | Eighty above (ūrdhvam).
02.05.133 | There, in each single sakthi (probably meaning limb), three in each toe (pādāṅguli), two in the great toe (aṅguṣṭha).
02.05.134 | Those are fourteen.
02.05.135 | One each in the ankles (gulpha), knees (jānu) and groins (vaṅkṣaṇa); three in the hip kapālas (kaṭikapāla, on a conjectural reading).
02.05.136 | Twenty-four in the spine (pṛṣṭhavaṃśa).
02.05.137 | Likewise in the two flanks (pārśva).
02.05.138 | Eight in the chest (uras).
02.05.139 | Eight in the neck (grīvā).
02.05.140 | Three in the kaṇṭhanāḍī (probably the windpipe).
02.05.141 | Eighteen, bound to the kaṇṭhanāḍī, in the heart (hṛdaya), liver (yakṛt), kloma (an organ of disputed identity) and channels (nāḍī).
02.05.142 | Thirty-two in the tooth-roots (dantamūla).
02.05.143 | One each in the kākala (a throat part of disputed identity), the nose (nāsā) and the head (mūrdhan).
02.05.144 | Two each in the joints (saṃdhi) of the cheek (gaṇḍa), ear (karṇa), temple (śaṅkha), eyelid (vartman), eye (netra) and jaw (hanu).
02.05.145 | Two above the two eyebrows (bhrū); five in the kapālas of the head (śiras).
02.05.146 | In the fingers or toes (aṅguli, on a conjectural reading), ankles, knees, wrists (maṇibandha) and elbows (kūrpara): (ones) named kora (bud).
02.05.147 | In the armpits (kakṣā), groins (vaṅkṣaṇa) and tooth-roots (dantamūla): (ones) named ulūkhala (mortar).
02.05.148 | In the shoulder-seats (aṃsapīṭha, probably the region of the shoulder-blade), anus (guda), bhaga (the female genital organ) and hips (nitamba, on a conjectural reading): (ones) of the sāmudga (casket) kind.
02.05.149 | In the neck and the spine: pratara (ones).
02.05.150 | In the head and hip kapālas (on a conjectural reading): (ones) named tunnasevanī (also on a conjectural reading; sevanī means a seam).
02.05.151 | On both sides of the two jaws (hanu): vāyasatuṇḍa (crow-beak) (ones).
02.05.152 | In the throat (kaṇṭha), eyes (netra), heart, liver, kloma (an organ of disputed identity) and channels (nāḍī): (ones) named maṇḍala (circle).
02.05.153 | In the ears (śrotra) and the śṛṅgāṭakas: śaṅkhāvarta (conch-whorl) (ones).
02.05.154 | The bone-joints (asthisaṃdhi) are of eight kinds.
02.05.155 | But together with (ones) resting on ligaments (snāyu), muscles (peśī) and veins (sirā): two thousand. Since nothing more is to be said about them in the application of the surgical instrument (śastrapraṇidhāna), (it) is not stated separately.

02.05.158 | Two hundred and thirty in the trunk (antarādhi).

02.05.179 | Twelve in the upper and lower gums (dantamāṃsa, literally tooth-flesh).
02.05.180 | Six in the head (mūrdhan).
02.05.181 | Among these, (ones) in the limbs and in the joints are tendrilled (pratānavat, having spreading branches).
02.05.182 | But the large sinews (snāva; probably ligaments or tendons) that are round, those are called kaṇḍarā (probably tendons).
02.05.183 | Hollow (ones) in the bladder (basti), in the seats of the raw and the cooked (āmāśaya and pakvāśaya, probably the stomach and the large intestine) and in the intestines (antra).
02.05.184 | Broad (ones) in the sides (pārśva), the back (pṛṣṭha), the chest (uras) and the head (śiras).
02.05.185 | For the body, well bound in the joints by the bonds of sinew (snāva), acts properly, like a boat.
02.05.186 | Therefore one should protect them with effort, even more than the bones (asthi), the vessels (sirā) and the rest.
02.05.187 | And he who knows well the sinews (snāva), external and internal, becomes able to extract even a hidden śalya (foreign body).
02.05.188 | Five hundred peśī (muscles).
02.05.189 | Of these, four hundred in the limbs (śākhā).
02.05.190 | Sixty in the trunk (antarādhi).
02.05.191 | Forty above.
02.05.192 | There, in each single leg (sakthi, lower limb), three in each toe.
02.05.193 | Those are fifteen.
02.05.194 | Ten each in the prapada (forepart of the foot), the sole of the foot (pādatala) and the ankle (gulpha).
02.05.195 | Likewise, (ones) situated above the foot, in the kūrma (probably a tortoise-shaped part of the foot).
02.05.196 | Twenty in the jaṅghā (lower leg).
02.05.197 | Five in the knee (jānu).
02.05.198 | Twenty in the thigh (ūru).
02.05.199 | One each in the meḍhra (penis) and the sevanī (probably the seam, the raphe).
02.05.200 | Two in the two testicles (vṛṣaṇa).
02.05.201 | Ten in the two buttocks (sphij).
02.05.202 | Three in the anus (guda); but those are called valī (folds).
02.05.203 | Two in the head of the bladder (bastiśiras).
02.05.204 | Four in the belly (udara).
02.05.205 | One in the navel (nābhi).
02.05.206 | Two in the heart (hṛd) and in the stomach (āmāśaya, probably the stomach).
02.05.207 | Six in the liver (yakṛt), the spleen (plīha) and the uṇḍuka (probably the caecum).
02.05.208 | Five in the back (pṛṣṭha), set upward.
02.05.209 | Ten long (ones) in the two sides (pārśva).
02.05.210 | Ten in the chest (vakṣas).
02.05.211 | Three above the shoulders (aṃsa) and the akṣaka (probably collar-bones), on a conjectural reading.
02.05.212 | Ten in the neck (grīvā).
02.05.213 | Eight in the two cheeks (gaṇḍa).
02.05.214 | Eight in the two jaws (hanu).
02.05.215 | One each in the gala (throat), the kākala (uvula or the laryngeal prominence; identity disputed), the tongue (jihvā) and the head (mūrdhan).
02.05.216 | Two, two, in the palate (tālu) and the forehead.
02.05.217 | In the nose, the lips and the ears.
02.05.218 | In women, however, twenty more.
02.05.219 | Among these, ten are in the two breasts (stana). Their increase takes place in youth.
02.05.220 | Ten in the yoni (female genital tract, probably).
02.05.221 | Of those, two resting inside (abhyantara); resting at the mouth (mukha), round, two.
02.05.222 | Three resting on the path of the fetus (garbha).
02.05.223 | In which the fetus (garbha) stays.
02.05.224 | The yoni (female genital tract, probably), however, has the shape of a conch's navel (śaṅkhanābhi) and, on a conjectural reading, is three-whorled. In its third whorl, in the interval between pitta and the pakvāśaya (abode of digested matter, probably the large intestine region), is the garbhaśayyā (bed of the fetus, probably the uterus), shaped like the mouth of a rohita fish (rohu carp).
02.05.225 | In it are three peśī (muscles) entering śukra (semen) and ārtava (menstrual blood, probably).
02.05.226 | For by those—thin, dense, large, minute, broad, round, short, long, firm, soft, smooth and rough—the joints (saṃdhi), bones (asthi), sirā (vessels) and snāva (sinews, snāyu) in the body are covered.
02.05.227 | Of the openings (mukha) of the sirā and dhamanī (vessels), however, when divided minutely, there are twenty-nine hundred-thousands and nine hundred and fifty-six.
02.05.228 | By those this body is latticed (gavākṣita), bound round and stretched out.
02.05.229 | And that many are the head-hairs (keśa), the beard-hairs (śmaśru) and the body-hairs (loman). For the openings of those are bound to them; therefore they are nourished by those.
02.05.230 | And by those, those carry the sweat (sveda).
02.05.231 | Likewise they carry the potency (vīrya) of oil massage (abhyaṅga), paste (lepa) and the like, fully matured in the skin; and they take up touch (sparśa).
02.05.232 | The srotas (bodily channels), however, are indeed countless.
02.05.233 | Even so, the sirā and the rest will be taught elsewhere, broadly (in outline) and as their use requires.
02.05.234 | Thus the corporeal, firm entities (bhāva) in the body have been enumerated.
02.05.235 | As for the liquids (drava): that which, falling away through excess, binds on to the dung (purīṣa), likewise the urine, the blood and the other dhātus in the body.
02.05.236 | And what has gone through the whole body, which the outer skin holds; and what, within the skin, having gone to a wound, is called by the word lasīkā (lymph, probably); and what, bound with heat, flows out from the hair-pores and is called sweat (sveda): that is water (udaka). Of that and of the first, the essence of food named rasa, and likewise of blood (rakta), dung (śakṛt), phlegm (śleṣman), Pitta (probably bile), urine (mūtra), vasā (muscle-fat, probably), medas (adipose tissue, probably) and marrow (majjan), in each case by its own measure of a cupped handful (añjali), there is an increase of number beginning with one, in accordance with the preceding.
02.05.237 | Half a cupped handful (añjali) of śukra (semen).
02.05.238 | Just that much: the brain (mastiṣka) and ojas (vital essence).
02.05.239 | In women, four cupped handfuls (añjali) of rajas (menstrual blood, probably).
02.05.240 | Of breast milk (stanya), two.
02.05.241 | And both of these as well are of the nature of the clear essence (prasāda) of rasa.
02.05.242 | This is the measure for one whose dhātus are in balance (sama).
02.05.243 | Therefore one should discern the increase and the decrease, each by its own signs, and also the other, un-enumerated (on a conjectural reading), corporeal and non-corporeal entities (bhāva).
02.05.244 | Namely.
02.05.245 | The malas (waste products) of flesh, ear and the like, and vāyu (wind), intellect (buddhi), memory (smṛti) and the like.
02.05.246 | The followers of Dhanvantari (dhānvantarīya), on the other hand, read (recite).
02.05.247 | For bodies are of dissimilar characteristics, and the doṣas, dhātus and malas are always unsteady.
02.05.248 | Therefore it is impossible to specify the evenness (samatva) even of water and the others by measure, as for flesh and the rest.
02.05.249 | One should only recognise by inference through mere health.
02.05.250 | But all the parts (avayava), by division into atoms (paramāṇu), because of extreme subtlety, come to be countless.
02.05.251 | In the union and separation of those atoms (paramāṇu), wind (vāyu), impelled by action (karma), is the cause.
02.05.252 | This, understood by distinction of parts and by division, leads to bondage or to liberation.
02.05.253 | And here there is.
02.05.254 | Thus he who knows the body (kalevara) part by part, in all its parts.
02.05.255 | He never engages in what is harmful (ahita) through delusion.
02.05.256 | Thus the fifth chapter, named Aṅgavibhāga (Division of the Parts of the Body).
