05.03.1 | Now, therefore, we shall explain the accomplishment (siddhi) in the mishaps of vomiting and purging.
05.03.2 | In one who is very hungry, soft in the bowel, with little phlegm, hard to make vomit, or weak; or if the vomiting-medicine is of deficient dose, very sharp, very cold, or is drunk in indigestion, the vomiting-medicine drunk goes downward.
05.03.3 | In that case what is desired is not attained, and the waste matter (mala) is stirred up.
05.03.4 | Having quickly made him unctuous again with suitable means, one should make him vomit, with regard to dose, time and so on.
05.03.5 | And, remembering the earlier error, one should avoid the causes of mishap.
05.03.6 | In one with indigestion or one having phlegm, a purgative that is very hot, sharp, salty, or of great dose, or another medicine, goes upward.
05.03.7 | There: the mishap as stated before, and the remedy (sādhana).
05.03.8 | Even after this it rises upward; except for the hṛdya, avalekhana and vaṭaka, one should not apply a third medicine.
05.03.9 | In one with a hard bowel and very sharp fire (agni, the digestive fire), or if the medicine is of little potency, whether directed to the upper part or to the lower part, it undergoes digestion like food.
05.03.10 | There the aroused doṣas, if not expelled at the proper time, produce a disturbance (vibhrama).
05.03.11 | One should make him, whose bowel is well unctuous, drink a plentiful amount of food and the medicine.
05.03.12 | A decoction of little content (pratyaya), or a dry medicine, drunk by one not oiled and sweated, or with cold treatment, becomes ayogi, with the doṣas stiffened and clumped.
05.03.13 | In that case one should make him vomit again with vomiting; one who has been oiled and sweated one should purge again with purging; or one whose doṣas are much agitated, on the third day.
05.03.14 | And in both purifications, one anointed with salt and oil should be made to sweat by lump (piṇḍa, probably bolus fomentation) and layer (prastara, fomentation on a heated spread bed).
05.03.15 | In one who was oiled and sweated, and who drank a purgative without having been made to vomit, or one with raw (āma) matter, or one mildly purged, there occur in the lower part excessive heaviness and stiffness of the belly, piercing pain (śūla, colic), and obstruction of wind (vāta) and stool.
05.03.16 | Having given even him, the ayogin one, āsthāpana (decoction enema) with sharp (substances) together with urine, having fed him with jāṅgala juices (meat broths of dry-land animals), having given anuvāsana (oil enema) with oil prepared with phala, dāru and pippalī (long pepper), and having made him further unctuous with Vāta-taking-away oils, one should purge him again with sharp (substances).
05.03.17 | For one with Kapha abundant, very unctuous, heavy in the bowel, or of slow fire (agni), a medicine mildly administered, having greatly agitated the doṣas, produces dullness, drowsiness, weakness and sinking of the limbs.
05.03.18 | One should scrape that up quickly.
05.03.19 | One who has been made to fast (laṅghana), whose fire (agni) has been kindled, and who has again been made unctuous, one should purify with sharp and hot (ones).
05.03.20 | For one with residue and bloating, one with Vāta and phlegm, one dry, one with udāvarta (upward-turning), or one of slow fire (agni), a cold or dry medicine given produces great bloating (ādhmāna).
05.03.21 | There, from obstruction of waste, having bound up, it burns the belly, and there occur inner piercing pain (śūla, colic), fullness of the two flanks like a leather bag, pain of the head and back, śvāsa (probably labored breathing, dyspnoea), and pricking in the anus and bladder.
05.03.22 | One should treat him with the procedures of anointing (abhyaṅga, oil massage), sweating, wick (varti, medicated suppository), kindling, powder and basti (medicated enema), which take away udāvarta (reversed movement of Vāta, with obstructed stool and flatus) and ānāha (distension with retention).
05.03.23 | When a strong medicine is taken by one who is lean, of little strength, with soft bowel and soft fire, dry or oiled, sweated or with raw (āma) matter, it corrupts wind (anila) together with Pitta and brings about parikartikā (cutting-around pain, scissoring pain in the lower belly and anus).
05.03.24 | There: in the navel, bladder, anus and male organ, cutting-around pain with burning, obstruction of wind (anila), and binding (viṣṭambha, retention with constipation).
05.03.25 | One should give him āsthāpana (decoction enema) with picchā-bastis (slimy enemas) joined with black tila (sesame), madhuka (probably liquorice) and honey.
05.03.26 | Or with milk boiled with the milk-bearing trees (kṣīrivṛkṣa, the group of milky-sap trees, chiefly figs).
05.03.27 | And one should give him, sprinkled with cold water and having eaten with milk, anuvāsana (oil enema) with ghee-maṇḍa and with oil of yaṣṭīmadhuka (liquorice).
05.03.28 | For one who is lean, every procedure that is sweet and bulking is desired.
05.03.29 | In one with raw (āma) matter: fasting (laṅghana) and kindling (dīpana), and food and drink that is light, dry and hot.
05.03.30 | When one has become free of raw (āma) matter (as with treatments of middle strength, as by wind and sun, by scattering dust and ash, and so on), in accordance with Vāta one should employ in food and drink what is light, kṣāra and sour; when Vāta is in excess, curd together with sour, joined with the skin of dāḍima (pomegranate).
05.03.31 | And one should drink ghee (sarpis) together with dāḍima (pomegranate) juice.
05.03.32 | Or, with hot water, a paste (kalka) of tila (sesame) and devadāru (deodar cedar).
05.03.33 | In one with a hard bowel and many doṣas, a purifying medicine applied, of small dose and quality, mild or unctuous, having agitated the doṣas does not expel them; and, little by little, mixed with Pitta and Kapha, it flows out all round.
05.03.34 | And it brings about binding (viṣṭambha, constipation with distension), heaviness, drying (śoṣa), koṭha (probably wheals, urticaria-like eruption), itching, paleness (pāṇḍutā, pallor), sinking of the limbs and piercing pain (śūla).
05.03.35 | One should give him āsthāpana (decoction enema) with decoctions (niryūha) of tiniśa, dhava (axlewood), aśvakarṇa (probably sal), palāśa (flame of the forest) and valā (country mallow), joined with honey.
05.03.36 | And one whose flowing out has subsided, having again been made further unctuous, one should purify with sharp (ones).
05.03.37 | And when purified, one should employ kindling (dīpana) ones: powders, āsavas, ariṣṭas (medicinal fermented drinks) and so on.
05.03.38 | When one who has drunk the medicine sends forth the urge, or holds it back,
05.03.39 | then pravāhikā (dysentery; literally a 'flowing forth') arises in him.
05.03.40 | In that, the patient, flowing forth violently, passes Kapha that is with burning and pain, with slime, white, black, or with blood.
05.03.41 | He should treat him by the procedure of parisrava (a flowing round, oozing).
05.03.42 | In one who has drunk the medicine, from holding back the medicine, belching, vomiting and the like, Vāta and the others, agitated, resort to the heart and bring on a dreadful hṛdgraha (heart-seizure).
05.03.43 | Then, from the affliction of the chief vital spot (marma), the patient is tormented excessively by pains; joined with bewilderment, hikkā (hiccup), kāsa (cough), saliva and pain in the side, trembling, with lost consciousness, gnashing his teeth, with eyes turned up, he bites his tongue.
05.03.44 | They call this hṛdayāpasaraṇa (heart-withdrawal). To him the physician, not being bewildered, should quickly give a sharp avapīḍa, after anointing first and having made him sweat with grain-sweating.
05.03.45 | Vomiting-treatment (vamana) with taṇḍula-water (rice-water) mixed with yaṣṭīmadhuka (liquorice).
05.03.46 | Or with pungents, for one in whom Kapha predominates.
05.03.47 | Then he should cause the remainder of the doṣa to be cooked with pācanīya substances, and should give bastis (enemas) according to the prominence of the doṣa.
05.03.48 | A dry medicine used in excess by one who is not oiled and sweated, not continent, who holds back urges, who has a soft koṣṭha, or who is delicate, agitates Vāyu (wind) through excessive purging.
05.03.49 | By that there may be a seizing of all the limbs, and especially in the sides, back, hips, nape and vital spots: pain, fainting, giddiness, trembling, stiffness, pricking, splitting, twisting, and loss of consciousness.
05.03.50 | Having anointed him and made him sweat with grains, he should give anuvāsana (unctuous enema) with oil cooked with yaṣṭīmadhu (liquorice).
05.03.51 | And food and drink that takes away Vāta.
05.03.52 | In one who is oiled and sweated, of very soft koṣṭha, or hungry, a medicine used that is of sharp force or very abundant, having drawn out all the waste (mala) and even liquefied the dhātus (body tissues), would make them flow out; it becomes excessive in application (atiyogi).
05.03.53 | Having anointed him with ghṛta washed a hundred times, he should make him firm (stambhayet) with astringent, sweet and cold things, as smearing, pouring, immersion, food and drink, and with licked preparations (leha) containing sugar and honey.
05.03.54 | He should give to drink caṃdana (sandalwood), aṃjana, uśīra (vetiver root), goat's blood, cold water, elā (cardamom), and saktu meal of lāja (parched rice).
05.03.55 | And he should give him a picchā-basti (a basti of slime, i.e. a mucilaginous enema).
05.03.56 | Milk-ghee cooked with the sweet group, or the maṇḍa of ghee, as anuvāsana (unctuous enema).
05.03.57 | And he should carry out the procedure for raktapitta (a bleeding disorder).
05.03.58 | But especially in excess application of vamana, he should drink a mantha (churned drink) with ghṛta (ghee), sitā (white sugar) and kṣaudra (honey), together with fruit juices.
05.03.59 | A thin gruel (peyā) cooked with shoots of the milky trees (kṣīrivṛkṣa), with mākṣika; or milk; and milk cooked with binding (saṃgrāhin) substances, he should administer at the meal.
05.03.60 | And in vomiting with belching, he should lick dhanikā, madhuka (liquorice), madhurasa, mustā (nut-grass) and aṃjana with honey.
05.03.61 | In obstruction of speech, closing of the jaws, and affliction by wind, he should drink a thin gruel (peyā) of few taṇḍula (rice) grains cooked with ghṛta and meat-broth, and should administer oiling and sweating.
05.03.62 | When the tongue goes in: oily, sour and salty mouth-holdings (kavala and gaṇḍūṣa), and pleasing goat meat-broths.
05.03.63 | And in front of him he should have sour fruits eaten.
05.03.64 | The tongue that has come out, smeared with a paste (kalka) of tila (sesame) and drākṣā (grape or raisin), or rubbed with powder of vyoṣa (dry ginger, long pepper and black pepper) and salt, he should make enter.
05.03.65 | He should press the two eyes, turned aside and anointed with ghṛta.
05.03.66 | To one who has lost consciousness he should make hear the sounds of sāman chant, flute and song.
05.03.67 | In excess application of virecana (purgation), he should vomit with taṇḍula-water (rice-water), mixed.
05.03.68 | And he should drink the priyaṅgu group with taṇḍula-water; or lodhra together with aṃjana, and the skins of dāḍima (pomegranate); or kamala (lotus), utpala (blue water lily), samaṅgā and padma-kesara (lotus filaments).
05.03.69 | Or kṣaudra (honey), śarkarā (sugar), madhuka (liquorice) and the skins of uduṃbara (cluster fig).
05.03.70 | And he should carry out the procedure for raktātisāra (blood-atisāra, bloody diarrhoea).
05.03.71 | Having anointed the protruded guda (rectum), made it sweat, and made it firm with astringent things, he should make it enter.
05.03.72 | And as before, he should deal with obstruction of speech and the rest.
05.03.73 | In both cases, for the sake of knowing life-blood and raktapitta, he should put a white pichu (wad) or a piece of cloth (plota) into the blood.
05.03.74 | In that, on cleansing, when it is clean after washing with slightly warm water, he should know it to be blood; but in discolouration he should know raktapitta (the bleeding disorder).
05.03.75 | Or he should give food mixed with that to a dog or a crow.
05.03.76 | When that is eaten, jīva (life-blood); when it is not eaten, pitta.
05.03.77 | And up to death, for him afflicted by thirst, fainting and intoxication (mada), he should carry out the Pitta-destroying treatment and what is stated for excess (atiyoga).
05.03.78 | And he should quickly take the blood of khaḍga (probably the rhinoceros) and the rest, with honey, by the method stated for excessive flow (atisruta).
05.03.79 | Or milk boiled with śyāmā (a plant of disputed identity), kāśmarya (gmelina tree), dūrvā (Bermuda grass) and uśīra (vetiver root), on a conjectural reading joined with goat's ghee-maṇḍa, cool, he should pour into the basti (enema).
05.03.80 | And in the remaining complications (upadrava) too, of emesis and purgation improperly administered, he should counter them according to the disease, having regard to the doṣas, and should look to the basti-vyāpat-siddhi.
05.03.81 | Thus too, in cleansing through both portions (ubhayatobhāga), he should rise up according to the condition.
05.03.82 | What is parikartikā (cutting pain) in purgation, that in emesis is injury of the throat (kaṇṭha-kṣaṇana); what is the discharge (parisrava), that is the flow of Kapha (kapha-praseka); what is straining (pravāhaṇa), that is dry belching (śuṣka-udgāra).
05.03.83 | And on this point there are (the following).
05.03.84 | Excessive, at the wrong time, scanty, not soaked (bhāvita) with (ones) of equal potency (vīrya),
05.03.85 | Improperly prepared, old, the medicine would surely go wrong (fail).
05.03.86 | Not vomiting (chardana) but, on a conjectural reading, bad vomiting; bad purging (durvireca), not purging (recana).
05.03.87 | He should make (him) drink the medicine again; it destroys that (or him); for indeed they are so.
05.03.88 | In whom what is drunk, ānulomika (going in the natural direction), goes upward mixed with Kapha,
05.03.89 | But him who has been made to vomit with plain (ones), is cleansed and has been made to fast, he should make drink (it).
05.03.90 | When the doṣa is held back, scanty, and flowing after a long time, he should drink hot water.
05.03.91 | By that, distension (ādhmāna), vomiting, thirst and obstruction (vibandha) subside.
05.03.92 | If the medicine, obstructed by a doṣa, proceeds neither upward nor downward,
05.03.93 | In that case, (if it is) with belching or with pain in the limbs, he should apply sweating (sveda).
05.03.94 | Of (a person) properly purged and with belching, the medicine he should quickly scrape up.
05.03.95 | But when (it is) undigested and does not set in motion, the physician should check (it) with very cold (ones).
05.03.96 | Sometimes the medicine, obstructed by Kapha (śleṣman), stays in the chest.
05.03.97 | When Kapha has diminished, in the evening or at night, it moves on.
05.03.98 | When (it is) digested, staying, or even gone upward, he should feed dry food.
05.03.99 | But when the medicine (is held) by Vāyu (wind), he should drink another (one), with fat (sneha) and salt.
05.03.100 | Thirst, delusion, giddiness, fainting and the rest may occur, and while the medicine is being digested.
05.03.101 | In that case a medicine that is Pitta-destroying, sweet and cool is recommended.
05.03.102 | From salivation, nausea (hṛllāsa), obstruction (viṣṭambha) and horripilation (hair standing on end), when (it is) enveloped by Kapha,
05.03.103 | In that case, the medicine that is sharp and hot, pungent and the rest, Kapha-dispelling, beneficial.
05.03.104 | Contrary movement of the anus, knottedness (grathitatva), with heaviness.
05.03.105 | Stirring up of the doṣa (utkleśa), severe distension (ādhmāna), cutting pain (parikarta), discharge (parisrava).
05.03.106 | Pravāhikā (probably dysentery), seizing of the heart (hṛdgrahaṇa), gripping of the whole body.
05.03.107 | With the flow of all the dhātus, these twelve are stated, with their remedies.
05.03.108 | Complications (vyāpad) from a lapse in the application (yoga), even in purgation of various kinds,
05.03.109 | And in those various complications that are arising through a deviation in cleansing (saṃśodhana),
05.03.110 | By this very direction he should quickly strive for the cure (siddhi); for the śāstra is the light of sound and unsound conduct (naya, durnaya).
05.03.111 | Thus the third chapter, named Vamana-virecana-vyāpat-siddhi (the cure of the complications of emesis and purgation).
