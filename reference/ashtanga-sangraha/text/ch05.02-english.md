05.02.1 | Now, therefore, we shall explain the preparations of purgation (virecana-kalpa).
05.02.2 | Among purgation substances the foremost are: among roots, trivṛt (turpeth); among barks, tilvaka; among milks, snuhī (a spurge); among fruits, harītakī (chebulic myrobalan).
05.02.3 | Indeed, the root of trivṛt (turpeth) is of two kinds: reddish and dark.
05.02.4 | Of these two, the reddish one is astringent and sweet, pungent in vipāka (the post-digestive stage), dry, and removes Kapha (śleṣman) and Pitta; moreover, through a particular preparation it pacifies all ailments; because it purges easily, it is recommended in the young, the aged, the delicate and those whose koṣṭha (abdomen, bowel) is soft; and it is what is denoted by the word trivṛt.
05.02.5 | The other one too is somewhat inferior to that in qualities; it is sharp, pulls at the throat and heart, and causes fainting and bewilderment; because it quickly removes the doṣas, it is recommended in those with many doṣas, who can bear distress, and whose koṣṭha (abdomen, bowel) is hard; and it is what is denoted by the word śyāmā.
05.02.6 | Now, of the two, one should collect the root that is not spread out sideways, has gone deep, and is smooth.
05.02.7 | Then, having dried the bark, one should keep it properly.
05.02.8 | In ailments of Vāta one should drink that powder, combined with śuṇṭhī (dried ginger) and saindhava (rock salt), with sour things or also with meat juice.
05.02.9 | In (ailments) arising from Pitta, (one should drink it) with milk, honey, drākṣā (grape or raisin), ikṣu (sugarcane), the expressed juice of kāśmarya (gmelina), and ghee, with sweet decoctions, and with sugar.
05.02.10 | In (ailments) born of Kapha, (one should drink it) with urine, honey, alkali (kṣāra), ariṣṭa (fermented medicinal liquor), pīlu juice, and the decoctions of triphalā (the three myrobalans) and of pañcakola (the five-kola group), mixed through with vyoṣa (the three pungents) powder.
05.02.11 | And with these very substances, each according to its own, one should prepare lehas (licked preparations), pills, modakas, and bhakṣya and bhojya foods: sweet with ghee, sugar, akṣoḍa (walnut) and tavakṣīrī; sour with the juice of mātuluṅga (citron), dāḍima (pomegranate), āmalaka (emblic myrobalan), kola (jujube), karamardaka (karaunda) and kapittha (wood-apple), and with buttermilk; salty with saindhava (rock salt); sharp with vyoṣa (the three pungents); filled with various vesavāra (a spiced preparation); and fragrant with sahakāra (mango) juice, trijātaka (cinnamon bark, cardamom and bay leaf), nāgakeśara (Ceylon ironwood stamens) and karpūra (camphor); thus.
