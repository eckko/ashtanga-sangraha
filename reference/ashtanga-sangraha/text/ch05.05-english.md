05.05.1 | Now, therefore, we shall explain the preparation (kalpa) of the siddhabasti (a named enema; lit. 'proven enema').
05.05.2 | One should always employ siddhabastis (the named enemas) for all embodied beings.
05.05.3 | (They are) free from mishap, of many benefits, producing strength and intelligence, and pleasant.
05.05.4 | Honey and oil, equal; a karṣa (a unit of weight); from rock salt (saindhava), two picu (a unit of weight); miśi (probably dill).
05.05.5 | The nirūha (decoction enema) made with a decoction of the root of eraṇḍa (castor), with honey and oil.
05.05.6 | It is rejuvenating (rasāyana) and dispels prameha (a group of urinary disorders), arśas (haemorrhoids), worms, gulma (abdominal lump) and antravṛddhi (intestinal hernia).
05.05.7 | And with licorice (yaṣṭīmadhu), good for the eyes, conquering raktapitta (a bleeding disorder).
05.05.8 | The yāpana (sustaining enema), with a thick paste, and having honey, oil, fat (vasā) and ghee.
05.05.9 | It conquers pain in the anus, knee, thigh, back and hip (on a conjectural reading of the damaged word), bladder region and penis (mehana).
05.05.10 | One should prepare, with prasṛta-portions (prasṛta, a handful measure), with ghee, honey, fat (vasā) and oil.
05.05.11 | The yāpana (sustaining enema), accompanied by half an akṣa (a unit of weight) of rock salt (saindhava) and half a pala (a unit of weight) of hapuṣā (probably juniper berries).
05.05.12 | A decoction of the root of eraṇḍa (castor), honey, oil, with rock salt (saindhava).
05.05.13 | This is the yuktaratha enema (basti), with vacā (sweet flag), pippalī (long pepper) and fruit (phala).
05.05.14 | With a decoction, honey, paiṃḍhuṃthā (on a conjectural reading; the printed word is unclear), śatāhvā (probably dill), asafoetida (hiṅgu) and rock salt (saindhava).
05.05.15 | Suradāru (devadāru, deodar cedar) and rāsnā (probably Pluchea lanceolata), removing the faults of the enema (basti-doṣa), auspicious.
05.05.16 | A decoction of the pañcamūla (the group of five roots), oil, māgadhikā (long pepper) and honey.
05.05.17 | With rock salt (saindhava) and madhuka (probably licorice), it is called the siddhabasti.
05.05.18 | One should cook the dvipañcamūla (the double five-root group, ten roots), triphalā (the three fruits), fruit (phala) and bilva (bael).
05.05.19 | In cow's urine, by that, and with the ground (ones) of pāṭhā (probably Cissampelos pareira), vatsaka (probably kuṭaja) and toyada (probably musta).
05.05.20 | With fruits, with honey and oil (both), with kṣāra and with salt (lavaṇa).
05.05.21 | The enema (basti) is suitable in Kapha disorders, in pāṇḍu disease (anaemia with pallor) and in visūcī (acute vomiting and purging, a cholera-like illness).
05.05.22 | In obstructions of semen and of wind (anila), and in āṭopa (rumbling distension) of the bladder region (basti), it is esteemed.
05.05.23 | Musta (nut-grass), pāṭhā, amṛtā (guḍūcī, Tinospora cordifolia), eraṇḍa (castor), balā (probably Sida cordifolia), rāsnā and punarnavā (Boerhavia diffusa).
05.05.24 | Mañjiṣṭhā (Indian madder), āragvadha (Indian laburnum), uśīra (vetiver), trāyamāṇā (identity disputed), akṣa and rohiṇī (on a conjectural reading of the end of the line: akṣa probably bibhītaka; which drug rohiṇī means is unclear).
05.05.25 | The lesser (kanīyaḥ) pañcamūla (group of five roots), of the weight of one pala, and eight madana (probably emetic nut, Randia dumetorum).
05.05.26 | One should cook that in an āḍhaka (a measure of volume) of water, with a quarter remaining, strained.
05.05.27 | Joined with two prasthas (a measure of volume) of milk, one should cook it again until the milk remains.
05.05.28 | With jāṅgala-rasa (the juice of dry-land animals) sapāda (one and a quarter; the unit is not stated), and with ghee, honey and rock salt (saindhava).
05.05.29 | With the ground (ones): yaṣṭī (licorice), miśi (probably dill), śyāmā (identity disputed), kaliṅga (probably kuṭaja seed), kara (an unclear word, on a conjectural reading) and añjana (collyrium).
05.05.30 | The enema (basti), pleasantly warm, increases flesh, the digestive fire (agni), strength and semen.
05.05.31 | Vātāsṛk (wind-blood disorder, probably gout), meha (urinary disorder), fat (medas), arśas (haemorrhoids), gulma (abdominal lump) and retention of stool and urine.
05.05.32 | Viṣamajvara (irregular fever), vīsarpa (spreading skin inflammation, erysipelas), an unclear word (on a conjectural reading dhmāna, flatulence), ādhmāna (bloating) and pravāhikā (dysentery with straining).
05.05.33 | The pains of the groin, thigh, waist, flank, nape of the neck, ear and head.
05.05.34 | It may destroy asṛgdara (abnormal menstrual bleeding), insanity (unmāda), swelling (śopha), cough (kāsa) and aśmakuṇḍala (an unclear word, on a conjectural reading).
05.05.35 | Good for the eyes, giver of sons, the king of the yāpanas (sustaining enemas), a rejuvenative (rasāyana).
05.05.36 | One should cook the pañcamūla (the five roots) without gokṣura (small caltrops), each separately in a portion of one pala.
05.05.37 | In an āḍhaka of milk (a measure of volume), four prasthas (a measure of volume), joined with the ground (ones) beginning with licorice (yaṣṭi).
05.05.38 | The enema (basti), joined with honey, oil, ghee and sindhūttha (rock salt), is highly esteemed.
05.05.39 | Especially for children, the old, women, the delicate and those of a comfortable (sukha) nature.
05.05.40 | Likewise, prepared with sahācara (probably prickly yellow barleria), balā (country mallow), sārivā (Indian sarsaparilla) and darbha (probably sacred kuśa grass).
05.05.41 | A milk enema (kṣīra-basti), likewise with abhīru (probably shatavari), guḍūcī (giloy) and the pair of bṛhatī.
05.05.42 | Milk cooked with a paste (kalka) of māgadhikā (long pepper) and yaṣṭīmadhuka (licorice).
05.05.43 | The pañcamūla (five roots), those beginning with bṛhatī, each drug measured at one pala.
05.05.44 | Two palas of śāli (winter rice), godhūma (wheat), yava (barley) and māṣa (black gram), together with yaṣṭikā (probably licorice).
05.05.45 | Goat's milk cooked with those, the fluid (rasa) of a fowl's (kukkuṭa) egg, and sitā (crystallised sugar).
05.05.46 | With ghee, honey and the two salts (dvilavaṇa), by those the enema (basti) is a maker of semen (śukra), supremely.
05.05.47 | This is the formula (kalpa) also in the fluids of the eggs of the peacock (śikhin), gonarda, fish and the like.
05.05.48 | The juice (rasa) of crab (kulīra) flesh, joined with the fluid of the eggs of the caṭaka (sparrow).
05.05.49 | With sugar (śarkarā), ghee and honey, the enema (basti) is considered the most aphrodisiac (vṛṣatama, 'most bull-like').
05.05.50 | With testes (muṣka) from the he-goat and the boar, and with the flesh of crab (kulīra) and sparrow (caṭaka).
05.05.51 | Milk cooked with the testis of a he-goat, uccaṭā (a plant, not identified) and ikṣuraka (probably kokilākṣa, Hygrophila spinosa), and honey.
05.05.52 | On a conjectural reading, an enema (basti) rich in ghee and with little salt is the most aphrodisiac (vṛṣatama), supreme.
05.05.53 | Food (bhojya) with milk cooked with ātmaguptā (cowhage), uccaṭā and ikṣura.
05.05.54 | Thereafter, he who uses the enemas (basti) at ten-ten days.
05.05.55 | On a conjectural reading, nourished like a stallion, [unclear word: 'sadṛṣaḥ'], he goes to a hundred women.
05.05.56 | These, joined with honey (mākṣika), make a man exceedingly potent (ativṛṣa, 'very bull-like').
05.05.57 | And they, stambhin (checking, retaining), bring about neither excess (atiyoga) nor lack (ayoga).
05.05.58 | For the most part, nirūha enemas are scraping (lekhana) and sneha enemas (sneha-basti) are nourishing (bṛṃhaṇa).
05.05.59 | In the yāpana enemas there is both; therefore anuvāsana (oil enema) in them is not desired.
05.05.60 | But he in whom these, when administered, should not return because of mildness.
05.05.61 | Having given an āsthāpana (nirūha) with sharp enemas together with urine, he quickly indeed.
05.05.62 | Swelling (śopha), loss of the digestive fire (agni), pallor, colic pain (śūla), haemorrhoids (arśas, piles), and cutting pains (parikartikā).
05.05.63 | Fever and diarrhoea may occur, from excessive use of yāpana.
05.05.64 | In that case a kindling treatment (dīpanī kriyā) with ariṣṭa, kṣāra, sīdhu and so forth is desired.
05.05.65 | Therefore one should use yāpana too, with judgement and as occasion arises.
05.05.66 | From the combination with mild and little medicine, and from a quantity reduced by a quarter.
05.05.67 | And from use for a short time; in those there is absence of avoidance (niṣparihāratā, no restrictions).
05.05.68 | In the drugs meant for yāpana, from cooked sneha, separately, each.
05.05.69 | Or in all, one should apply enemas (basti) free from restrictions.
05.05.70 | From sahācara, abhīru, balā, rāsnā and gokṣuraka (puncture vine), separately.
05.05.71 | A tulā (of each), in a hundred droṇas of water, when five droṇas remain.
05.05.72 | When strained and cooled: bisa (lotus stalk), drākṣā (grape or raisin), nata (probably tagara), vakṣīrī (unclear) and nidigdhikā (yellow-berried nightshade).
05.05.73 | On a conjectural reading, a list of drugs, most of it unclear, in which the name mahāsahā is possible.
05.05.74 | Jīvaka, ṛṣabhaka, udīcya (probably hrīvera), mṛṇāla (lotus fibre), utpala (blue lotus or water lily) and candana (sandalwood).
05.05.75 | Kharjūra (date), the kernel (majjā) of tāla (palmyra), ātmaguptā (cowhage), āmalakī (Indian gooseberry) and kaṇā (probably long pepper).
05.05.76 | Paṭola (pointed gourd), medā, bark (tvak) and leaf (patra), śītapākī, and those named odana.
05.05.77 | Having made them into a paste (kalka), one should put (them) into that, and separately a prastha measured.
05.05.78 | The juice (rasa) arising from the testes of the boar, buffalo and he-goat, likewise.
05.05.79 | Oil and ghee (taila and sarpis), arising from the eggs of the peacock, the cock and the swan,
05.05.80 | The expressed juice (svarasa) of dhātrī (emblic myrobalan) and of vidārī (probably Pueraria tuberosa), and two āḍhakas of cow's milk,
05.05.81 | And that which is prepared with the sounds of brahma, bherī (kettledrum) and mṛdaṅga (drum),
05.05.82 | Shaded by a white umbrella, covered with a white cloth,
05.05.83 | Placed on the shoulder of an elephant, having worshipped Vṛṣadhvaja (the bull-bannered one, Śiva),
05.05.84 | Having performed the svastyayana (auspicious rite), one should give a sneha-basti (unctuous enema) without restriction.
05.05.85 | Having obtained excessive virility by that, he would exhaust a hundred women.
05.05.86 | And he would become free of wrinkles and grey hair, handsome and long-lived.
05.05.87 | Beneficial for those whose semen (śukra) is lost, who are injured, who are wasted, and who have irregular fever (viṣamajvara).
05.05.88 | For those whose menstrual blood (ārtava) and semen (śukra) are spoiled, a giver of sons and a rasāyana (rejuvenating tonic).
05.05.89 | And in this way other bastis (enemas) also are to be devised, and are to be cooked, in many ways.
05.05.90 | One should have it cooked a hundred times or a thousand, when it is possible.
05.05.91 | Sneha-bastis (unctuous enemas) that destroy the doṣas will be described, together with their precautions (parīhāra).
05.05.92 | The daśamūla (the group of ten roots), varā (probably triphalā), rāsnā (probably Pluchea lanceolata), aśvagandhā (ashwagandha, Withania somnifera) and punarnavā (Boerhavia diffusa),
05.05.93 | Guḍūcī (giloy), eraṇḍa (castor), bhūtīka, bhārgī (Clerodendrum serratum), vṛṣaka (probably Justicia adhatoda) and śirīṣa (Albizia lebbeck),
05.05.94 | Śatāvarī (Asparagus racemosus), sahacara (probably Barleria), kākanāsā, and (the one) having a pala as share.
05.05.95 | Yava (barley), māṣa (black gram), atasī (flax), kola (probably jujube berries) and kulattha (horse gram), measured by prasṛta.
05.05.96 | Having boiled (it) in a vaha of water, with a droṇa remaining, and with that.
05.05.97 | One should cook an āḍhaka of oil with (ones) to be ground, the life-promoting (jīvanīya) (ones), measured by a pala.
05.05.98 | This, called anuvāsana (unctuous retention enema), dispels all disorders of Vāta.
05.05.99 | The fat (vasā) of the marsh-dwelling (ones), prepared similarly with the jīvanīya (ones).
05.05.100 | Oil cooked with śatāhvā (dill), yava (barley), bilva (bael) and sour (ones), for wind (samīraṇa, that is Vāta).
05.05.101 | Ghee (ghṛta), heated with rock salt (saindhava) of fire colour, and conquering Vāta.
05.05.102 | Jīvantī (Leptadenia reticulata), madana (emetic nut), medā, śrāvaṇī (probably Sphaeranthus indicus), madhuka (probably liquorice) and balā (Sida cordifolia),
05.05.103 | Śatāhvā (dill) and ṛṣabhaka, kṛṣṇā (probably pippalī, long pepper), kākanāsā and śatāvarī,

05.05.106 | Nourishing (bṛṃhaṇa), destroying Vāta and Pitta, increasing strength, semen (śukra) and the fire (agni, the digestive fire).
05.05.107 | Removing ailments of menstrual blood (rajas) and semen (śukra), and the putrīya (son-related) anuvāsana.

05.05.130 | Then, when the bull among sages (muni-vṛṣabha) had spoken his words and stopped, he again made a request.
05.05.131 | This very profound (matter): bowed down to the ground together with the other disciples, having placed the joined hands (añjali) in front.
05.05.132 | The basti (enema), said by the teacher to produce varied actions and benefits, goes upward then, and not from the navel.
05.05.133 | And quickly arrives, 'anusa' (unclear), from the body on all sides; how does it drive away the doṣas?
05.05.134 | The teacher said: this of the body, whatever is dependent outside or within, is subject to the wind (māruta) that is stimita (still, stagnant).
05.05.135 | Producing the natural direction (anulomatā) in that spoiled (wind), the basti destroys, on a conjectural reading, the 'viṣamāntirogapūgān' (unclear; the end of the word is 'groups of diseases').

05.05.138 | For wind (vāyu) is the doer of the scattering and the gathering of the accumulations of wastes (mala): faeces, drying (śoṣa), Pitta and so on.

05.05.145 | Fully, very soon, the samāna (wind) obtains (it).
