02.02.1 | Now, therefore, we shall explain the Śārīra (the section on the body) called Garbhāvakrānti, the descent (entry) of the embryo.
02.02.2 | When the old rajas (menstrual blood) has gone, the new one is established, and the receptacle of the embryo (the womb, garbhāśaya) and the passage are clean, the śukra (semen), in the form of seed, unaltered, impelled by unaltered wind (vāyu), followed by the other great elements (mahābhūta), mingled with ārtava (menstrual fluid), probably at once, and joined with the jīva (the living soul) - which follows the sway of the afflictions beginning with passion, is impelled by its own action (karma) and is swift as the mind - goes to the womb.
02.02.3 | And because effects follow their causes, it attains the nature of that (cause).
02.02.4 | And for that very reason: a male from the abundance of śukra (semen), a female from the abundance of ārtava (menstrual fluid), a napuṃsaka (neither male nor female) from the equality of the two.
02.02.5 | When śukra and ārtava (semen and menstrual fluid) are split into pieces by the wind (anila), there is the origin of offspring according to the divisions.
02.02.6 | When vitiated: of the forms of other wombs.
02.02.7 | Now the sign of a woman who has just conceived: the taking in of the seed by the yoni (vagina), satisfaction, heaviness, throbbing, and the continued connection of śukra and ārtava.
02.02.8 | Likewise, on a conjectural reading: great joy, nausea, drowsiness, limb-weariness, flow (praseka), heart-distress, exhaustion and thirst.
02.02.9 | But gradually, in a woman whose pregnancy has become manifest: heaviness of the belly (kukṣi) alone, on a conjectural reading, weakness of eye and voice, balancing (saṃtulana) of eye and hair (sense unclear), sleep, yawning, fainting, vomiting (chardi), distaste for food, swelling of the feet, and craving for the sour, in those various states.
02.02.10 | And the paths of her srotas (bodily channels) that carry rajas (menstrual blood) are blocked by the embryo.
02.02.11 | Therefore, from then on, the ārtava (menstrual fluid) is not seen.
02.02.12 | Then that, checked below—other, other—increasing of itself, they call aparā (probably the afterbirth, placenta).
02.02.13 | Others say: jarāyu (probably the foetal membrane).
02.02.14 | But when the blood has settled, a line of hair (romarājī) appears, and the blood goes upward to the remainder of the jarāyu.
02.02.15 | From that, plumpness of cheeks and breasts, and blackness of lips and nipples.
02.02.16 | That which has its resting place in the breasts, on a conjectural reading tinged by Kapha, having reached the state of breast-milk (stanyatā) in her who has given birth, is nourished again by the rasa of food.
02.02.17 | Of these, in the first month a kalala (the first embryonic mass, probably the early embryo) arises.
02.02.18 | In the second month, a dense mass (ghana), or a peśī (muscle-like lump), or an arbuda (lump); from these, in order, males, females and neuters (napuṃsaka).
02.02.19 | In the third month it sprouts in five ways.
02.02.20 | Namely:
02.02.21 | The two thighs (legs), the two arms and the head.
02.02.22 | And at the very time of the sprouting of the thighs and the rest, all the limbs, parts and sense organs (indriya) come to be simultaneously, except those born in the time after birth, the teeth and the rest; but in order they become distinct.
02.02.23 | This is the prakṛti (the natural, normal state).
02.02.24 | Otherwise than this is vikṛti (deviation, the abnormal state).
02.02.25 | And, each according to its own, the states (bhāva) of the embryo that conform exactly to the sign (liṅga) of one of male, female or neuter become established in the mind and in the body.
02.02.26 | On a conjectural reading: but in a mixture of signs, the distinguishing (determination) is from where there is greater abundance; from that, the states are those of one or the other.
02.02.27 | Namely:
02.02.28 | Klaibya (unmanliness), timidity, lack of confidence, bewilderment, unsteadiness, heaviness below, lack of compactness, laxity, softness, the states of the womb and seed, and likewise other fitting (ones): these are the woman-making states.
02.02.29 | Those opposite to these are the man-making (ones).
02.02.30 | The mixed (ones) are the neuter-making (ones).
02.02.31 | And then its consciousness (cetanā) becomes fully manifest.
02.02.32 | And from then on it moves (throbs) and has desire for the five objects of the senses.
02.02.33 | For its heart is born of the mother, and is joined to the mother's heart by dhamanīs (vessels) that carry the rasa of that (tad, referent not stated).
02.02.34 | Therefore, in the two, through those (vessels), a longing (śraddhā) arises.
02.02.35 | And so they call a woman with two hearts (dvihṛdayā) daurhṛdinī (a woman with longings).
02.02.36 | Others, however, give the time of the daurhṛda (longing) as from three fortnights up to the fifth month.
02.02.37 | And when the wish is intense, one should give her, as desired, even the unwholesome, joined with the wholesome.
02.02.38 | For, from disregard of the daurhṛda (longing), the wind (vāyu), agitated and moving about inside the body, may cause destruction (probably miscarriage) or deformity of the embryo.
02.02.39 | But she whose longing (daurhṛda, the longing of pregnancy) has been fulfilled bears a vigorous and long-lived son.
02.02.40 | In the fourth, on a conjectural reading, the division into limbs and minor limbs is manifest, and the embryo (garbha) becomes stable.
02.02.41 | In the fifth, the mind (manas) becomes more awakened, and there is an increase of flesh (māṃsa) and blood (śoṇita).
02.02.42 | In the sixth, hair, body-hair, nails, bones (asthi), sinews (snāyu) and so on become manifest, and there is an increase of strength and complexion (varṇa).
02.02.43 | In the seventh, completeness of all the limbs.
02.02.44 | In the eighth, the embryo (garbha) from the mother and the mother from the embryo, by the channels (vāhinī) that carry rasa (nutrient fluid), repeatedly take ojas (vital essence) from each other.
02.02.45 | Therefore at that time the pregnant woman is at one moment glad and at another languid, and likewise the embryo (garbha).
02.02.46 | Thus the birth of the embryo (garbha) is then accompanied by calamity (vyāpatti).
02.02.47 | Because of the unsteadiness of ojas (the vital essence).
02.02.48 | For thus, in this one who is on the point of coming out, experiencing turning round and the like, this is the separation from ojas (the vital essence).
02.02.49 | And even if there may be breathing of this one for some time, that is made by the continuation of the impression (saṃskāra) of ojas, like that of a severed limb.
02.02.50 | But in the mother, because of the stability of her ojas, when rasa (nutrient fluid) has passed over in one part, there is only weariness (glāni).
02.02.51 | Others, however, say.
02.02.52 | Because, on a conjectural reading, it is the Nairṛta share (the share belonging to Nirṛti), there, death of the embryo (garbha).
02.02.53 | Therefore, in order to prevent delivery (prasava), the woman should be bathed, pure, chaste (brahmacāriṇī, observing brahmacarya) and intent on the worship of the deities.
02.02.54 | And here one should offer a bali (offering) of meat (māṃsa) and boiled rice (odana).
02.02.55 | Even when one day has passed in that (period), they speak of a time of delivery up to a year.
02.02.56 | After this, (he) becomes disordered (vikārin, subject to disorder).
02.02.57 | The embryo (garbha), facing the mother's back, with the palms joined (añjali) at the forehead and the limbs contracted, stays in the garbha-koṣṭha (probably the uterine cavity), the male (pumān) resting on the right side, the female (strī) on the left, the neuter (napuṃsaka) in the middle.
02.02.58 | And staying there, the embryo (garbha) sleeps when the mother is sleeping and wakes when she is awake.
02.02.59 | And because of the dependent functioning (paratantra-vṛtti) of the embryo (garbha).
02.02.60 | From niṣeka (impregnation) onward, the moistening (upasneha) and sweating or warming (upasveda) of the womb (garbhāśaya) are the means of sustenance (vartana). Then, in this one whose limbs and minor limbs are becoming manifest (on a conjectural reading), a duct (nāḍī, probably the umbilical cord) is fastened at the navel; in the duct, the aparā (placenta; on a conjectural reading); in that, the mother's heart (hṛdaya).
02.02.61 | Then from the mother's heart the rasa of food (āhāra-rasa), pulsating through the vessels (dhamanī), reaches the aparā (placenta).
02.02.62 | Then, in sequence, to the navel.
02.02.63 | And then it again, in the embryo's pakvāśaya (the site of digested matter, probably the large intestine), being cooked by its own bodily fire (kāyāgni), becomes, through the abundance of the clear part (prasāda), nourishing to the dhātus and the rest.
02.02.64 | Likewise, through the hair-pores there is moistening (upasneha), and the rasa itself having become payas (milk or water).
02.02.65 | In the embryo (garbha) not yet born, because food and drink do not enter directly and because the rasa is free from mala (waste; amala), there is no arising of gross waste (sthūla-mala).
02.02.66 | But at the time of birth, through contact with the wind of parturition (prasūti-māruta, probably the contractions of labour), having turned round, he comes out head downward; and afterwards the aparā (placenta), detached from the mother's heart.
02.02.67 | This is the natural state (prakṛti).
02.02.68 | Otherwise than this is abnormality (vikṛti).
02.02.69 | There, she who puts the right foot forward first, whose right arm is strong and who acts with it, in whose breast milk is seen first, whose belly (kukṣi) is very raised and the embryo (garbha) round (on a conjectural reading of this compound), who delights in longings (daurhṛda) for seeing, touching and asking about male-named (ones), who sees dreams bearing male names, with a clear complexion of the face, keen hunger, an upward line of body-hair, and who desires men, she bears a son; in the opposite case, a girl; and in a mixture, a napuṃsaka (neuter).
02.02.70 | At the time of the question, whichever limb or external substance having a gender (liṅga) the woman touches, one should declare her embryo (garbha) to have that same gender.
02.02.71 | But when Vāta, gone astray, dries up the channels (srotas) of the embryo (garbha) that carry rasa, then he is born suffering from a Vāta disease and deficient.
02.02.72 | Or he stays in the belly (udara) for many years.
02.02.73 | When the purpose (artha) is accomplished equally for woman and man.
02.02.74 | Or the seed (bīja) of the two, of equal portion and face to face.
02.02.75 | And when the seed (bīja) becomes vitiated in a part, then a napuṃsaka (neuter) comes to be, having two natures (dvi-prakṛti) and two seeds (dvi-retas).
02.02.76 | When the woman first has her purpose accomplished, then afterwards semen (śukra) is emitted by the man, and the Vāta of the woman, whose mind is unsteady with joy, vitiates [it] and damages the channels (srotas) of this one that carry virility (puṃstva), then a vātendriya (lit. 'Vāta organ') comes to be.
02.02.77 | In sexual union it releases only Vāta (wind-humour).
02.02.78 | When, however, it wholly damages, or, without damaging, simply closes the mouth of the channel (srotas),
02.02.79 | Then (it) becomes outside refining treatment (saṃskāra). There the treatment is aphrodisiac (vājīkara) enemas (basti), eating or swallowing (abhyavahāra), and mind-gladdening (ones) too.
02.02.80 | For these, bestowing strength on the semen (śukra), nourish its channels (srotas).
02.02.81 | When a man of little seed and little strength, agitated, possessed of aversion to women, or desiring another, causes an obstruction of intercourse with the woman, or the woman is of that kind towards the man,
02.02.82 | Then āsekya. The woman and the man become of that kind. Then he becomes vakradhvaja (one with a bent dhvaja, the penis); his dhvaja (penis) is not made stiff at all.
02.02.83 | When an embryo (garbha) arises of a woman (on a conjectural reading of the printed striyā as striyāḥ) with a putrid vagina (yoni),
02.02.84 | From tasting that semen (śukra) he gets the raising of the dhvaja (penis).
02.02.85 | When 'pumurubhāvapi tatsaugaṃdhikāraṇyaṃ' (printed forms unclear; on a conjectural reading only), he obtains strength from the smell of the yoni and the dhvaja.
02.02.86 | When the two, overcome by jealousy and of little delight, are joined, then the name 'īrṣyālu' (jealous one)
02.02.87 | Of him, on seeing another who has become coupled (in union), the engagement in sexual intercourse arises.
02.02.88 | When there is destruction of the testes (vṛṣaṇa) of the male embryo through the fault of wind (vāyu) and fire (agni), then (it) becomes the one called vāyupiṇḍaka.
02.02.89 | These eight kinds (yoni) of ṣaṇḍha (the impotent), because of the variety of action (karma), are said to be mixed.
02.02.90 | When, however, the doṣas slightly vitiate the woman's blood (śoṇita) and uterus (garbhāśaya), then in the embryo that comes to be, whichever mother-born part (avayava) of that embryo has its doṣa reach provocation in the seed (bīja) or in the seed-portion, into that very part a deformity (vikṛti) enters.
02.02.91 | When, indeed, in her seed called blood (śoṇita), situated in the uterus (garbhāśaya), the forming seed (nirvartaka bīja) is vitiated, then it produces a barren one (vandhyā).
02.02.92 | When its part (avayava), then, on a conjectural reading, (one) of spoiled offspring (pūtiprajā) —
02.02.93 | Of her, each one born dies.
02.02.94 | When again that part (avayava) is one part of those seed-portions (bījāṃśa) that produce the female-making (ones) and the body-dependent (ones) beginning with downward heaviness,
02.02.95 | Then it produces (one) mostly of form and shape, a non-woman (astrī), named vārtā.
02.02.96 | They call her a strī-vyāpad (a woman's defect).
02.02.97 | In just the same way, for a man: 'when, in the seed called śukra, the seed is vitiated' and so on, one should apply it: a barren (one), one of spoiled offspring; and likewise (one) mostly of male shape, a non-male, named mṛṇāsuliṅgā, it produces. By this explanation of the deformity of the mother-born and father-born parts, the deformity of the parts born of sātmya (what suits), of rasa and of sattva (mind) is explained too.
02.02.98 | Of whichever limb-part (aṅga-avayava), in its seed or seed-portion, an origin occurs, of that very limb-part a deformity arises.
02.02.99 | (It) does not arise, because of absence of affliction.
02.02.100 | And when (she) who has obtained a pregnancy resorts, indeed, to Vāta-increasing (ones), the printed 'anakṣam' being unclear (conjecturally 'constantly'), then her provoked Vāyu, moving along the body and lodging in the uterus (garbhāśaya), produces in the embryo the states of being dull (jaḍa), deaf (badhira), mute (mūka), nasal-voiced (minmiṇa), stammering (gadgada), lame (khañja), hunchbacked (kubja), dwarfish (vāmana), deficient in limbs and excessive in limbs, or another Vāta disorder (vātavikāra).
02.02.101 | Likewise Pitta, like Vāyu: baldness (khalati), grey hair (palita), then the printed 'śmaśruhīnatvakchranakhe śa' (unclear; on a conjectural reading only), tawniness (paiṅgalya) and so on.
02.02.102 | Kapha, however: kuṣṭha (leprosy and related skin disease), kilāsa (probably vitiligo), the unclear printed 'sadaṃtavā', and so on.
02.02.103 | Disorders (vikāra), the printed 'trivargaumiśrān' being unclear (on a conjectural reading only).
02.02.104 | Moreover.
02.02.105 | Light (tejas) that has not reached the share of sight (dṛṣṭibhāga) is congenital blindness (jātyandhatva). That same, followed by Vāta, has deformed, dry, reddish eyes; followed by Pitta, tawny eyes; followed by Kapha, white eyes; followed by blood (rakta), red eyes.
02.02.106 | For the pregnant woman (garbhiṇī) has the same welfare (yogakṣema) as the embryo.
02.02.107 | Therefore one should protect her especially, by the pleasant and beneficial (ones), from those that cause injury to the embryo.
02.02.108 | As the embryo reaches growth, so, from the burden, the food and the error (apacāra), on a conjectural reading, there is loss of the woman's strength.
02.02.109 | In this matter, these are the (ones) causing injury to the embryo.
02.02.110 | Namely —
02.02.111 | Sexual intercourse (vyavāya), exertion (vyāyāma), emaciation (karśana), blows (abhighāta), excessively agitating riding in vehicles, keeping awake at night, sleep by day, holding back urges (vega), undigested (ajīrṇa), sun-heat, fire, anger, grief, fear, fright, fasting, squatting (utkaṭaka, probably), uneven and hard seats or postures, falling into a pit or well, seeing and hearing disagreeable (things), and so on.
02.02.112 | In brief, all food and drink that is excessively heavy, hot, sharp (tīkṣṇa) and dry, and harsh activities.
02.02.113 | Likewise.
02.02.114 | For the sake of protection from deities, rakṣas (demons) and non-increase (anupacaya), (she) should not wear red garments, should not mount a vehicle, should not consume madya (an intoxicating drink) and meat.
02.02.115 | And whatever else old women may also say.
02.02.116 | For a woman who sleeps stretched out and face up, again, the fetus's nāḍī (probably the umbilical cord), resting on the navel, having wound around the neck, causes a mishap (vyāpatti).
02.02.117 | She who sleeps uncovered, and she who roams at night, produces a mad (one).
02.02.118 | She who is quarrelsome by nature: one with apasmāra (epilepsy).
02.02.119 | She who is given to sexual intercourse (vyavāya): an ill-shaped, shameless (on a conjectural reading) or womanish (straiṇa) (one).
02.02.120 | She who is constantly given to grief (śoka): a timid, wasted or short-lived (one).
02.02.121 | She who is covetous: one who torments others, a jealous or womanish (one).
02.02.122 | She who is a thief, however: one abounding in toil (āyāsa), hostile, or given to inaction (akarma).
02.02.123 | She who is intolerant: a fierce or probably deceitful (aupadhika) (one).
02.02.124 | She who is given to sleep: a drowsy, foolish (one), or one with small fire (agni), weak digestion.
02.02.125 | She who is constantly given to madya (an intoxicating drink): one prone to thirst, of poor memory, or unsteady.
02.02.126 | She who is constantly given to godhā-flesh (meat of the monitor lizard): one with aśmarī (urinary stone), or one who passes urine slowly.
02.02.127 | She who is constantly given to varāha-flesh (meat of the wild boar or pig): a red-eyed (one), a krathana (sense uncertain), or one with very rough hair.
02.02.128 | She who is constantly given to fish-flesh: one with slow blinking, or a stiff-eyed (one).
02.02.129 | She who is constantly given to the sweet (madhura): one with prameha (a group of urinary disorders, probably diabetes-type), a mute, or a very stout (one).
02.02.130 | She who is constantly given to the sour (amla): one with raktapitta (a bleeding disorder, probable), or one with diseases of skin and eyes.
02.02.131 | She who is constantly given to the salty (lavaṇa): one with early wrinkles (valī) and greying (palita), or a bald (one).
02.02.132 | She who is constantly given to the bitter (tikta): one with śoṣa (a wasting disease, probably consumption-type), a weak, or a wasted (one).
02.02.133 | She who is constantly given to the pungent (kaṭuka): a weak (one), one with little śukra (semen), or one without offspring.
02.02.134 | She who is constantly given to the astringent (kaṣāya): a dusky (one), one with ānāha (distension of the belly with retention), or one with udāvarta (a disorder in which Vāta runs upward).
02.02.135 | Whatever is the cause (nidāna) of whatever disease, the pregnant woman (antarvatnī) who indulges in each of those produces offspring abounding in the corresponding disorders.
02.02.136 | By this, the faults of śukra (semen) that are born of the father are also explained.
02.02.137 | Therefore the two, wishing for success of offspring (prajā-sampad), should look after themselves well.
02.02.138 | Especially the woman (nārī).
02.02.139 | And one should treat her diseases with mild, sweet, cool and pleasant medicines (auṣadha), foods and regimens (vihāra).
02.02.140 | And one should not apply to her emesis (vamana) and the rest.
02.02.141 | Not bloodletting (raktāvasecana); but only, beginning from the eighth month, āsthāpana (decoction enema) or anuvāsana (oil enema).
02.02.142 | But in disorders curable by emesis (vamana) and the rest, one should proceed with mild (ones) or with those that accomplish the same purpose.
02.02.143 | Like a full vessel of oil, one should attend to the pregnant woman (garbhiṇī) without shaking (her).
02.02.144 | And on this matter there are these.
02.02.145 | Thus the rule in a non-critical (anātyayika) disease; but in a very severe (one)
02.02.146 | Even with sharp applications of treatment (kriyāyoga), one should carefully protect the woman.
02.02.147 | For woman is the root of offspring; for woman, when protected, protects.
02.02.148 | Of all the āśramas (stages of life), first, blameless, the state of a householder.
02.02.149 | When the mouth is covered by the jarāyu (fetal membrane) and the throat is enveloped by Kapha,
02.02.150 | And because of obstruction of the path of wind (vāyu), the one staying in the womb does not cry out.
02.02.151 | Thus the second chapter, named Garbhāvakrānti (the descent of the embryo).
