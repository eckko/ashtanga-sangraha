05.01.1 | Salutation to the revered Gaṇeśa.
05.01.2 | Now, therefore, we shall explain the Vamana-kalpa (the formulations for emesis).
05.01.3 | Thus indeed said Ātreya and the other great sages.
05.01.4 | Among the substances for emesis, the fruits of madana (emetic nut), jīmūtaka, ikṣvāku, kukkṛtikā, śātakī and kuṭaja (kurchi) are the best.
05.01.5 | Among these too, the madana (emetic nut) fruit.
05.01.6 | And the precedence among these, and among these preparations, is according to doṣa, dūṣya (what is vitiated) and the like.
05.01.7 | And because of the very many different states of doṣa and the rest, the formulations, mishaps and means in emesis and the like are innumerable.
05.01.8 | Therefore, in order to show the intelligent the path of variations, the Kalpasiddhi section is taught as a mere example.
05.01.9 | Then, in the interval between spring and summer, on an auspicious day and at an auspicious moment, one should take madana (emetic nut) fruits that are neither very green nor very pale.
05.01.10 | Having wiped them, tied them in a bundle of kuśa grass and smeared it with cow-dung, and (having kept them) for eight nights in a heap of any one of yava (barley), husk (busa), māṣa (black gram), śāli (winter rice), vrīhi (rice), kulattha (horse gram) or mudga (green gram), one should take them out and dry them in the sun.
05.01.11 | From the well-dried ones, having taken out the pippalīs, rubbed with curd, ghee, honey and palala, and dried them again, one should fill a new, dust-free pot up to the neck and have it stored well guarded.
05.01.12 | Then, at the time of use, one should coarsely crush a fist (with the mouth inward) of those fruit-pippalīs, or as much as one thinks fit, and, in the decoction of any one of yaṣṭīmadhuka (liquorice), kovidāra (orchid tree), karbudāra, nīpa (kadamba), vidula, bimbī (ivy gourd), śaṇapuṣpī, sadāpuṣpī or pratyakpuṣpī (prickly chaff-flower), or else kept for a night, rubbed and strained, have it drunk by the method stated in the Sūtra section.
05.01.13 | Especially in phlegm (śleṣma), fever, gulma (abdominal lump), pratiśyāya (coryza, common cold) and internal abscesses, again until Pitta comes.
05.01.14 | By that one vomits well.
05.01.15 | Or the powder of fruit-pippalī steeped in its own decoction, with triphalā (harītakī, bibhītaka and āmalakī: the three myrobalans) powder in three parts, one should drink with a decoction of kovidāra and the rest, in strong Kapha, praseka (excessive salivation), apaci (glandular swellings of the neck), arbuda (tumour), granthi (nodular swelling, cyst), fever, udara and arocaka (loss of relish for food).
05.01.16 | Or the powder of the kernel of the madana (emetic nut) fruit, with a decoction of jīmūtaka and the rest, when Pitta has gone to the seat of Kapha.
05.01.17 | Or milk cooked with the kernel of the madana (emetic nut) fruit, or a gruel (yavāgū) in that, in raktapitta (a bleeding disorder) in the lower part and in burning of the heart (heartburn).
05.01.18 | Or a palmful of the santānikā (skin, milk-cream) of that cold milk, when Pitta is aggravated and the chest, throat and heart are smeared with thin Kapha.
05.01.19 | Or the curd born of that, or the upper part of the curd (dadhyuttara), in Kapha, vomiting, praseka (excessive salivation) and tamaka (a form of breathlessness, asthma).
05.01.20 | Or the fresh butter produced from that milk, cooked with a decoction of the six beginning with phala and mixed with the powder of the six phalas, when the fire (agni, the digestive fire) is overcome by Kapha and the body is drying up.
05.01.21 | Or one should use an electuary (leha) prepared with the decoction of any one of the substances beginning with āragvadha (golden shower tree), excluding ghoṇṭā, bhūnimba and bāṇa, together with soma-bark and the pañcakolaka (pippalī, pippalīmūla, cavya, citraka and dry ginger), mixed with the powder of the fruit kernel.
05.01.22 | Or, mixed with the powder of the fruit kernel, one should eat an utkārikā or a modaka prepared with the decoction of any one of these twenty-two: reṇukā, elā (small cardamom), śatāhvā, kustumburu (coriander), tagara, kuṣṭha (costus root), tvac, koraka, maruvaka (marjoram), aguru (agarwood), guggulu (gum guggul), vālaka, śrīveṣṭaka, paripelava, māṃsī (spikenard), śaileyaka (stone-flower lichen), sthauṇeyaka, surasā (holy basil), pāledhana, pūti, aśoka and rohiṇī.
05.01.23 | Or, with ground tila (sesame) and śāli (winter rice) grain pressed with the juice and decoction of fruit-pippalī and mixed with its decoction, or mixed with the decoction of any one of the substances beginning with surasā, one should prepare śaṣkulī cakes, apūpa cakes or another food, and eat it.
05.01.24 | Or the juice of the fruit-pippalīs, as heard in the bhallātaka (marking nut) procedure, having cooked it until it coats the ladle, one should have it licked.
05.01.25 | Or one should mix that electuary in foods and drinks.
05.01.26 | And one should prepare foods and drinks with its decoctions alone.
05.01.27 | Or the decoction of the fruit kernel, with an addition of vatsaka (kuṭaja) and the rest, mixed with the decoction of either nimba (neem) or arka (Calotropis, madar), removing all Kapha ailments arising from satiation (saṃtarpaṇa).
05.01.28 | Or the fruit-pippalīs, well steeped twenty-one times in a decoction of phala and the rest, one should dust over with a powder like flower pollen; in the evening, in a lake, a large lotus; that kept for a night, and in the morning taken out and powdered again; having drunk up to the neck any one of haridrā (turmeric), kṛsarā (a rice-and-pulse dish, khichdi), milk or gruel, with rock salt, jaggery and phāṇita (thick cane-juice syrup) added, smelling, a delicate person, whose Kapha and Pitta are stirred by a fitting abundance of fragrance and who hates medicine, thus vomits easily.
05.01.29 | By this, all garlands, perfumes, wraps and cloths are explained.
05.01.30 | And in just this way, in the absence of fruit, one should prepare madana (emetic nut) flowers and unripe fruits (śalāṭu).
05.01.31 | Thus the procedure for the fruit has been stated.
05.01.32 | And in this way one should prepare jīmūtaka and the rest also.
05.01.33 | But especially in fever, śvāsa (breathlessness, asthma), hiccup and the like, jīmūta.
05.01.34 | There: in flowers, milk; in fruits that are hairy, a thin gruel (peyā); in the remaining fruits, santānikā (milk-cream); in those without hair, curd or the upper part of the curd.
05.01.35 | In the green and pale-yellow ones, milk or sour curd.
05.01.36 | Or one should use surā (grain liquor) mixed with its decoction in Kapha, arocaka (loss of relish for food), cough, pāṇḍu disease (anaemia) and yakṣman (consumption; includes what is now called tuberculosis).
05.01.37 | The powder of the ripe, old, or dried fruits of jīmūtaka (bitter luffa), in vātapitta.
05.01.38 | Two or three (of them), crushed, steeped in the decoction of any one of the fresh ones beginning with āragvadha (golden shower tree) or of paṭolī (pointed gourd), and churned, in fever (jvara) born of Pitta and phlegm.
05.01.39 | Jīmūtaka powder or paste with cold water, in what is born of Pitta.
05.01.40 | That same, with warm (water), in what is born of Vāta and Kapha, or of Kapha.
05.01.41 | With the expressed juice of any one of jīvaka, ṛṣabhaka, ikṣu (sugarcane) and śatāvarī (wild asparagus), in what is born of Pitta.
05.01.42 | Or ghee cooked as before.
05.01.43 | Ikṣvāku (bitter bottle gourd), however, is especially to be applied in kāsa (cough), śvāsa (breath-trouble; dyspnoea), jvara (fever), chardi (vomiting), viṣa (poison) and phlegm-ailments.
05.01.44 | Milk boiled with the sprouts of that one while it is without flower and fruit, in one whose Pitta is excessive and in fever of Pitta and Kapha; but in the case of the flowers and the rest, the applications are like those of rāta-jīmūta.
05.01.45 | Or the patient, desiring emesis, should drink the expressed juice of the ikṣvāku fruit prepared in threefold milk, in Pitta, in kapha-pīnasa (chronic nasal catarrh) and in svarasāda (hoarseness, loss of voice).
05.01.46 | Or one should pour milk into an old fruit from which the seeds have been taken out.
05.01.47 | The curd formed in it, in phlegm-kāsa (cough), śvāsa (breathlessness), aruci (distaste for food, anorexia) and chardi (vomiting).
05.01.48 | Or ikṣvāku seeds soaked in she-goat's milk, in viṣa (poison), gulma (abdominal lump), udara (belly-ailment, abdominal enlargement), galagaṇḍa (neck-swelling, goitre), granthi (knot, nodular swelling), arbuda (growth, tumour) and ślīpada (elephantiasis, filariasis).
05.01.49 | Or the inner part of the ikṣvāku fruit, in kuṣṭha (skin diseases, including leprosy), viṣa, gulma (abdominal lump), udara (belly-ailment), gara (concocted poison), gaṇḍa (swelling), granthi (nodular swelling), arbuda (tumour) and ślīpada (filariasis).
05.01.50 | Ikṣvāku fruit with whey, in kuṣṭha (skin diseases, including leprosy), viṣa and pale-ailments (pāṇḍvāmaya, anaemia).
05.01.51 | Or sadripaka; or buttermilk with honey and saindhava.
05.01.52 | Or one should eat that same inner part together with a pala of jaggery.
05.01.53 | Or one should drink the oil of the ikṣvāku fruit.
05.01.54 | Or a mantha (stirred drink) with saktu (parched-grain flour) soaked in the expressed juice of that same fruit, in phlegm-fever, kāsa (cough), throat-ailments and arocaka (distaste for food, anorexia).
05.01.55 | Ikṣvāku paste with meat broth, in Vāta-Kapha, gulma (abdominal lump) and persistent fevers.
05.01.56 | Or one should boil tumbī (bottle gourd) seeds in a decoction of a prastha of bilva (bael) root.
05.01.57 | Then, into that, in which the ghee is a third part, one should add tumbī (bottle gourd) seeds ground equal to the ghee, and, each at half that portion, jīmūta, mahājālinī, vatsaka (kuṭaja) and kṛtavedhana, and trikaṭuka (dry ginger, long pepper and black pepper) equal to the decoction.
05.01.58 | Then, having boiled it again, one should prepare a leha (licked preparation).
05.01.59 | Having licked it, one should drink a pramathyā afterwards, and likewise ghee; the procedure (kalpa) is to be understood as stated separately in each of the four members of the great five roots, beginning with kāśmarya.
05.01.60 | Dhāmārgava, especially in gara (concocted poison), gulma (abdominal lump), udara (belly-ailment), kāsa (cough) and the ailments arising from an accumulation of Kapha; in those that are firm and heavy, in those located in the seat of phlegm, and in Vāta-phlegm whose seat is the throat.
05.01.61 | Having prepared lehas (licked preparations) joined with the powder of any one of the jīvanīya (life-promoting) group, with honey and sugar, and made with decoctions of those, one should prescribe them in the phlegm condition in which Pitta is secondary.
05.01.62 | Dhāmārgava paste with a broth of tumbaru and dhānya, in poisons.
05.01.63 | With the decoction of any one of sumanas, saumanasyā, yavānī (ajwain), haridrā (turmeric), coraka, haimavatī, mahāsahā, kṣudrasahā, vṛścīva, arbī seeds, punarnavā (hogweed) and kāsamarda, or one or two of dhāmārgava, in disorders of the mind.
05.01.64 | Ghee produced from milk cooked with the root of dhāmārgava, or ghee cooked with the six beginning with phala, in disorders of the mind.
05.01.65 | One should prepare kṛtavedhana, exceedingly pungent, sharp and hot, especially for gara, udara, gulma (abdominal lump), plīha (spleen), pale-ailments (pāṇḍu-roga, anaemia) and swelling (śvayathu, oedema).
05.01.66 | One should apply kuṭaja (kurchi) fruit, on the other hand, in delicate persons, in those in whom blood, Pitta and Kapha are prominent, and in vātaśoṇita (gout), visarpa (erysipelas), jvara (fever), kuṣṭha (skin diseases, including leprosy) and the rest.
05.01.67 | Having soaked kuṭaja fruit powders for seven days in the milk of arka (Calotropis), or in the decoction of any one of the madanaphala (emetic nut), jīmūtaka, ikṣvāku, jīvantī and jīvaka roots, or in water of any one of sarṣapa (mustard), madhūka (mahua) and salt, or kuṭaja fruit powder with kṛśarā, one should prepare it for emesis.
05.01.68 | And here there is this:
05.01.69 | This is the indication of emetics, of those especially useful.
05.01.70 | With this seed, the wise one should devise others also.
05.01.71 | Thus the first chapter, named Vamanakalpa (preparations for emesis).
