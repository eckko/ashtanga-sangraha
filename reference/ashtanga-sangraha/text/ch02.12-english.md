02.12.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the Dūtādivijñānīya (knowledge of the messenger and the rest), belonging to the body (śārīra).
02.12.2 | Riṣṭa (a sign of approaching death), which rests on the patient, has been indicated.
02.12.3 | Of the messenger too, the kind (jāti), dress, speech, behaviour, place and time, and of the physician, his meeting with him, intention, setting out, going and entering, the movement of the planets, a previous occurrence, and the sights of dreams of the patient, indicate what is auspicious and inauspicious.
02.12.4 | Among these, a messenger who is higher in varṇa (class) than the patient, of another stage of life (āśrama), a heretic (pākhaṇḍa), a eunuch, shaven-headed, matted-haired, a woman, diseased, wearing a single garment of many kinds (aneka): old, discoloured, dirty, torn, wet; smeared with mud and oil, with red garlands and ointment, deformed in limbs, form and actions, riding a donkey, camel or buffalo, agitated, panting, weeping, harsh, speaking inauspicious things, calling from afar, a bearer of grass, clod, charcoal (on a conjectural reading), ashes, husk, lead, bone, hair, head, forehead, nose, mouth, tooth, tongue, neck, nail, ring finger, finger, breast, belly, navel, back, waist, penis, lower leg (jaṅghā), skull (kapāla; or potsherd), flesh (pala), stone, pestle, winnowing basket, shoe, broom, saline soil (on a conjectural reading), weapon, edge of a cloth, fringe, cotton, broken, fallen, spṛk (unclear), wood, staff, balance, rope, noose, leather, bamboo, empty vessel and vessel of oil or fat (sneha); releasing the ground with his feet, scratching, having taken a ripe fruit or a worthless (one) or another of that kind, in a place or at a time similar in quality to the disease, approaching the physician, is inauspicious.
02.12.5 | That is as follows.
02.12.6 | On a conjectural reading, at dawn, one standing near liquids (ghee, water and the like), very cold and stiff, is inauspicious in a Kapha disease, but auspicious in a Pitta disease.
02.12.7 | Likewise, at the breaking of a dam it is inauspicious in vomiting (chardi), meha (urinary disorder) and diarrhoea (atisāra) and so on, but at the building of a dam it is auspicious.
02.12.8 | Likewise, at midday, at both twilights, at midnight (on a conjectural reading), on the fourth, sixth and ninth lunar days, on parva days, on seeing an eclipse (uparāga) of a graha or portents (utpāta), and under the nakṣatras Bharaṇī, Kṛttikā, Ārdrā, Āśleṣā, Maghā, Mūla and the Pūrvā ones, (one) is inauspicious; and he who approaches the physician when he is asleep, naked, lying on the ground, anointed with oil, with loosened hair, facing south, agitated, cutting, breaking, inclined to fire-rites and rites for the ancestors, thinking and speaking what is inauspicious; or he who, having made an añjali toward the southern direction, stands on one foot, unevenly; and when the messenger is telling the abode of the patient, if the physician notices an inauspicious sense-object or imagines one, he should not follow him.
02.12.9 | In the contrary case, however, by one who is like mother and father to the patient, with a mind soft with compassion and endowed with cheerfulness, complete health (ārogya) is to be held out to the patient by comforting.
02.12.10 | At setting out, going and entering: curd, honey, ghee, parched grain (lāja), flowers, unbroken rice, udumbara (cluster fig), aśvattha (peepal), pearl, gem, kuśa grass, a mirror (on a conjectural reading), a maiden, a banner, a chariot, a kingdom, a gift, a die, a king, prosperity (śrī), a tree, an auspicious seat; crops, fish, horses, a swan or goose, a cāṣa (probably the Indian roller), a bull, a lion, a right-turning conch, a lotus, white mustard (siddhārthaka), rocanā (probably gorocanā), a svastika, an arched gateway, kīcaka (probably hollow bamboos that sound in the wind), bamboos, an unclear word (vaṃruṇa) with an elephant whose cheek is drenched with honey(-like) rut-fluid, a hanging cloud heavy with water (on a conjectural reading), a full pitcher, turmeric, a mountain, Indra, fruits, fresh meat (on a conjectural reading), a white chowrie, a cow with calf, a woman with a son, a human, vardhamāna, a thousand men, harnessed vehicles, Dhanvantari, a dwarf (vāmana), a brāhmaṇa, the earth raised up, cooked food, a boar risen from the water, the three-eyed one, the slayer of Madhu, Skanda, the lotus-seated one, the world-guardians, the gods, the sky, the directions, fire, lakes, flowing rivers, oceans, sacrifices, wind, the science of the heavenly bodies (jyotiṣa), the treatises on dharma, sacred bathing places (tīrtha), poetry resting on dharma, artha and kāma, the Vālakhilyas and others, the word of the Veda, a treasure, Kaustubha, gold, Maṇibhadra, Sumeru, priyaṅgu (probably Callicarpa macrophylla), a lamp, vacā (sweet flag) and a modaka (a sweet), Diti, Aditi, Arundhatī, understanding (buddhi), Sāvitrī, Umā, the celestial Gaṅgā, Śacī, Devasenā, nectar, peace, Lakṣmī, Sarasvatī, offspring, forbearance, the word 'svasti', the vaṣaṭ-call, the syllable oṃ, the puṇyāha formula and acts of dharma: this hundred and eight (aṣṭottara) of auspicious things (maṅgala), by sight, by touch and by mention, remove the inauspicious and bestow the highest success of the purpose.
02.12.11 | And further.
02.12.12 | Birds with male names, on the left, are auspicious; śivā (probably the she-jackal), śyāmā, piṅgalā, kokilā (cuckoo), an unclear word (ralā), the musk shrew (chuchundarī), the sow, the house lizard and single-hoofed animals, having female names, are auspicious on the right; the peacock, bear, śrīkaṇṭha and dog, thus, not; the elephant, the goat and payas (milk or water) also; but at entering they are reversed. And everywhere, on a conjectural reading, the going of a dog and a jackal from the right to the left is undesired; the reverse for beasts and birds.
02.12.13 | In every way, also, the sight of the bharadvāja (probably the skylark), cāṣa (probably the Indian roller), bhāsa (probably the vulture) and mongooses.
02.12.14 | Of hare, snake, goat, sheep, monitor lizard, rājāhaka (unclear) and boar (on a conjectural reading), the mention is desired, but not the sight; and the cry; the reverse for bear and monkey.
02.12.15 | But everything of the saraṭa (probably the chameleon or garden lizard), fowl, cat and owls is undesired.
02.12.16 | Likewise, in granthi (nodule), arbuda (tumour) and the like, the word 'cutting' (cheda) is desired; in gulma (abdominal lump), udara (probably an abdominal disease) and the like, the word 'breaking' (bheda); in atisāra (diarrhoea), raktapitta (a bleeding disorder) and the like, the word 'stopped' (ruddha); in aśmarī (urinary stone), udāvarta (upward-turning of wind) and the like, the word 'impelling' (pravartana): thus.
02.12.17 | In this way other sounds too are to be applied.
02.12.18 | And everywhere, the words sthira (firm), sthāvara (stationary), vardhamāna (increasing), trāta (protected), acala (immovable), dhruva (fixed), kṣema (safety), śiva (auspicious) and the like, and other sounds of musical instruments, are best.
02.12.19 | The words sneeze (kṣuta), struck, fallen, outcry, blow, cutting, breaking, burning, 'hā hā', prohibition and the like are forbidden; in a forbidden and in a blazing direction, cruel cries of birds and beasts; or on a dry, withered tree with creepers and thorns, or in a place of that kind; and, at the entering, the sight of grass, palala (sense uncertain: mud, flesh or sesame paste), a bundle of wood, a potsherd, a clod and so on, of a thorn, of one with raised foot, of vehicles, beds, seats and so on, broken, household articles, empty pots, madya (an intoxicating drink), fat (vasā), oil, a pregnant woman, a śvapāka (dog-cooker, outcaste), one in distress (vyasanin) and the dead; and, on a conjectural reading, the going out of the previously mentioned auspicious things from the patient's house; and for the physician who has entered, no honour, and gain, satisfaction and praises, auspicious and inauspicious (ones) of the patient; on a conjectural reading, the planets (graha) retrograde and following-retrograde (vakra, anuvakra), standing in condemned positions; the overpowering of the birth-nakṣatra by Ketu, Saturn and Rāhu; or the appearance of condemned signs in house, wife, bed, seat, vehicle, gem, jewel, equipment: for the prohibition of treatment.
02.12.20 | A dream, seen by oneself or by another, is a cause of the auspicious and the inauspicious. It is of seven kinds: seen, heard, experienced, desired, imagined, bhāvika (relating to what will be), and born of the doṣas.
02.12.21 | Among these, the first five kinds are fruitless; likewise (one) according to one's own natural constitution (prakṛti), forgotten, by day, too short, too long, and seen when not deeply asleep; in the first part of the night it gives a little fruit, and after a long time.
02.12.22 | At gosarga (daybreak, the time cows are let out), great and at once.
02.12.23 | And not struck down again by sleep, or by contrary words, or by giving, appeasing (śamanīya) and the like.
02.12.24 | Among these, he who, drinking madya (an intoxicating drink) with the departed (pretas), is dragged by a dog, is struck down by fever (jvara).
02.12.25 | He who sees an ambara (garment or sky) of the hue of red lac, or who, with red body, garland and cloth, laughing, is carried off by a woman, he by raktapitta (a bleeding disorder).
02.12.26 | He who the southern direction by any one of donkey, camel, buffalo and boar, he by yakṣman (wasting disease; consumption).
02.12.27 | He in whose heart a thorny creeper, a bamboo or a palm grows, he by gulma (abdominal lump).
02.12.28 | He on whose head, he by a disease of the head or by the cutting off of the head.
02.12.29 | He of whom, naked, sprinkled with ghee, offering into a flameless fire, lotuses grow on the chest, he by kuṣṭha (a skin disease; the leprosy group).
02.12.30 | He who drinks sneha (oil or fat) with caṇḍālas (outcastes), he by prameha (a urinary disorder, probably of the diabetes type).
02.12.31 | He who, dancing with demons (rakṣas), sinks in the waters, he by unmāda (insanity).
02.12.32 | He who is intoxicated, he by apasmāra (epilepsy).
02.12.33 | He who sees an eclipse of the moon and the sun, in him a disease of the eyes arises.
02.12.34 | He who the falling of those two, for him destruction of the eyes.
02.12.35 | One who, having eaten or swallowed śaṣkulī (probably a kind of fried cake) or apūpa cakes, vomits on waking; on whose head creepers and the like grow and birds settle, or foolishness (mauḍhya) occurs; who is attended by vultures, owls, crows and the like, or by pretas (departed spirits), piśācas (flesh-eating demons), rakṣases (demons), Draviḍas, Āndhras, women, caṇḍālas (men of the lowest caste) and the like; or who, facing south, is dragged by a dancing woman, or by crows and the like; or who himself, naked (sky-clothed), goes to a deserted forest, or in a monkey-chariot; who is embraced by pretas or by renunciants (pravrajita); or who, naked, wears a red garland; who, smeared with mud, laughs or dances; who gets entangled in a thicket of vetra (probably cane), bamboo, thorns and grass; or who falls on ground that has dust for a pillow, or in anthills, ashes, cremation grounds, pits, abodes; or who sinks in mud, wells, turbid water, or is carried along by a current; or who enters a land with forests of red flowers, a cave filled with darkness, an abode of evil action, a confined place, or a mother; or who is swallowed by a fish; and who undergoes beard-work, growth of the body and marriage; who, on a conjectural reading, falls from the top of a mountain or palace, or climbs sacrificial posts, flower-rich (ones), śālmalī (silk-cotton tree), kiṃśuka (flame-of-the-forest), kovidāra (probably mountain ebony) and pāribhadraka (probably coral tree); or who is defeated, is bound, vomits, is purged, rejoices, or obtains gold, iron, salt, oil, oil-cake, cotton; eats cooked food, drinks madya (an intoxicating drink) or sneha (oil or fat); or is anointed, or is rebuked by angry forefathers; or sees any one of moon, sun, stars, lamp, deities, eyes, teeth perishing; or a crooked (thing), or an arm, or a naked (one) being split apart; or men who are naked, wearing ochre-coloured (kaṣāya) garments, fierce (asaumya, not gentle), with staff and noose in hand, black, red-eyed; or a woman who is black, of white appearance, with long hair and breasts, having the form of Kālarātri (dark night): they, being sick, attain pañcatā (lit. 'five-ness', death).
02.12.36 | But the healthy (ones): a doubt about life.
02.12.37 | And on this matter there are:
02.12.38 | Because of the fullness of the mind-carrying channels (srotas) with powerful malas (waste matter),
02.12.39 | Terrible dreams are seen by (ones) whose selves are turned toward destruction.
02.12.40 | One who, having seen even an inauspicious dream, right there again
02.12.41 | should see a gentle, auspicious (one), for him the result would be auspicious indeed.
02.12.42 | Gods (deva), twice-born (dvija), cows and bulls, living friends, kings,
02.12.43 | Good men (sādhu), famous (ones), a kindled fire, very clear water-bodies (jalāśaya),
02.12.44 | A maiden, boys, fair (ones), white-clothed (ones), very radiant (ones),
02.12.45 | A man-eating (one), a blazing-bodied (one), sprinkled with blood all around,
02.12.46 | He who should see, or who obtains, an umbrella (chatra), a mirror (ādarśa) and viśāmiṣa (unclear)
02.12.47 | White flowers (sumanas), cloth, a smearing with impure (matter), fruit,
02.12.48 | Mountains, palaces, fruit-bearing trees, lions, men, elephants,
02.12.49 | Should climb (them) and a horse-vehicle, and, on a conjectural reading, should cross rivers, lakes (hrada) and oceans (udadhi).
02.12.50 | Going by the east-north (north-east), approaching the unapproachable, death,
02.12.51 | Emerging from a confined place, the touching of stars, mountains and radiant (ones),
02.12.52 | Weeping, the rising of one who has fallen, and the crushing of enemies,
02.12.53 | He for whom there would be life, health (ārogya) and wealth, he often attains, on a conjectural reading.
02.12.54 | Of forefathers (pitṛ), gods (deva) and twice-born (dvijāti) who are angry, frightened and emaciated in person,
02.12.55 | of (ones) with dirty garments and flowers, the sight is not praised.
02.12.56 | But of those very (ones) when pleased, pure in person with flowers and garments,
02.12.57 | the sight is praised in a dream, and conversation with them is auspicious.
02.12.58 | Having seen terrible or good (śobhana) dreams, bathed in the morning, sarpaṣānamivarṇān (unclear),
02.12.59 | having offered with the Sāvitrī (a sacred verse) (ones) smeared with ghee, or, on a conjectural reading, sesame seeds, purified, one is freed from sins and from diseases.
02.12.60 | The retinue endowed with auspicious conduct, and likewise the patient (ātura),
02.12.61 | Having faith, favourable, and having an abundant collection of materials (dravya),
02.12.62 | Association with the marks of sattva (a quality of mind), devotion to physicians and twice-born,
02.12.63 | Non-despondency in treatment: that is the sign of health (ārogya).
02.12.64 | Thus, here, since birth and death have been properly set forth,
02.12.65 | therefore this section (sthāna) of the body is called Śarīra (body).
02.12.66 | Thus the twelfth chapter, named Dūtādijñānīya
02.12.67 | Thus, in the Aṣṭāṅgasaṅgraha composed by the illustrious teacher (ācārya) Vāgbhaṭa, son of the illustrious Siṃhagupta, lord of physicians (vaidyapati), the second Śārīrasthāna (section on the body) is complete.
02.12.68 | May it be an offering to Śrī Kṛṣṇa.
