05.04.1 | Now, therefore, we shall explain the Basti-kalpa (the preparations for basti, the medicated enema).
05.04.2 | Balā (country mallow), guḍūcī (giloy), triphalā (the three myrobalans) together with rāsnā (identity disputed), and the ten-root group (dvipañcamūla), measured by the pala (a unit of weight).
05.04.3 | Eight fruits, and half a tulā (a unit of weight) of goat meat, one should cook in water, with a quarter remaining.
05.04.4 | When strained, of yavānī-phala (ajwain fruit), bilva (bael), kuṣṭha (costus root), vacā (sweet flag), śatāhvā (probably dill), ghana (probably nut-grass) and pippalī (long pepper) —
05.04.5 | Joined with pastes, with mṛdvīkā (grape or raisin), honey (kṣaudra) and ghee, together with oil, pleasantly warm, and endowed with salt.
05.04.6 | The basti is supremely a destroyer of all diseases (gada), beneficial for a healthy person, and life-giving and nourishing (bṛṃhaṇa).
05.04.7 | And in whichever basti no paste has been introduced, in every case one should give this very one there.
05.04.8 | The liquid (rasa) of the ten-root group and of good goat meat, joined with something sour (amla), and — on a conjectural reading of the damaged word 'sapairbapeṣyaḥ' — together with what is to be ground.
05.04.9 | Joined with the three fats (trisneha), the foremost nirūha (decoction enema), declared to be a remover of all diseases of anila (wind, vāta).
05.04.10 | From what is well cooked of balā, paṭolī (pointed gourd), the small five-root group (laghupañcamūla), trāyantikā (identity disputed), eraṇḍa (castor) and yava (barley)
05.04.11 | A prastha (a measure) from the liquid (rasa), joined with goat's rasa (broth), is to be prepared again, until it equals a prastha.
05.04.12 | Joined with a paste of priyaṅgu (identity disputed), kṛṣṇā (long pepper) and ghana (probably nut-grass), together with oil, ghee, honey and saindhava (rock salt).
05.04.13 | It would be a kindler (dīpana), a giver of flesh and strength, and it at once bestows strength of the eyes.
05.04.14 | Three palas from the root of eraṇḍa (castor); likewise from palāśa (probably flame-of-the-forest) a portion of one pala; the small five-root group (laghupañcamūla).
05.04.15 | Rāsnā, balā, chinnaruhā (giloy), aśvagandhā (winter cherry), punarnavā (hogweed), āragvadha (Indian laburnum) and devadāru (deodar).
05.04.16 | And eight fruits: with two āḍhakas (a measure) of water one should cook thoroughly, with an eighth of this remaining.
05.04.17 | Vacā (sweet flag), śatāhvā (probably dill), puṣpāmi (unclear in print), priyaṅgu (identity disputed), yaṣṭī (liquorice), kaṇā (long pepper), the seeds of vatsaka (kuṭaja, kurchi) and musta (nut-grass).
05.04.18 | One should give it well ground, together with tārkṣyaśaila (possibly rasāñjana, an extract of Indian barberry; disputed), of the measure of an akṣa (a unit of weight), joined with a portion of salt.
05.04.19 | That basti, with mākṣika (probably honey), joined with oil, with urine, scraping (lekhana) and kindling (dīpana), would overcome —
05.04.20 | Pain in the shanks, thighs, feet, trika (lumbosacral region), back, koṣṭha (belly), heart and private part; heaviness; and obstruction (vibandha, constipation) —
05.04.21 | and, on a conjectural reading, those various diseases that arise from gulma (abdominal lump), aśma (stone), badhma (unclear in print), grahaṇī (chronic diarrhoea) and the anus (guda), and the diseases born of Kapha and Vāta.
05.04.22 | On a conjectural reading of the damaged print: from goat fried in four palas of oil and of ghee, sour with śatāhvā (probably dill), curd and dāḍima (pomegranate) —
05.04.23 | The liquid (rasa), together with what is to be ground; on a conjectural reading, beneficial to strength, complexion, flesh, semen (retas) and the digestive fire (agni); mirṣa (unclear in print); and desired in one with head-pain.
05.04.24 | Milk cooked with eight palas of the one named yaṣṭī (liquorice), with śatāhvā (probably dill) fruit and pippalīs (long pepper).
05.04.25 | Joined with ghee and honey, the nirūha (decoction enema) is beneficial in vātarakta (a gout-type disease), visarpa (erysipelas) and vaisvarya (hoarseness, change of voice).
05.04.26 | Milk boiled with yaṣṭyāhva (liquorice), rodhra (lodhra), āmaya (unclear here) and candana (probably sandalwood), and with kamala (lotus) and utpala (blue water lily): valuable (arghya; reading doubtful).
05.04.27 | With sugar, honey and ghee, well cooled, it destroys the ailments of Pitta, together with jīvanīya (a group name).
05.04.28 | On a conjectural reading, with mopāṅganā (unclear in print), candana (sandalwood), śītapākī (unclear), drākṣā (grape or raisin), honey (kṣaudra), kāśmarya (probably gambhārī, Gmelina arborea), madhuka (probably liquorice) and a last word that is unclear (saiṃnyaiḥ).
05.04.29 | Boiled milk, on a conjectural reading with those named śrāvaṇī (probably mundi), mudgaparṇī (wild mung) and balā, with pastes of guptā (identity disputed) and madhuyaṣṭi (liquorice) —
05.04.30 | Joined with wheat powders of the measure of a picu (a small measure), and with the juice of sugarcane and of vidārī (probably Indian kudzu).
05.04.31 | With oil prepared with yaṣṭīmadhu (liquorice), and with sitopalā (sugar candy), honey and ghee, cooled —
05.04.32 | That basti is recommended in burning of the whole body, with pain, or seated in a limb or part.
05.04.33 | In gulma (abdominal lump), atisāra (diarrhoea), bhrama (giddiness), mūtrakṛcchra (difficult urination) and in the wasted (kṣīṇa), in injury (kṣata), and in the wasting of ojas and strength.
05.04.34 | Kośātaka (probably ridge gourd), āragvadha (Indian laburnum), devadāru (deodar), mūrvā (probably Marsdenia tenacissima), aśvadaṃṣṭrā (probably puncture vine), kuṭaja (kurchi), arka (giant milkweed) and pāṭhā (probably velvet leaf).
05.04.35 | Having cooked kulattha (horse gram) and bṛhatī (probably Indian nightshade) in water, there should be ten prasṛtas (handfuls, a measure) of that liquid (rasa).
05.04.36 | Those, joined with mustard oil and madana (disputed: emetic nut or beeswax), with kuṣṭha (costus root), of the measure of an akṣa, and with prasṛtas (handfuls) —
05.04.37 | Of honey, of oil (taila), of phalāhvaya, of kṣāra, and of mustard (sārṣapa) oil.
05.04.38 | One should give the nirūha (decoction enema) to one afflicted with Kapha disease, to one with slow digestive fire (mandāgni), and to one with aversion to food.
05.04.39 | Punarnavā (spreading hogweed), eraṇḍa (castor), bṛhatī (probably Indian nightshade), aśmabheda (probably Bergenia), vṛścīva, bhūtīka, balā (country mallow) and palāśa (flame-of-the-forest).
05.04.40 | And the dvipañcamūla (the ten roots), (ones) with a pala as the share, crushed (ones), washed (ones), and eight fruits.
05.04.41 | Bilva (bael), yava (barley), aṅkola (probably Alangium), kulattha (horse gram) and dhānya (grain); and the fruits should be measured by prasṛtas (handful-measures).
05.04.42 | Milk prepared with two āḍhakas of water, that milk remaining, strained through a clean cloth.
05.04.43 | Of vacā (sweet flag), śatāhvā (probably dill), amaradāru (deodar cedar), kuṣṭha (costus), yaṣṭyāhva (liquorice), siddhārtha(ka) (probably white mustard) and pippalī (long pepper).
05.04.44 | Joined with pastes (kalka), with yavānī (ajwain) and with madana (emetic nut); neither too hot nor too cold; smeared with jaggery (guḍa) and rock salt (saindhava).
05.04.45 | Joined with a set of three prasṛtis (handful-measures) of honey, of oil and of fresh ghee.
05.04.46 | One who knows the rule should give that nirūha (decoction enema) by the rule, the destroyer of ailments produced by the combination of all.
05.04.47 | Therefore they declare the bastis arranged half and half to be varied (citra).
05.04.48 | On a conjectural reading: kośātakī (probably ridged gourd), dvyeśvāku (unclear in the print), fruit (phala), jīmūta (probably bristly luffa) and vatsaka (kuṭaja).
05.04.49 | The root of śyāmā and trivṛt (turpeth), and likewise (that) born of dantī (probably wild croton) and dravantī.
05.04.50 | Prakīryā and udakīryā, kṣīriṇī, nīlinī (probably indigo), fruit.
05.04.51 | Samalā, śaṅkhinī, rodhra (lodhra), and the fruit of kampillaka (kamala tree).
05.04.52 | (Ones) joined with paste (kalka) and rock salt (saindhava), cleansers of the pakvāśaya (the seat of digested matter, the colon).
05.04.53 | On a conjectural reading: dhātakī (woodfordia) flowers, takrīṃr (unclear in the print), the root of jīvantī (probably Leptadenia), and vatsaka (kuṭaja).
05.04.54 | Pragraha, khadirī, kuṣṭha (costus), śamī (probably the khejri tree), piṇḍītakā and yava (barley).
05.04.55 | Priyaṅgu (disputed) and red root(s) (raktamūla; reading uncertain), and bhairaṇī, jāti (probably jasmine) and yūthikā (probably Jasminum auriculatum, a jasmine).
05.04.56 | Vaṭa (banyan) and the rest, kiṃśuka (flame-of-the-forest), rodhra (lodhra): thus they are considered saṃgrāhika (binding).
05.04.57 | And in sequence, with decoctions and pastes of the binding (grāhi) priyaṅgu, ambaṣṭhā and the rest.
05.04.58 | The additive (pratīvāpa) of uṣaka (probably a saline earth) and the rest; the scraping (lekhana) triphalā juice.
05.04.59 | Nourishing (bṛṃhaṇa), with a paste of madhuka (probably liquorice) and with ghee together with rasa.
05.04.60 | The sprouts of badarī (jujube), airāvaṇī, śailū (probably the Indian cherry), śālmalī (silk-cotton tree) and dhanvana (probably Grewia).
05.04.61 | (Ones) with honey, joined with milk and with blood are held to be slimy (picchila).
05.04.62 | On a conjectural reading: jujube (kola) with kataka (clearing nut), kāṇḍekṣu, darbha (kusha grass), pāṭhā and elavālu.
05.04.63 | Destroying burning (dāha), with ghee and milk; and the second (one) with utpala (probably blue water lily) and the rest.
05.04.64 | A fistful of the stalks of śālmali (silk-cotton tree), cooked in milk, accompanied by ghee.
05.04.65 | Beneficial in pravāhaṇa (straining); likewise with the stalks of śālmalaka.
05.04.66 | With karbudāra, āḍhakī (pigeon pea), nīpa (probably kadamba) and vidula, (ones) prepared with milk.
05.04.67 | In parikarta (cutting pain), likewise with the stalks born of śrīparṇī (probably Gmelina) and kovidāra (probably the orchid tree).
05.04.68 | On a conjectural reading: in parisrava (flowing out), cooked milk; sadṛśavipunarnava (unclear in the print; it appears to contain the name punarnavā).
05.04.69 | With ākhukarṇikā (identity disputed), likewise, joined with taṇḍulīyaka (probably a spiny amaranth).
05.04.70 | Aśvāvarohaka, kākanāsā and rājakaserukā (probably a kind of kaserukā, the sedge Scirpus).
05.04.71 | In atiyoga (excess), they should be cooked in milk, joined with honey, añjana and ghee.
05.04.72 | And with the four beginning with nyagrodha (banyan); by that very method, another.
05.04.73 | Bṛhatī (Indian nightshade), kṣīrakākolī, pṛśniparṇī and śatāvarī (wild asparagus).
05.04.74 | Kāśmarya (gmelina), badarī (jujube), mūrvā (probably bowstring hemp), and likewise uśīra (vetiver) and priyaṅgu (identity disputed).
05.04.75 | In jīvādāna, in boiled milk, two (ones) joined with ghee and añjana (a collyrium substance).
05.04.76 | The two bastis should be given by the physician, cool, with honey and sugar.
05.04.77 | With the milks of cow too, goat and she-buffalo, also joined with jīvanīya (life-promoting) ones.
05.04.78 | With the blood of hare, eṇa (antelope), bear (on a conjectural reading), cat, she-buffalo and other-born (ones).
05.04.79 | With fresh, crushed (ones), basti is recommended in jīvādāna.
05.04.80 | Now, of the delicate, these mild nirūhas (decoction enemas) and, on a conjectural reading, snehanas (oleating ones),
05.04.81 | And for those afflicted by the action (karman), I shall state, by prasṛtas (palmful measures), separately.
05.04.82 | Two prasṛtas are to be made from milk; three from honey, oil and ghee (on a conjectural reading).
05.04.83 | The basti churned with a khaja (churning stick) is Vāta-destroying and makes strength and colour.
05.04.84 | One prasṛta each of oil, prasannā (probably clear liquor), honey and ghee.
05.04.85 | From the decoction of the roots beginning with bilva (bael), two (ones); from kaulattha (horse gram), two (ones); that one conquers Vāta.
05.04.86 | Of the water of paṭola (pointed gourd), nimba (neem), bhūtīka, rāsnā and saptacchada (devil's tree),
05.04.87 | A prasṛta, separately, and (one) from ghee; a basti having a paste (kalka) of mustard.
05.04.88 | With the five bitters (pañcatikta), destroying abhiṣyanda, worms (kṛmi), kuṣṭha (skin disease, traditionally "leprosy") and prameha (a group of urinary disorders, probably linked to diabetes).
05.04.89 | Viḍaṅga (false black pepper), triphalā (the three fruits: harītakī, bibhītaka and āmalakī), the fruit of śigru (drumstick tree), mustā (nutgrass) and ākhukarṇī (probably mūṣākarṇī).
05.04.90 | Five prasṛtas of decoctions (kaṣāya), and one from oil; having churned them,
05.04.91 | A nirūha (decoction enema), destroying worms, joined with a paste (kalka) of vella and pippalī (long pepper).
05.04.92 | Of payasyā, ikṣu (sugarcane), sthirā, rāsnā, vidārī (Indian kudzu), honey and ghee,
05.04.93 | One prasṛta each; the basti, on a conjectural reading from a paste (kalka) of kṛṣṇā (long pepper), makes virility (vṛṣatva).
05.04.94 | Four, from oil, cow's urine, dadhimaṇḍa (the watery part of curd) and sour kāñjika (a fermented gruel).
05.04.95 | A slightly warm basti is considered best in (one) with ānāha (belly distension with obstruction) and in mūtrakṛcchra (painful urination).
05.04.96 | In one who has become dull (sluggish) from mild bastis, another, sharp basti is desired.
05.04.97 | In one drawn out (depleted) by sharp (ones): oily, sweet, cool, mild.
05.04.98 | Sharpness, by urine, bilva (bael), agni, salt, kṣāra and mustard.
05.04.99 | But mildness is to be produced, in due time, by milk, ghee and so on.
05.04.100 | The basti, applied after distinguishing strength, time, region, sātmya (what suits by habit) and constitutions,
05.04.101 | with their own respective groups of drugs, causes their own respective diseases to turn back (cease).
05.04.102 | For those afflicted by heat, cool (ones); for those afflicted by cold, likewise pleasantly warm (ones).
05.04.103 | One should apply bastis joined with drugs suitable to that, having considered (on a conjectural reading).
05.04.104 | And one should give nourishing (bṛṃhaṇīya) bastis in diseases that are fit to be cleansed (viśodhanīya).
05.04.105 | The fat (ones), (those) to be cleansed, and the men who are afflicted by kuṣṭha (skin disease) and meha (urinary disorder).
05.04.106 | Not of those with wasted, injured, weak, fainted, thin, dried-up and purified bodies.
05.04.107 | One should give cleansing (ones), and those who (have) life bound up in the doṣas.
05.04.108 | Thus the fourth chapter, named Bastikalpa (the basti preparations).
