05.07.1 | Now, therefore, we shall explain the accomplishment (siddhi) concerning the mishaps (vyāpad) of sneha (the unctuous substance).
05.07.2 | In the unctuous enema (sneha-basti), by wind (marut, i.e. Vāta), Pitta, Kapha, over-eating and accumulation of stool,
05.07.3 | In the covering (āvṛti), on a conjectural reading in 'bhukta-datta-āma' (eaten, given, raw), the mishaps (vyāpad) are remembered as sevenfold.
05.07.4 | Cold or little when Vāta is in excess; very hot in Pitta; mild in Kapha.
05.07.5 | When over-fed, heavy; and likewise of little strength when stool has accumulated.
05.07.6 | The sneha that has been given, covered by them, does not come (out), also on account of being overpowered.
05.07.7 | By stiffness, weakness of the thighs, distension of the belly, fever, pain (śūla) and aching of the limbs,
05.07.8 | By pain in the flanks and by twisting (veṣṭana, cramp-like), one should know the sneha to be covered by Vāyu (wind).
05.07.9 | That (covered sneha): with (ones) unctuous, sour, salty and hot, with the oils of rāsnā (probably Pluchea lanceolata) and pītadru (identity disputed),
05.07.10 | prepared with sauvīraka (a sour fermented gruel), surā (a fermented intoxicating drink), kola (probably jujube), kulattha (horse gram) and yava (barley),
05.07.11 | one should expel it well with nirūhas (decoction enemas), with urine and with those of the five roots (pāñcamūlika).
05.07.12 | And with those very two oils, in the evening, in one who has eaten, one should give an anuvāsana (oil enema).
05.07.13 | By thirst, burning, redness, bewilderment, discolouration, tamaka (probably the asthma-type breathing disorder) and fever,
05.07.14 | One should know it to be covered by Pitta; one should remove it with enemas that are sweet and bitter.
05.07.15 | By drowsiness, cold, fever, lassitude, praseka (flow, trickling), aversion (aruci) and heaviness,
05.07.16 | By fainting together with exhaustion, one should know the sneha to be covered by Kapha.
05.07.17 | (ones) astringent, bitter and pungent, prepared with surā (a fermented intoxicating drink) and urine,
05.07.18 | one should expel it completely with enemas joined with fruit (phala) and oil, and with sour (things).
05.07.19 | By vomiting (chardi), fainting, aversion (aruci), exhaustion, pain (śūla), sleep and aching of the limbs,
05.07.20 | By signs of āma (the raw, undigested state) together with burning, one should know it to be covered by over-eating.
05.07.21 | A digestive (pācana) with decoctions and powders of pungent (ones) and of salts.
05.07.22 | A mild purgation; and all that is prescribed for āma is beneficial there.
05.07.23 | On a conjectural reading, by blockage of stool, urine and wind, and pain, heaviness, distension and seizing (grasp) of the heart (hṛdgraha),
05.07.24 | Having known the sneha to be covered by stool, with sneha and sweating (sveda), together with enemas,
05.07.25 | and with nirūhas (decoction enemas) cooked with śyāmā (identity disputed), bilva (bael) and the rest, together with anuvāsanas (oil enemas),
05.07.26 | one should expel it properly according to the rule, and with (one) that removes udāvarta.
05.07.27 | In one who has not eaten, or in one with a swollen anus, or of one who has eaten only peyā (thin gruel),
05.07.28 | The sneha placed in the anus rushes with force, not covered.
05.07.29 | On a conjectural reading: upward from the body, then from the throat it goes also to the upper orifices.
05.07.30 | cooked with urine, śyāmā (identity disputed) and trivṛt (turpeth), having yava (barley), kola (probably jujube) and kulattha (horse gram),
05.07.31 | A nirūha (decoction enema), with oil cooked in that, together with an anuvāsana (oil enema), should be given.
05.07.32 | Of it, as it comes from the throat, by stiffening (stambha), throat-seizing (kaṇṭhagraha) and purgations (virecana),
05.07.33 | and by actions that destroy vomiting (chardi), one should bring about its turning back.
05.07.34 | One should not administer unripe (uncooked) sneha, for it smears the anus.
05.07.35 | Then, on a conjectural reading, it would cause retention with pain, itching and swelling; and the treatment here
05.07.36 | A sharp enema (basti), and oil boiled in the juice of arka (Calotropis, madar) leaves.
05.07.37 | Short, long, thin, thick, old, with loose binding.
05.07.38 | With a hole in the side (pārśvacchidra), and likewise crooked: one should avoid the eight nozzles (netra, the tube).
05.07.39 | Non-arrival (aprāpti), over-going (atigati), agitation (kṣobha), dragging (karṣaṇa), wounding (kṣaṇana) and leaking (srava).
05.07.40 | Pain in the anus and a crooked course: their faults, in due order.
05.07.41 | Fleshy (māṃsala), unctuous (snigdha), uneven (viṣama), net-like (jālavat), thick (sthūla) and windy (vātala).
05.07.42 | Cut (chinna) and moist (klinna): one should avoid those eight bastis (the enema bags) in the procedures (karma).
05.07.43 | Unevenness of going (gati-vaiṣamya), bad smell (daurgandhya), crookedness (jihmatva), leaking (sruti) and difficulty in holding (durgraha).
05.07.44 | Foaminess and, on a conjectural reading, difficulty in holding it firm: [these] would be of the basti (basteḥ), from the basti's faults (basti-doṣataḥ).
05.07.45 | The first word is unclear; on a conjectural reading: high, head-down; staying drawn up above.
05.07.46 | Supine (uttāna), the right side, and sama (even) beds: one should avoid them.
05.07.47 | On a conjectural reading of the printed mūtraughāta, obstruction of urine (mūtra-āghāta, retention of urine), reaching the intestine (antra-saṃprāpti), non-arrival (aprāpti) and, on a conjectural reading, improper going (gati).
05.07.48 | On a conjectural reading: quick coming (āśu-āgati), aggravation of wind (marut-kopa), and of the pakvāśaya (large-intestine region) as well; the word straibhiḥ is unclear and is not rendered.
05.07.49 | There would be their faults (taddoṣa); and here, arrangement is to be made as suitable.
05.07.50 | On a conjectural reading: with wind (saṃvāta), struck too much (atihata), lifted up (utkṣipta), slanting (tiryak), hurried (druta), shaken (kampita).
05.07.51 | Too slow (atimandaka), outer (bāhya), too forceful (ativega): these are faults, on a conjectural reading of those who administer.
05.07.52 | In the continuation of non-exhalation (anucchvāsa), or when [it] is given, or [when it] is completely without remainder (niḥśeṣa).
05.07.53 | The agitated wind (vāyu), having entered, would become a producer of pain (śūla) and pricking pain (toda).
05.07.54 | Here: oil massage (abhyaṅga) on the anus, sweating (sveda, on a conjectural reading of the printed snedo), and Vāta-destroying foods.
05.07.55 | When [it] is introduced quickly, or drawn out, or else lifted up suddenly.
05.07.56 | There would be stiffness (stambha) and severe splitting pain (bhedana) of the waist, anus, calves, thighs and bladder (basti).
05.07.57 | Here: Vāta-destroying food, sweating (sveda) and oil massages (abhyaṅga), together with bastis (enemas).
05.07.58 | When the opening is covered by a slanting fold (valī), or even bound (baddha), it does not go.
05.07.59 | In the nozzle: having drawn that out straight and having cleansed [it], one should lead [it] again.
05.07.60 | When [it] is being pressed and is let go in between, in the anus the wind (anila), obstructed (pratihata) ...
05.07.61 | The strong one (balī, on a conjectural reading of the printed valī) would produce pain in the chest and head, and sinking (sāda, exhaustion) of the thighs.
05.07.62 | There the basti (enema) should be one with bilva (wood-apple) and the rest, fruit, śyāmā and the rest, and urine.
05.07.63 | There would be burning (dāha), burning distress (davathu), swelling (śopha) and trembling (kampana), when the anus is injured.
05.07.64 | Here: astringent and sweet, cool affusions (seka), together with bastis (enemas).
05.07.65 | By a nozzle introduced beyond measure (atimātra), from the wounding of the fold (bali),
05.07.66 | On a conjectural reading there would be pain, burning (dāha), pricking pain (nistoda), and the passing of heavy stool.
05.07.67 | There ghee (sarpis), picchu (unclear; on a conjectural reading a slimy substance), milk, and the picchā-basti (enema) are recommended.
05.07.68 | The slow (one) does not impregnate (bhāvayati), and the outer (one) returns quickly.
05.07.69 | There an unctuous substance (sneha) should be administered again properly, by one who wishes for success (siddhi).
05.07.70 | Pressed too much, [it] stays in the koṣṭha (belly) or comes to the throat.
05.07.71 | There: basti (enema), purgation (vireka), and the procedure (karma) beginning with pressing of the throat (galapīḍā).
05.07.72 | Purified by emesis (vamana) and the rest, and with emaciated body, strength and fire (anala).
05.07.73 | As a tender egg, as a vessel full of oil, so ...
05.07.74 | The physician (bhiṣak) should protect [him] with effort from all wrong conduct (apacāra).
05.07.75 | One should give sweet and agreeable (ones); then the two tastes (rasa), sour (amla) and salty (lavaṇa).
05.07.76 | Likewise again the sweet (svādu) and bitter (tikta) ones, then the astringent (kaṣāya) and pungent (kaṭuka) ones.
05.07.77 | Of the mutually opposed tastes, and of the unctuous and the dry,
05.07.78 | By using them in alternation, in due order, one should lead him to his natural state (prakṛti).
05.07.79 | One who has reached his natural state (prakṛti) is to be known as all-enduring and of steady strength.
05.07.80 | In this interval one should give up, especially, the eight (ones) beginning with speaking (bhāṣya).
05.07.81 | From speaking loudly: head disease, timira (dimness of vision), and pains of the chest and voice
05.07.82 | On a conjectural reading: spitting of blood, 'maka' (unclear), fever and so on; in that case the remedy (sādhana)
05.07.83 | On a conjectural reading: anointing (abhyaṅga, oil massage), sweating (svedana), [nasya, nasal medication] and the use of unctuous substances (sneha) taken after food (adhobhakta)
05.07.84 | Silence (mauna); the procedure that removes Vāta; or, as is proper to each, one that conquers the disorder.
05.07.85 | On a conjectural reading: from excessive sitting and travelling by vehicle, pain of the joints, head, trika (probably the lower back at the sacrum) and so on
05.07.86 | From excessive walking about: sinking (sadana, exhaustion) of the feet, shanks, thighs and so on
05.07.87 | For these, everything that removes Vāta, beginning with oleation (sneha) and sweating (sveda), is recommended.
05.07.88 | From eating while undigested (ajīrṇa-bhojana): āma-viṣa, vomiting (chardi), fever (jvara) and so on
05.07.89 | In that case the procedure that destroys āma, as stated in the Mātrāśitīya, is to be applied.
05.07.90 | From unwholesome food there may be diseases according to the doṣa, and remedies as well.
05.07.91 | From day-sleep, halīmaka (probably a severe stage of the pāṇḍu condition) and the other diseases (gada) have been declared earlier.
05.07.92 | In those overcome by Kapha one should prescribe smoke (dhūma), dry food and fasting (laṅghana, lightening).
05.07.93 | From sexual intercourse (vyavāya), from the falling away (bhraṃśa) of life (jīvita), by those various Vāta-ailments (anilāmaya) of his
05.07.94 | The anus is, as it were, being torn off, and consciousness, as it were, whirls.
05.07.95 | The penis smokes (emits smoke); the mind will enter, as it were, into darkness.
05.07.96 | The use of milk and ghee filled with jīvanī (probably the jīvanīya group of drugs)
05.07.97 | In that case, food that is bulking (bṛṃhaṇa), and those bastis (enemas) that are virilising (vṛṣya).
05.07.98 | Those who, as stated earlier, are always ailing because of holding back of urges and upward Vāta
05.07.99 | In them, when the wind (pavana) is obstructed and afflicting the whole body,
05.07.100 | One should first give a phalavartti (suppository), then a basti (enema) that brings strength.
05.07.101 | Nikumbha (probably wild croton), kumbha (unidentified), agni (probably leadwort), urubūka (castor), dhāvanī (disputed, perhaps pṛśniparṇī), punarnava (spreading hogweed), dāru (probably deodar) and the great group of five (mahat pañcaka)
05.07.102 | And the fruit, all boiled together in urine; ghee with oil; five salts.
05.07.103 | Him who has been given nirūha (decoction enema) one should feed with dhanva-rasa (probably the meat broth of dry-land animals), then give anuvāsana (oil enema) with nikumbha oil.
05.07.104 | Balā (country mallow) with rāsnā (identity disputed); the fruit and root from citraka (leadwort); the ten roots (dvipañcamūla); the fruit from kṛtamālaka (Indian laburnum)
05.07.105 | Barley (yava) and horse gram (kulattha): one should boil in water; the rasa (extract) has a paste (kalka) with musta (nut-grass), pāṭhā (probably velvet-leaf) and indrayava (kurchi seed).
05.07.106 | With oil and ghee, jaggery and rock salt (saindhava), it is beneficial to those who are always ailing, giving strength, nourishment and complexion.
05.07.107 | Likewise, what is to be given as anuvāsana (oil enema), prepared with madhuka (probably liquorice), with young bilva (bael), or with śatāhvayā (probably dill).
05.07.108 | But for a child the rasa with jīvanīya (drugs) is beneficial in anuvāsana (oil enema), and in nirūhaṇa (decoction enema) the one without salt.
05.07.109 | And there is no other quick increaser of the strength of the limbs than nirūha basti (decoction enema); beneficial to those who are neither child nor aged.
05.07.110 | Thus the seventh chapter, named Sneha-vyāpat-siddhi (the cure of the complications of oleation).
