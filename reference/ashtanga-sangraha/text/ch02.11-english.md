02.11.1 | Now, therefore, we shall explain the chapter on the knowledge of abnormal (vikṛta) diseases.
02.11.2 | He whose disease arises with all its causes, premonitory signs (prāgrūpa) and signs (rūpa), each in its own way, to excess; or a sick man in whom quickly there are, on the face, moles (piplu, probably), blemishes (vyaṅga, probably), lines (lekhā) and tilakālaka (black specks of the skin, probably), or, on the nails and teeth, flowers (puṣpa), or, on the belly, veins (sirā) of various colours; he who, while wasting away, has a coating on the eyes (netra-upadeha), a swelling of either the eyelid or the nose, or a drooping of the eyelid; he who, being wasted, has hunger or thirst that is not allayed even by many agreeable foods and drinks; he from whom frothy blood is discharged, with pain in the flank and (pain) of the chest; or he whose phlegm (śleṣman) is blue, yellow and red, and repeatedly so; he who has a strong crushing of the neck, dryness of the tongue, and inflammation (pāka, literally 'ripening') of the throat, mouth and anus; he who has parikartikā (cutting pain) arising in the āmāśaya (probably the stomach), excessive thirst and breaking of the stool; he who has parikartikā arising in the pakvāśaya (probably the large intestine), excessive thirst and an excessive grasp of the anus (gudagraha); he in whom wind (vāyu), having resorted to either the āmāśaya or the pakvāśaya and having destroyed consciousness (saṃjñā), makes a gurgling sound (ghurghuraka) in the throat; he who, wasted, has increase of thirst and pain in the heart, and likewise hiccup and bloody diarrhoea, or bloating and diarrhoea, and who is greatly thirsty: he is parāsu (one whose life-breaths are gone).
02.11.3 | And on this matter there is:
02.11.4 | A fever (jvara) that is strong, deep (gambhīra) and long-lasting (dīrgharātrika, literally 'of long nights') kills.
02.11.5 | Accompanied by delirium (pralāpa), giddiness (bhrama) and breathlessness (śvāsa); the one who is wasted, swollen, whose fire (anala, probably the digestive fire) is destroyed.
02.11.6 | The one who is not emaciated (akṣāma), whose speech is faltering (sakta-vacana, literally 'speech stuck'), who is red-eyed and has pain in the heart (hṛd).
02.11.7 | A very dry cough (saṃśuṣka-kāsa) that may occur in the forenoon or even in the afternoon.
02.11.8 | Of one deprived of strength and flesh, accompanied by phlegm-cough (śleṣma-kāsa).
02.11.9 | Raktapitta (a bleeding disorder, probably), very red, black, with the lustre of the rainbow (Indra's bow).
02.11.10 | It should show a copper-coloured, turmeric-coloured, green and red (rakta) appearance (rūpa).
02.11.11 | Issued through the hair-pits (roma-kūpa); an unclear word (printed sajataṃ) in the throat, mouth and heart (hṛdaya), on a conjectural reading.
02.11.12 | Staining of clothing; putrid (pūti), forceful and also very abundant.
02.11.13 | An unclear word (printed charddaṃ); the one who has pāṇḍu (anaemia with pallor), fever (jvara), vomiting (chardi), cough (kāsa), swelling (śopha) and diarrhoea (atisāra), on a conjectural reading.
02.11.14 | Cough and breathlessness (kāsa and śvāsa); the one with fever (jvara), vomiting (chardi), thirst (tṛṣṇā), diarrhoea (atīsāra) and swelling.
02.11.15 | Yakṣman (consumption, probably tuberculosis); the one with pain in the side, distension (ānāha), vomiting of blood, and without burning heat (asaṃtāpin).
02.11.16 | Vomiting (chardi), forceful, smelling of urine and faeces, with candrikā (literally 'moonlight'; its sense here is uncertain).
02.11.17 | With asra (blood, or tears); an unclear compound (printed viṭpūyarukkāsaśvāsavatya); an unclear word (printed guṃṣiṇo); on a conjectural reading.
02.11.18 | Thirst (tṛṣṇā); the one worn down by other ailments, with the tongue outside, unconscious.
02.11.19 | Madātyaya (an ailment from intoxicating drink, probably an alcohol-related disorder); the one afflicted by great cold, wasted, whose face has the lustre of oil.
02.11.20 | Arśas (haemorrhoids, piles); the one with swelling of the hands, feet, navel, anus, testicles (muṣka) and mouth.
02.11.21 | The one afflicted by pain in the heart, side and limbs, vomiting, inflammation (pāka, literally 'ripening') of the anus, and fever.
02.11.22 | Diarrhoea (atīsāra), with liver-lumps (yakṛt-piṇḍa), meat-washings (māṃsa-dhāvana) and dark (mecaka) ones.
02.11.23 | Similar to oil, ghee, milk, curd, marrow, fat (vasā) and āsava (a fermented liquor).
02.11.24 | With mastuluṅga (brain-matter, probably), soot (maṣī), pus (pūya), veśavāra (a preparation of minced meat), water and honey, on a conjectural reading.
02.11.25 | Very (ati) red, black, unctuous (snigdha), putrid (pūti), clear (accha), thick (ghana), with pain (vedana).
02.11.26 | Mottled (karbura); an unclear word (printed prasravandhāttriṣpūrīṣo); or else with excessive faeces (ati-viṭ); on a conjectural reading.
02.11.27 | Fibrous (tantumat), overspread (ākrānta) with soot-specks (maṣikā), joined with unclear words (printed rājair māṃseṃdrakair); on a conjectural reading.
02.11.28 | The one whose folds (vali) of the anus are withered, whose stalk (nāla) is released (its sense here is uncertain), who has pain in the joints and bones; on a conjectural reading.
02.11.29 | The one with a slipped anus (srasta-pāyu, probably prolapse of the anus or rectum), wasted in strength; an unclear word (printed annevopaveśayan); on a conjectural reading.
02.11.30 | With thirst, cough, fever, vomiting, burning (dāha), distension (ānāha) and pravāhikā (flux with straining, probably dysentery); on a conjectural reading.
02.11.31 | Aśmarī (urinary stone); the one with swollen testicles, with obstructed urine, afflicted by pain.
02.11.32 | Meha (prameha, a urinary disorder, probably the diabetes-type); the one with pricking pain (tud), burning, boils (piṭakā), decay of flesh and diarrhoea.
02.11.33 | Boils (piṭikā) situated in the vital spots (marma), heart, back, breast, shoulder, anus and head; on a conjectural reading.
02.11.34 | (Boils) situated in the joints, feet or hands; the one of low energy (manda-utsāha) who has prameha (a urinary disorder).
02.11.35 | And all, with decay of flesh (māṃsa-saṃkotha), burning (dāha), thirst (tṛṣṇā), intoxication (mada) and fever (jvara).
02.11.36 | With visarpa (erysipelas, a spreading skin inflammation, probably), obstruction of the vital spots (marma-saṃrodha), hiccup (hidhmā), breathlessness (śvāsa), giddiness (bhrama) and fatigue (klama).
02.11.37 | Gulma (an abdominal lump, probably), broad in girth, dense (ghana), raised like a tortoise.
02.11.38 | Bound by sirās (veins), accompanied by fever, vomiting, hiccup, bloating (ādhmāna) and pain.
02.11.39 | Having cough, pīnasa (chronic nasal catarrh, probably), nausea (hṛllāsa), breathlessness, diarrhoea (atīsāra) and swelling.
02.11.40 | With retention (saṃgraha) of faeces and urine, breathlessness, swelling, hiccup, fever and giddiness.
02.11.41 | And with fainting (mūrcchā), vomiting and diarrhoea, jaṭhara (belly; here probably the belly disorder, an ascites-type) kills the weak (one).
02.11.42 | (One) with swollen eyes, crooked genitals (upastha) and moist, thin skin.
02.11.43 | On a conjectural reading: (one) harmed by purgation (virecana) and distended (ānāha: bloating with retention) again and again.
02.11.44 | Pāṇḍu disease (pāṇḍuroga: anaemia with pallor) with swelling (śvayathu); (one) in whom yellow eyes and nails are seen.
02.11.45 | Having drowsiness (tandrā), burning (dāha), distaste for food (aruci), vomiting (chardi), fainting (mūrchā), flatulent distension (ādhmāna) and diarrhoea (atisāra).
02.11.46 | Joined with many complications (upadrava); a man spread out from the two feet.
02.11.47 | Swelling (śopha) kills a woman from the face; from the belly and the private parts (guhya) it kills both as well.
02.11.48 | Covered with streaks (rājī), oozing; (one) with vomiting (chardi), fever (jvara), breathlessness (śvāsa) and diarrhoea (atisāra).
02.11.49 | Fever and diarrhoea at the end of the swelling, or swelling when those two decline.
02.11.50 | (They) arise, especially, for the end of a weak embodied one (dehin).
02.11.51 | He whose swelling is situated in the foot, and whose two calves are slack (on a conjectural reading),
02.11.52 | and whose two thighs indeed sink (give way) — that one the physician should avoid.
02.11.53 | Of whom, while wasting away (drying up), the face and the hands and feet especially,
02.11.54 | swell, or (swell) apart from the body: he reaches pañcatā (death, literally the state of five) within a month.
02.11.55 | Whoever has udara (belly disease, probably ascites-type; probable), or swelling (śopha), does not see his own penis (mehana).
02.11.56 | Or he whose garment (paridhāna) does not stay in place because of the raised belly (jaṭhara), on a conjectural reading.
02.11.57 | Visarpa (spreading skin inflammation, erysipelas-type; probable), having cough (kāsa), discolouration (vaivarṇya), fever (jvara), fainting (mūrchā) and breaking pain in the limbs (aṅgabhaṅga).
02.11.58 | Having giddiness (bhrama), swelling of the mouth (āsya), nausea (hṛllāsa), bodily exhaustion (sāda) and diarrhoea (atisāra).
02.11.59 | Kuṣṭha (leprosy; the term covers a group of skin diseases); (one) whose limbs are falling apart, with red eyes and a lost voice.
02.11.60 | (Kuṣṭha) kills one with weak digestive fire (agni), infested by creatures (jantu: worms or vermin), with thirst and diarrhoea.
02.11.61 | Wind (vāyu); (one) with numb skin, bent, afflicted with tremor, swelling and pain.
02.11.62 | On a conjectural reading, vātāsra (probably vātarakta, a gout-type disease); accompanied by delusion (moha), an unclear stretch of print (sūrcchāyamadāsvama) and fever (jvara).
02.11.63 | Having an unclear stretch of print (śiropahāruciśvārasa), contraction (saṃkoca), blisters (sphoṭa) and putrefaction (kotha).
02.11.64 | By head disease (śiro-roga), distaste for food (aruci), breathlessness (śvāsa), meha (urinary disorder, probably of the prameha group), loose stool (vidbheda), thirst and giddiness (bhrama).
02.11.65 | All diseases kill (one) whose voice, dhātus (body-tissues), strength and fire (anala, probably the digestive fire) are wasted.
02.11.66 | One with a Vāta disorder (vātavyādhi), one with apasmāra (epilepsy), one with kuṣṭha (leprosy), one with rakta (blood; sense uncertain, on a conjectural reading), one with udara (belly disease, probably ascites-type), one with kṣaya (wasting disease, probably tuberculosis).
02.11.67 | One with gulma (abdominal lump), and one with meha (urinary disorder, probably of the prameha group) — those, when wasted, one should avoid even in a slight disorder.
02.11.68 | Severe loss of strength and flesh, increase of disease, and loss of appetite (arocaka).
02.11.69 | The patient in whom (these) are observed lives for three fortnights.
02.11.70 | A vātāṣṭhīlā (a hard, round, stony lump due to Vāta), greatly enlarged, remaining dreadful in the heart (hṛd),
02.11.71 | Of one beset by thirst, (it) at once steals life.
02.11.72 | Wind (vāyu), having brought the two calves to slackness and the nose to crookedness (on a conjectural reading),
02.11.73 | Or, of a wasted one, having drawn tight the two manyā (side of the neck; probable), (the wind) at once steals life.
02.11.74 | Having gone to the space between the navel and the anus, or taking refuge in the two groins,
02.11.75 | Having seized the anus (pāyu) and the heart (hṛdaya), or (in the case) of one whose body is wasted, the strong one,
02.11.76 | Having obstructed the waste products (mala) at the head of the bladder (basti, probable) and the navel, producing pain,
02.11.77 | causing pain in the two groins, thirst, and the state of having broken (probably loose) stool (bhinnapurīṣatā),
02.11.78 | or producing breathlessness (śvāsa), the wind (vāyu), having seized the anus and the groins,
02.11.79 | having spread out, having seized the tips of the ribs (parśukā) and the chest, the wind (māruta),
02.11.80 | Of one who is still (stimita), with raised eyes (on a conjectural reading), (the wind) at once steals life.
02.11.81 | Suddenly burning heat of fever (jvara), thirst, fainting and loss of strength,
02.11.82 | and loosening of the joints arises in one about to die.
02.11.83 | He from whose face, at daybreak (gosarga), sweat drips profusely
02.11.84 | On a conjectural reading, of one afflicted with lepa-jvara (a fever of the 'coating' kind, whose exact identity is uncertain), his life is hard to obtain
02.11.85 | He on whose body there are masūrikā (lentil-like eruptions, probably a pox-type disease) that look like pellets of coral
02.11.86 | Having arisen, (they) quickly vanish; before long (lit. not after a long time) he perishes
02.11.87 | Resembling a split lentil, and likewise resembling coral
02.11.88 | Blisters (visphoṭa) with the mouth turned inward, looking like kiṇa (probably a scar or callus), destroying the body
02.11.89 | Jaundice (kāmalā) in the two eyes, the face full, and in the two temples (śaṅkha) a state of shed flesh (loss of flesh)
02.11.90 | He in whom there is fright (saṃtrāsa) and heat in the body, him one should avoid
02.11.91 | Suddenly, running after (or along), and rubbed, resting in the skin
02.11.92 | Having the smell of sandalwood (candana), uśīra (vetiver root), madirā (an intoxicating drink), a corpse (kuṇapa) and a crow (dhvāṅkṣa)
02.11.93 | Having the lustre of śaivāla (water-moss; the exact plant is disputed), a cock's comb, kuṅkuma (saffron), āla (probably orpiment) and ink
02.11.94 | Wounds (vraṇa) with inner burning, without heat, causing the destruction of life
02.11.95 | The Vāta-born (one) which would not be for pain, and the Pitta-born (one) which would not be for burning
02.11.96 | The Kapha-born (one) which would not be for pus, and the marma-born (one; marma = vital spot) which would not be for pain
02.11.97 | Where one without powder, looking as if strewn with powder, and suddenly, is seen
02.11.98 | The form of a spear (śakti), a banner (dhvaja) and so on; one should avoid all those wounds
02.11.99 | Bhagandara (fistula in ano) that carries faeces, urine and wind, and has worms
02.11.100 | Striking knee with knee, and, having raised the two feet, dropping (them)
02.11.101 | On a conjectural reading (vaktram), the patient who repeatedly turns his face aside does not live
02.11.102 | Cutting the tips of the nails with the teeth, and with those (teeth) hairs or blades of grass
02.11.103 | Scratching the ground with a piece of wood, striking a clod with a clod
02.11.104 | On a conjectural reading (hṛṣṭaloma), he who has bristling hair, thick urine, dry cough and fever
02.11.105 | He who, repeatedly laughing, repeatedly humming (kṣveḍ), strikes the bed with his foot
02.11.106 | The patient who repeatedly touches the holes (chidra) does not live
02.11.107 | Him whose breath is upward (probably laboured breathing), whose heat has gone, whose groins (vaṅkṣaṇa) are afflicted by pain
02.11.108 | And him who does not obtain ease, the wise one should avoid
02.11.109 | He in whom the abnormalities (vikāra) increase and the natural state (prakṛti) diminishes
02.11.110 | Suddenly, suddenly, Death takes away his life
02.11.111 | Having in view whom as patient, a physician, to prepare medicine (auṣadha)
02.11.112 | Striving, he is not able; his life is hard to obtain
02.11.113 | Known, successful many times, and applied according to rule
02.11.114 | For whom medicine does not succeed, for him there is no treatment (cikitsita)
02.11.115 | He for whom, when medicine or food is being prepared, there would be a reversal (viparyaya)
02.11.116 | On a conjectural reading (varṇādeḥ): suddenly, from colour and the like, even one who is healthy does not live
02.11.117 | He whose light (jyotis), even with fuel, goes out in a windless place
02.11.118 | Of the patient in whose house (ones) break or fall
02.11.119 | Excessively, the vessels (amatra); his life is hard to obtain
02.11.120 | The weak man whom a disease suddenly leaves
02.11.121 | Ātreya considers his life to have reached a state of doubt
02.11.122 | Thus the eleventh chapter, named Vikṛtavyādhivijñānīya.
