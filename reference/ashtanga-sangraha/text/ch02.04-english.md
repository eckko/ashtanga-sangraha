02.04.1 | Now, therefore, we shall explain garbhavyāpad (mishaps of the garbha, i.e. of the embryo or pregnancy), the śārīra (that which relates to the body).
02.04.2 | If a woman who is pregnant (āpannagarbhā, lit. 'one whose garbha has come about') should indulge in avoidable (ones),
02.04.3 | In that case, in her for whom there should be pain in the basti (bladder region), the flanks, the hips and the mouth of the yoni (vaginal opening), or the appearance of puṣpa (the 'flower': an idiom for bleeding, probably menstrual-type bleeding),
02.04.4 | Her, staying on a bed with a soft-surfaced, cool spread and with the head slightly lowered, one should treat with cool pradeha (plasters), pariṣeka (sprinklings) and the like.
02.04.5 | That is as follows.
02.04.6 | With ghee (sarpis) washed a thousand times, one should smear (the area) all round below the navel.
02.04.7 | Having soaked a picu (a pad or wad of cotton) in madhuka-ghee (madhuka: licorice) that is very cool, one should place (it) near the yoni.
02.04.8 | One should sprinkle (her) with cow's milk, with madhuka-water, or with a decoction of nyagrodha (banyan) and the others.
02.04.9 | And one should have oils (tailāni) placed in the yoni; the compound describing them, paripītatatvarasāni, is unclear, and on a conjectural reading they are oils that have thoroughly absorbed (paripīta) the juices (rasa) of an unclear substance.
02.04.10 | Or a picu (wad) of the milk-and-ghee cooked with its śṛṅga.
02.04.11 | But when the blood flows too much, one should make (her) take, from that very (one), a measure of an akṣa.
02.04.12 | Or from the milk-and-ghee alone.
02.04.13 | And one should make (her) lick the filaments (kiñjalka) of padma (lotus), utpala (probably a water lily) and kumuda (probably the white water lily), together with honey and sugar (śarkarā).
02.04.14 | Or a powder of dhātakī (woodfordia flowers), gairika (red ochre), sarjarasa (probably sal resin) and rasāñjana (probably barberry extract), with honey.
02.04.15 | Or a paste (kalka) of the bark and the tender shoots (pravāla) of nyagrodha and the others.
02.04.16 | Or one should make (her) drink the blood of the hare, (probably) of the eṇa antelope and of the deer, with honey.
02.04.17 | Or vṛddhi (identity disputed), vidārī (probably Indian kudzu) and jīvantī (Leptadenia reticulata, a milky climber; standard), with milk.
02.04.18 | Nīlotpala (blue water lily), sitā (crystal sugar), pakloṣṭa [unclear], sandalwood (candana) and the like; or taṇḍulāmbu (rice-water); or sarasānyasareṇa [unclear].
02.04.19 | Gandha, priyaṅgu (probably Callicarpa macrophylla), dark utpala, śālūka (probably the edible rhizome of a water lily), the unripe fruits of udumbara (cluster fig) and the shoots of nyagrodha; or with goat's milk.
02.04.20 | Or with a decoction of kaseruka (probably Scirpus grossus), lotus stalk (mṛṇāla), śṛṅgāṭaka (water chestnut), kāśmarya (Gmelina arborea), parūṣaka (phalsa, Grewia asiatica), drākṣā (grape or raisin), sārivā (Hemidesmus indicus), [unclear syllable ma], puṇḍarīka (on a conjectural reading; probably the white lotus), ananta (identity disputed), utpala, tuber(s) (kanda) and seed(s) (bīja), sweet with sugar and honey.
02.04.21 | Ground śāli (rice) flour; or milk boiled with the jīvanīya (group).
02.04.22 | And one should feed red śāli rice-meal (odana), very cool, with milk boiled (on a conjectural reading) with balā (Sida cordifolia), atibalā (probably Abutilon indicum), śāli, ṣaṣṭika (sixty-day rice), sugarcane root (on a conjectural reading) and kākolī (identity disputed), together with honey and sugar; or with the broth of jāṅgala (dry-land game) that is cool in vīrya (potency).
02.04.23 | And one should apply the treatments (kriyā) of raktapitta (a bleeding disorder, lit. 'blood-pitta'), excluding the cleansing (śodhana) ones.
02.04.24 | And gentle (saumya) talks that are agreeable to the mind.
02.04.25 | But for her in whom blood has not been seen, one should make (her) drink milk cooked with madhuka, payasyā (identity disputed) and asura-dāru (a drug name, unidentified).
02.04.26 | Or with tāmravallī (unidentified), aśmantaka (identity disputed; usually Bauhinia racemosa), śatāvarī (asparagus) and payasyā (identity disputed).
02.04.27 | Or with the group (gaṇa) beginning with vidārī.
02.04.28 | Or ghee (sarpis) cooked in the svarasa (expressed juice) of śvadaṃṣṭrā (caltrop) and milk.
02.04.29 | Or (that) cooked in the juice of palāṇḍu (onion), with honey.
02.04.30 | But when puṣpa (bleeding) is seen in her whose three months are not complete, the garbha (embryo) generally does not stay, because sāra (essence, firmness) has not yet developed.
02.04.31 | And in the āma-anvaye (the connection with āma, raw undigested material), even afterwards, because of conflicting (viruddha) treatment.
02.04.32 | In that case one should apply dry (rūkṣa) and cool (ones).
02.04.33 | That is as follows.
02.04.34 | First fasting; likewise the drinking of a decoction of durālabhā (identity disputed; Alhagi or Fagonia), amṛtā (guḍūcī, Tinospora cordifolia), uśīra (vetiver), parpaṭaka (probably Fumaria parviflora), ghana (on a conjectural reading; mustā, nut-grass), sandalwood (candana), ativiṣā (Aconitum heterophyllum), balā (Sida cordifolia), kaṭvaṅga (on a conjectural reading; unidentified) and dhānyaka (coriander); and, as food, thin gruel (peyā) of tṛṇadhānya (grass-grains), śāli and ṣaṣṭika, with soups (yūṣa) of mudga (green gram) and the like; but when āma has been overcome, unctuous and cool (ones) as before.
02.04.35 | But when the āma-garbha (unripe embryo; probably an early miscarriage) has fallen, one should make (her) drink any one of the madyas (intoxicating drinks), according to capacity, for the cleansing of the garbha-koṣṭha (the 'storehouse' of the embryo, probably the womb) and for forgetting the pain (arti).
02.04.36 | One should treat a non-drinker of madya (intoxicating drink) with yavāgūs (thick gruels) of uddālaka (identity disputed; probably a kind of kodo millet) and the like, joined with pācanīya (digestion-promoting) substances, without sneha (oil, fat) and salt, and with sesame, for as many days as there are months.
02.04.37 | Then with foods that are gratifying (prīṇana), protective of strength and non-unctuous (without sneha), until the doṣas, dhātus and kleda are cleansed.
02.04.38 | For, because of a remnant of the āma-garbha, pain (śūla) would keep clinging on again and again.
02.04.39 | Therefore one should treat with sharp (tīkṣṇa) (ones), and leave no remnant.
02.04.40 | After that, with drinks of sneha (oil, fat), with bastis (enemas) and with foods that are dīpanīya (digestive-fire kindling), pācanīya (digestion-promoting), jīvanīya (life-promoting), bṛṃhaṇīya (nourishing), sweet (madhura) and Vāta-removing.
02.04.41 | In her for whom the puṣpa (the 'flower': probably menstrual-type bleeding) is due (arhati), the fetus (garbha) having acquired its essence (jātasāra), through non-avoidance of the things to be avoided there may be the sight of puṣpa, or some other flow from the yoni (vagina).
02.04.42 | For her, on that account, Vāyu (Vāta), provoked, having seized Pitta and Śleṣman (Kapha), presses upon the fetus's rasa-carrying nāḍī (channel, probably the umbilical cord) and stays there.
02.04.43 | Then, the nāḍī (probably the umbilical cord) being covered over by the doṣas, as a canal (kulyā) is by grass, leaves and the like, owing to the improper carrying of rasa the fetus does not attain growth, and settles down (upaviśati) or dries up (upaśuṣyati).
02.04.44 | Therein, in her in whom, on a conjectural reading, the ārtava (menstrual blood) and the flow (parisrāva) are occasional and are seen scanty, and the fetus constantly throbs (sphurati) without diminishing from the measure (parimāṇa), and the belly does not grow, that they call upaviṣṭaka. The word 'prāmāt' is unclear and left untranslated.
02.04.45 | When, however, there is ārtava (menstrual blood) every month or flow (parisravaṇa) every day, not very scanty, and so the fetus, wasting (parihīyamāṇa), moves a little after a long time.
02.04.46 | And the belly (kukṣi), though grown, diminishes: that is upaśuṣkaka, and nāgodara.
02.04.47 | Those two, however, nourished by the slight tejas (vigour) of the mother's food, when they become well-nourished, then even after groups of years the woman (pramadā) gives birth indeed.
02.04.48 | Therein, in upaviṣṭaka and upaśuṣkaka, by Vāta, the vāyu is obstructed; dung (śakṛt) is passed noisy, frothy and broken; urine is held back; there are pain in the waist, back and heart (hṛdaya), and yawning; loss of sleep, repeated pratiśyāya (nasal catarrh), dry cough and lassitude (sāda); the two ears, as it were, hum, the two temples are, as it were, pricked; the body is, as it were, joined with ants; as if cutting about, vāyu wanders in the belly (kukṣi); one is, as it were, made to enter darkness (tamas); digestion of food is with difficulty; there is decline day after day; and there is the state of cracked, discoloured, rough skin.
02.04.49 | By Pitta, a coppery-green (one) is passed; dhūmika and amlaka (words of uncertain sense, 'smoky' and 'sour'); vomiting (chardi); fainting (mūrcchā); burning in the belly and heart (hṛdaya); the state of the eyes, urine and nails being yellow, red and cow-urine-hued; and the woman becomes dark (kālī), weak and constantly in pain (śūla).
02.04.50 | By Śleṣman (Kapha): the state of a sweet mouth, nausea (utkleśa), vomiting up of phlegm, aversion to food, whiteness of the hands, feet and eyes, cough, and breathlessness (śvāsa).
02.04.51 | For those two, the use of ghees (sarpis) cooked with life-promoting (jīvanīya), nourishing (bṛṃhaṇīya) and sweet (madhura) medicines (auṣadha).
02.04.52 | Likewise, (the use) of milks (payas), rasas (juices or broths) and āmagarbhas (literally 'raw fetuses'; exact sense uncertain) is promoting the growth of the fetus.
02.04.53 | Likewise, feeding (saṃbhojana) with these very (ones), with ghee and the others.
02.04.54 | Of one well provided with food (subhikṣā), repeatedly, the bringing about (upapādana) by means of carriages, vehicles and the like, and by shaking (kṣobhaṇa), rubbing (mārjana) and yawning (jṛmbhaṇa).
02.04.55 | Then, especially in Vāta, first of all, having given a milk-basti (enema) provided with saindhava (rock salt), one should feed her, bathed in cold water, soft boiled śāli rice (śāli-odana).
02.04.56 | Then one should give an anuvāsana (unctuous enema) with ghee (sarpis) prepared with the vidārī-and-others group (vidārī-ādi-gaṇa; vidārī probably Indian kudzu, Pueraria tuberosa).
02.04.57 | And one should resort to shelter from the wind (nivāta).
02.04.58 | In Pitta, one should drink milk cooked with a decoction (niryūha) of madhuka (probably licorice) and vidārī (probably Indian kudzu).
02.04.59 | Having crushed in a mortar, with milk and water, three large hens (kukkuṭī, fowl) and an āḍhaka (a measure) of husked māṣa (black gram), and having prepared, with its rasa and with ghee, the rasa (broth) of either tittiri (probably partridge) or kapiñjala (a game bird, identity disputed), one should make (her) drink it, for the increase of upaviṣṭaka and for the growth of upaśuṣkaka.
02.04.60 | One should drink a yavāgū (thick gruel) prepared with goat ghee, with milk, and with the jīvanīya (life-promoting) (ones).
02.04.61 | One should cook ghee with milk and with a paste (kalka) of payasyā (identity disputed), the pair of kākolī (identity disputed) and suniṣaṇṇaka (probably Marsilea).
02.04.62 | With that, one should take (it), having milk as the after-drink (anupāna).
02.04.63 | Or one should eat with the rasa (broth) of āmagarbha (literally 'raw fetus'; exact sense uncertain).
02.04.64 | And those very (ones) there; and, on a conjectural reading, flesh of vulture (gṛdhra), (one word unclear), and of water-movers (jalacara).
02.04.65 | In Kapha, however, one should drink clear surā (an intoxicating drink), with meat as its relish, for three nights, for an unclear number of nights, or, on a conjectural reading, for seven nights. The word 'āmrodaka' is unclear and left untranslated.
02.04.66 | Having combined a pātra (vessel-full) of ghee with the five: sesame (tila), mung bean (mudga), black gram (māṣa), salt (lavaṇa) and bilva (bael) leaves (patra), one should have (it) put away, well guarded.
02.04.67 | Then, in the morning, one should eat a lump (piṇḍa) of the size of a bilva (bael), with boiled, cooled goat's milk as the after-drink (anupāna).
02.04.68 | Or, right here, one should also put a pātra of badara (jujube) powder.
02.04.69 | Thus, when there is no growth, one should purge with sharp (ones), and should cause (it) to fall with those that cause the aparā (placenta) to fall.
02.04.70 | Of her, however, whose channels (srotas) are afflicted by Vāta, the fetus, clinging (saṃlīna) and, on a conjectural reading, stupefied, does not move; that they call līna.
02.04.71 | In that case, one should feed (her) red śāli rice (rakta-śāli-odana) with the rasa (broth) of any one of probably hawk (śyena), probably osprey (utkrośa), cow (go), fish (matsya), monitor lizard (godha), peacock (śikhin), fowl (kukkuṭa) and probably partridge (tittirī), made with abundant ghee, or with a māṣa-yūṣa (black-gram soup) or a mūlaka-yūṣa (radish soup) rich in ghee.
02.04.72 | Or one should make (her) drink saktu (flour of roasted grain) combined with sesame, black gram, bilva (bael) and śalāṭu (unripe fruit), with milk.
02.04.73 | Or mārdvīka (grape wine), having meat that is probably fatty (medya) as its relish.
02.04.74 | And one should repeatedly anoint, with oil, the belly (udara), groin (vaṅkṣaṇa), thighs (ūru), waist (kaṭī), flanks (pārśva) and back (pṛṣṭha).
02.04.75 | And one should gladden her again and again.
02.04.76 | And one should look to what was said before.
02.04.77 | Of her, however, who may have udāvarta (a disorder in which Vāta runs upward) and vibandha (constipation), one should treat her with Vāta-removing, unctuous foods and drinks.
02.04.78 | In the eighth month, however, one should give an anuvāsana (unctuous enema) with oil cooked with madhuraka (probably a group of sweet drugs).
02.04.79 | If that does not succeed, having extracted, with milk and half water, the rasa (decoction) of the roots of vīraṇa (probably vetiver grass), śāli (rice), kuśa (kusha grass), kāśa (kans grass), ikṣuvālikā (identity disputed), vetasa (identity disputed) and parivyādha (unidentified), and of bhūtikā (unidentified), ananta (on a conjectural reading; probably sārivā), kāśmarya (gmelina), parūṣaka (phalsa), madhuka (probably licorice) and mṛdvīkā (grape), one should give a nirūha (decoction enema) with that, combined with a paste (kalka) of the kernels (majjā) of piyāla (probably Buchanania) and bibhītaka and of sesame (tila), slightly salty and warm.
02.04.80 | And, her whose constipation (vibandha) has gone, who has bathed and eaten, one should give in the evening an anuvāsana (unctuous enema) with that very oil.
02.04.81 | Udāvarta (a disorder in which Vāta runs upward in reverse), if neglected, would suddenly destroy the pregnant woman together with her fetus.
02.04.82 | Of her whose fetus (garbha) is released from its bond, whether by an excessive accumulation of doṣas, or by those stated, vyavāya (sexual intercourse) and the like, or by other diseases, or by the earlier accumulated action of the mother and of the offspring, like a fruit from its stalk: that fetus, with its bond released, having passed beyond the bed of the fetus (garbhaśayyā, probably the womb), hanging in the gaps of the liver (yakṛt), spleen (plīha) and intestines (antra), brings about a disturbance of the koṣṭha (storehouse, belly).
02.04.83 | Then, from the disturbance of the koṣṭha, the Apāna wind (vāyu), having repeatedly brought about pain in the flank (kukṣi), basti (probably the bladder), sides, belly and yoni, distension (ānāha), and retentions of stool and urine, causes the young fetus to fall away in the form of blood.
02.04.84 | That has been stated before.
02.04.85 | But when the fetus (garbha) has grown, the belly (udara) appears stiff, still, cold, as if it had a stone inside.
02.04.86 | Pain arises in excess, and the fetus does not move; other (ones) appear; the yoni does not flow; and, on a conjectural reading, her two eyes sink in; likewise she, greatly beset by arati (restlessness), is distressed, acts differently, languishes, tires, weeps day and night, does not sleep, breathes with difficulty at each breath, lives (breathes) in great distress, and wastes away. One should know a woman of this kind to be mṛtagarbhā (one with a dead fetus).
02.04.87 | That fetus (garbha), however, which sometimes has entered the path of the offspring (apatyapatha, probably the birth passage) wrongly and in many ways, afflicted and bewildered by deranged wind (vāyu), they call mūḍhagarbha.
02.04.88 | And, on a conjectural reading, (one) of countless courses, owing to the variety of the pressing by deranged wind (anila).
02.04.89 | In brief, however, the course (gati) is of three kinds: upward, transverse and face-down.
02.04.90 | The positions (saṃsthāna), however, are eight.
02.04.91 | Those will be told just here, further on.
02.04.92 | In all of those too, one should avoid the woman whose sense-objects are reversed, who is overtaken by ākṣepa (convulsion), slipping of the yoni, saṃvaraṇa (sense unclear), makkala (sense unclear), breathing trouble and giddiness, whose body is cold, and who has foul belching.
02.04.93 | But one should not neglect the garbhaśalya (the fetus as a dart) even for a moment.
02.04.94 | For that, having made (her) breathless, would quickly kill the mother.
02.04.95 | And a fetus (garbha) that has consciousness, while being split open with a śastra (cutting instrument), may throw its limbs about unevenly.
02.04.96 | Therefore, in the obstruction (saṅga) of that, on a conjectural reading, they should strive for delivery, and one should apply the earlier-stated mantras and medicines (auṣadha).
02.04.97 | The obstruction (saṅga), however, is of three kinds.
02.04.98 | In the head, in the shoulder, or in the jaghana (hip region).
02.04.99 | For a mūḍhagarbha, however, the procedure (karma) is the same as the causing of the jarāyu (afterbirth) to fall, some say.
02.04.100 | Some say that the procedure beginning with mantras is prescribed in the Atharvaveda.
02.04.101 | Some say: the extraction of the śalya (dart, foreign body) is by a śalya-remover (śalyahartṛ) whose work has been seen (dṛṣṭakarman).
02.04.102 | In truth, śastrakarma (work with the cutting instrument, surgery) is the most difficult.
02.04.103 | Since, among the yoni, liver, spleen, intestines, womb (garbhāśaya) and the like, the instrument has to be fixed (avadhārya) by one who does not injure them and does not cause touching; and pulling up, pulling down, turning round, cutting out, cutting through, splitting, pressing and straightening are to be done with one hand.
02.04.104 | Therefore, having consulted the īśvara (lord), and having resorted to supreme effort, accompanied by those who know it, one should undertake it.
02.04.105 | In inaction, death is certain.
02.04.106 | Likewise also of udara (a belly disease, probably ascites-type), aśmarī (urinary stone) and so on.
02.04.107 | Then, in the case of her who lies face up, with the thighs bent and the hip region raised by a cloth cumbhala (probably a pad), having anointed the hand and the yoni with dhanvanāgamṛttikā (unclear), śālmalī-picchā (probably the gum of the silk-cotton tree, Bombax ceiba) and ghee (ghṛta), one should extract the fetus (garbha).
02.04.108 | There, of one who has come by the two thighs, because of ānulomya (following the natural direction), only cut (chinna).
02.04.109 | For one who has presented with one thigh, having stretched out the other thigh; for one who has come at the buttock region, having pressed down the buttock region and lifted upward, having stretched out the two thighs; for one who has come transversely, having turned round, or having lifted the hind half and brought the front half to the path of the offspring straight; for one whose head is turned to the side, having pressed the shoulder and lifted upward, having brought the head to the path of the offspring; for one who has presented both arms, having pressed the two shoulders upward and brought the base of the head, one should draw out.
02.04.110 | But the one who, with its middle bent, comes to the yoni with hands, feet and head.
02.04.111 | And the one who, just bent, (comes) with one foot to the yoni, with the second to the anus (pāyu).
02.04.112 | Those two, mūḍha, cannot be extracted by the hand; thus one should apply the instrument (śastra).
02.04.113 | Then, having comforted the woman, having split open with a maṇḍalāgra (probably a round-tipped knife) or an aṅguliśastra (probably a finger-knife), having removed the pieces of the skull, having seized with a hook (śaṅku) at the chin, the palate, the chest or the armpit, one should draw (it) away.
02.04.114 | Or one whose head is unsplit, at the akṣikūṭa (the eye-socket rim, probably) or at the cheek (gaṇḍa).
02.04.115 | Likewise, for one stuck at the shoulder, having cut off the arm at the shoulder region; for one swollen like a skin-bag (dṛti), having split open the belly (udara) full of wind (vāta) and cast out the intestines, one should extract (it) when it has become slack.
02.04.116 | For one stuck at the hips (jaghana), having broken the hip bones (jaghana-kapāla).
02.04.117 | And on this there are (verses).
02.04.118 | Whatever limb of the fetus (garbha) may get stuck by the power of wind (vāyu), piecemeal.
02.04.119 | Having cut that and that, one should remove it properly, and protect the mother from injury.
02.04.120 | Then the physician should cause the aparā (placenta) that is not falling to fall, as before.
02.04.121 | Thus one should sprinkle with warm water her from whom the śalya (dart, foreign body) has been drawn out.
02.04.122 | Then one should give, to her whose body has been anointed, a swab (picu) of sneha (oil or fat) in the yoni (vagina).
02.04.123 | By that the yoni (vagina) becomes soft, and her pain subsides.
02.04.124 | Dīpyaka (probably ajwain), ativiṣā (atis), rāsnā (on a conjectural reading of the printed rāstrā; identity disputed), hiṅgu (asafoetida), elā (small cardamom) and the pañcakolakas (the group of five; usually pippalī, pippalīmūla, cavya, citraka and śuṇṭhī, though this line does not name them).
02.04.125 | One should make (her) drink the powder with sneha (oil or fat), or the kalka (paste), or the decoction (kvātha), likewise.
02.04.126 | Kaṭukā (kutki), ativiṣā (atis), pāṭhā (probably velvetleaf), the bark of śāka (probably teak), hiṅgu (asafoetida, on a conjectural reading of the printed ghigu) and tejanī (identity disputed).
02.04.127 | And likewise, for the flowing off of the doṣa and for the relief of pain.
02.04.128 | Thus for three nights, for seven days; then she should drink sneha (oil or fat) alone.
02.04.129 | In the evening she should drink ariṣṭa (a fermented medicinal liquor), and likewise a well-made āsava (also a fermented medicinal liquor).
02.04.130 | And one should place in the yoni (vagina) swabs (picu) of the decoction of śirīṣa (siris) and kakubha (arjuna).
02.04.131 | And whatever other complications (upadrava) there may be, those one should treat each according to its own.
02.04.132 | (What is) cooked mostly with Vāta-removing (ones) is beneficial in the meal for ten days.
02.04.133 | And the rasa (juice) for ten days; thereafter, on a conjectural reading, she who takes light, wholesome and little food.
02.04.134 | Intent on sweating (sveda) and anointing (abhyaṅga), she should resort to sneha (oils or fats) beginning with balā oil.
02.04.135 | After four months, she, gradually, and pleasant (ones).
02.04.136 | Now we shall teach the oil of balā (country mallow), balātaila.

02.04.138 | This king of oils, approved by the venerable Dhanvantari, is to be used in drinking, nasal administration (nāvana), anointing and enema (basti) for kings, for rājamātras (a term of unclear sense), for the emaciated, for women recently delivered (sūtikā), for infants, for those past age, for the delicate, for those seized by great graha (seizers), for those whose bodies are wasted by long illness, for those whose limbs are pierced at a marma (vital spot), fallen, struck, broken and dislocated, and, on a conjectural reading, for those afflicted by divine injury, by cursing and by mind-fever (manojvara).
02.04.139 | By that, strength, manly vigour (pauruṣa), memory, understanding, intellect (medhā), digestive fire (agni), vīrya (potency) and ojas (vital essence) increase, and the various Vāta disorders beginning with pakṣaghāta (hemiplegia), ākṣepaka (convulsive disorder) and ardita (facial paralysis), gulma (abdominal lump), hidhmā (hiccup), śvāsa (breathlessness) and kāsa (cough), cease.
