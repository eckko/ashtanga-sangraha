02.07.1 | Now, therefore, we shall explain the Śārīra (the section on the body) called Marmavibhāga, the division of the marmas (vital points of the body).
02.07.2 | A hundred and seven marmas (on a conjectural reading: a hundred with seven more).
02.07.3 | Among these: forty-four in the limbs (śākhā), twenty-six in the trunk (antarādhi), and above, thirty-seven (on a conjectural reading).
02.07.4 | Now, in each sakthi (lower limb), near the middle toe, in the middle of the sole of the foot, the marma named talahṛdaya (sole-heart); for one pierced there, death through pain.
02.07.5 | Between the big toe (aṅguṣṭha) and the toe (aṅguli), kṣipra; there, death by ākṣepaka (probably a convulsive spasm).
02.07.6 | Two aṅgulas (finger-breadths) above kṣipra, on both sides, kūrca; there, on a conjectural reading, whirling (bhramaṇa) and trembling (vepana) of the foot occur.
02.07.7 | Below the ankle-joint (gulpha-saṃdhi), kūrcaśiras (head of the kūrca); there, on a conjectural reading, pain (rujā) and swelling (śopha).
02.07.8 | At the joining of the foot and the shank (jaṅghā), the gulpha (ankle); there, pain, stiffness of the sakthi (thigh), or numbness (jaḍatā).
02.07.9 | Starting from the heel (on a conjectural reading), at twelve aṅgulas, in the middle of the shank (jaṅghā), indrabasti; there, death from loss of blood (śoṇita).
02.07.10 | At the joining of the shank (jaṅghā) and the thigh (ūru), the jānu (knee); there, lameness (khañjatā).
02.07.11 | Three aṅgulas above the knee (jānu), on both sides, āṇi; there, increase of swelling (śopha) and stiffness of the sakthi (thigh).
02.07.12 | In the middle of the thigh (ūru), urvī; there, from loss of blood (rakta), wasting (śoṣa) of the sakthi (thigh).
02.07.13 | And above urvī and below the groin-joint (vaṅkṣaṇa-saṃdhi), at the root of the thigh, the one named lohita ('red'); there, from loss of blood (rakta), pakṣāghāta (hemiplegia, paralysis of one side).
02.07.14 | Between the groin (vaṅkṣaṇa) and the testes (vṛṣaṇa), viṭapa; there, ṣāṇḍhya (impotence, probably) or scantiness of śukra (semen).
02.07.15 | Like the sakthi (lower limb), in the two arms (bāhu).
02.07.16 | The special point, however: gulpha—maṇibandha (wrist), and likewise jānu—kūrpara (elbow); in the piercing of those two, kuṇita (bentness, crookedness).
02.07.17 | And like viṭapa: in the middle of the armpit (kakṣā) and the akṣa (sense uncertain), kakṣādhāra; there, kuṇitva (crookedness).
02.07.18 | The guda (anus), bound to the large intestine (sthūlāntra), the expeller of wind (vāta) and stool (varcas); there, immediate death.
02.07.19 | The basti (urinary bladder): having little flesh and blood, internally kṭacā (an unclear printed word, so on a conjectural reading), bow-curved, with one opening, with its mouth turned downward; there too, immediate death, except from the wound of aśmarī (urinary stone).
02.07.20 | Even there, when pierced on both sides, one does not live; when split on one side, a wound discharging urine (mūtra) results; that, however, treated with effort, heals.
02.07.21 | In the middle of the pakva (digested) and the āmāśaya (probably the stomach), the nābhi (navel); there too, immediate death.
02.07.22 | In the middle of the koṣṭha (belly) and the chest (vakṣas), and of the two breasts, the hṛdaya (heart), the seat of sattva, rajas and tamas (the three guṇas, qualities), the gate of the āmāśaya (probably the stomach); there too, immediate death.
02.07.23 | Two aṅgulas below the two breasts, on both sides, the two stanamūla (breast-roots); in those two, death of one whose koṣṭha (belly) is full of Kapha.
02.07.24 | Two aṅgulas above the two nipples (cūcuka), on both sides, the two stanarohita; immediate death of one whose koṣṭha (belly) is full of blood.
02.07.25 | On both sides of the chest (uras), two nāḍī (channels) carrying vāta (wind), the two apastambha; in those two, death through the koṣṭha being full of blood, and through cough (kāsa) and śvāsa (probably breathlessness).
02.07.26 | Below the two aṃsakūṭa (shoulder-tops), above the parts of the two sides (pārśva), the two apālāpa; in those two, death by blood that has turned into pus (pūya).
02.07.27 | On both sides of the spine (pṛṣṭhavaṃśa), towards the two śroṇīkarṇa (hip-'ears'), two bones, the kaṭīkataruṇa; in those two, from loss of blood (śoṇita), one dies pale (pāṇḍu), discoloured (vivarṇa) and of diminished appearance.
02.07.28 | On the two sides, in the outer part of the jaghana (probably the hip region), on both sides of the spine, the two kukundara; in those two, absence of perception of touch (sparśa-ajñāna) in the lower body, on a conjectural reading, and loss of movement (ceṣṭā).
02.07.29 | Above the two śroṇīkarṇa (hip-'ears'), the two nitamba, covering the āśaya (receptacle) and bound to the interior of the sides (pārśva-antara); in those two, swelling (śopha) and weakness in the lower body, and death.
02.07.30 | Bound to the lower interior of the sides, in the middle between the jaghana (probably the hip region) and the side (pārśva), slanting and upward, the two pārśvasandhi (side-joints); in those two, death through the koṣṭha being full of blood.
02.07.31 | In a straight line (ārjava) with the two stanamūla, on both sides of the spine, the two bṛhatī; in those two, death through complications (upadrava) whose cause is an excessive flow of blood.
02.07.32 | On both sides of the spine, the two aṃsaphalaka (shoulder-plates, probably the shoulder-blades), joined to the roots of the arms; in those two, numbness (svāpa) and wasting (śoṣa) of the arm.
02.07.33 | In the middle of the neck, arm and head, the two aṃsa (shoulders), binders of the aṃsapīṭha (shoulder-seat) and the skandha (shoulder-top); in those two, stiffness of the arms.
02.07.34 | On both sides of the kaṇṭha-nāḍī (throat-tube, probably the windpipe), four dhamanī (vessels); of these, two nīlā, two manyā; in those, dumbness (mūkatā), alteration of the voice, or inability to perceive taste (rasa) occurs.
02.07.35 | In the neck, on both sides, four sirā (vessels), the mātṛkā; in those, immediate death.
02.07.36 | At the junction of head and neck, the two kṛkāṭikā; in those two, an unsteady head (cala-mūrdha-tā).
02.07.37 | The two vidhura (marmas) situated below the backs of the two ears: of those two, deafness (bādhirya).
02.07.38 | On both sides of the nose-passage, on the inside, bound to the ear-passage, the two phaṇa (marmas): of those two, non-perception of smell.
02.07.39 | Below the tail-ends of the two brows, on the outside of the two eyes, the two apāṅga (marmas): of those two, blindness (āndhya).
02.07.40 | Above the tail-ends of the two brows, toward the ear, on the forehead, the two śaṅkha (marmas, probably the temples): of those two, death at once.
02.07.41 | Above the two brows, in the two hollows, the two āvarta (marmas): of those two, blindness or damage to sight.
02.07.42 | Above the two śaṅkhas, at the hair-ends, the two utkṣepa (marmas): of those two, one with the dart (śalya) still in, or one whose dart has fallen out through suppuration (pāka), may live; not one whose dart has been pulled out.
02.07.43 | In the middle of the two brows, the sthapanī (marma); there, as with the utkṣepa.
02.07.44 | Five joints (sandhi) on the head, transverse and upward, called sīmanta (marmas): in those, death by way of insanity (unmāda), giddiness (bhrama) and loss of mind (citta-nāśa).
02.07.45 | Of the vessels (sirā) that satiate the tongue, nose, eye and ear, the meeting is in the palate (tālu); their four mouths are named śṛṅgāṭaka; in those, death at once.
02.07.46 | Inside the head, above, the meeting of vessels (śirā) and joints (sandhi), a whorl of hair: that is called adhipati; there too death occurs at once.
02.07.47 | Further, the general sign of the marmas: when pressed (or injured), arising of pain and irregular throbbing.
02.07.48 | And by the general (one) alone.
02.07.49 | Dehapramusi (unclear), heaviness (gurutā), bewilderment (saṃmoha), desire for cold (śīta-kāmitā).
02.07.50 | Sweating, fainting (mūrcchā), vomiting and breathlessness (śvāsa): the sign of one pierced in a marma.
02.07.51 | And further.
02.07.52 | Because of causing death, marma; and that, moreover, the meeting of flesh, vessels, sinews, bone and joint.
02.07.53 | Therefore, when that is being pressed (or injured), especially affliction of the life-breath (prāṇa).
02.07.54 | But the designation: by preponderance (bāhulya).
02.07.55 | Therefore, on a conjectural reading, according to their basis in flesh and the rest, the marmas are divided in five ways.
02.07.56 | That is as follows.
02.07.57 | Talahṛdaya, indrabasti, guda (anus, rectum) and stanarohita: eleven flesh-marmas (māṃsa-marma).
02.07.58 | Urvī, lohitākhya, nābhi (navel), hṛdaya (heart), stanamūla, apastambha, alāpa, pārśvasandhi, bṛhatī, nīlā, manyā (on a conjectural reading), mātṛkā, phaṇa, apāṅga, sthapanī and śṛṅgāṭaka: forty-one vessel-marmas (sirā-marma).
02.07.59 | Kṣipra, kūrca, kūrcaśiras, viṭapa, kakṣādhara, āṇi, basti (bladder), aṃsa (shoulder), vidhura and utkṣepa: twenty-seven sinew-marmas (snāva-marma).
02.07.60 | Kaṭīkataruṇa, nitamba, aṃsaphalaka (probably the shoulder-blades) and śaṅkha (probably the temples), however: eight bone-marmas (asthi-marma).
02.07.61 | Gulpha (ankle), maṇibandha (wrist), jānu (knee), kūrpara (elbow), kukundara, kṛkāṭikā, āvarta, sīmanta and adhipati: twenty joint-marmas (sandhi-marma).
02.07.62 | And again, in five ways: gudaba (unclear), stana (reading doubtful), nābhi (navel), hṛdaya (heart), mātṛkā, śaṅkha, śṛṅgāṭaka and adhipati: nineteen that take life at once; because of their fiery nature (āgneya), the time for them is within a samarātra (unclear).
02.07.63 | Talahṛdaya, kṣipra, indrabasti, vakṣomarma (reading doubtful), kaṭīkataruṇa, nitamba, pārśvasandhi, bṛhatī and sīmanta: thirty-three that take life after an interval; because of their being saumya (soft, of Soma) and fiery (āgneya), the time for them is within a fortnight (pakṣa).
02.07.64 | Even among those, however, the kṣipra (ones), sometimes quickly.
02.07.65 | The two utkṣepas and the sthapanī: three viśalyaghna (killing when freed from the dart), because of their nature of Vāyu (wind).
02.07.66 | For as long as Vāyu (wind), blocked at the mouth of the dart, stays here, so long (he) lives.
02.07.67 | Kūrca, jānu, kūrpara, āṇi, urvī, lohitākhya, viṭapa, kakṣādhara, kukundara, aṃsaphalaka, nīlā, manyā (on a conjectural reading), kṛkāṭikā, vidhura, phaṇa, apāṅga and āvarta: forty-four that cause impairment (vaikalya), because of their saumya (soft, of Soma) nature.
02.07.68 | For Soma sustains the life-breaths (prāṇa) by steadiness and coolness.
02.07.69 | And those too, when struck, sometimes kill.
02.07.70 | Kūrcaśiras, gulpha (ankle) and maṇibandha (wrist), however: eight sources of pain (ruj), because of an abundance of the qualities of Agni, Vāyu and Soma.
02.07.71 | For Agni and Vāyu, especially, the two sources of pain.
02.07.72 | But Soma upholds the life-breaths (on a conjectural reading); thus it has been said.
02.07.73 | Others, however, say.
02.07.74 | Viśalyaghna (ones, those that kill when the dart is removed), through the coming together of even the five, flesh and the rest, greatly increased.
02.07.75 | Of those reduced by one each, and of the small ones, in order, the other (ones); and others say, by nature alone.
02.07.76 | With each its own fixed function, like the unions of the candra and sūrya stones (probably moonstone and sunstone) and the ayaskānta (lodestone) with water, fire and iron; and likewise, when the hands and feet, though having many marmas, are cut off, by nature itself no death.
02.07.77 | Others again state a reason here also.
02.07.78 | For, on the cutting off of hands and feet, the sirā (vessels, probably veins) contract; hence, from slight flow of blood, life; but, on the piercing of a marma, from excessive flow of blood and from abnormality of wind (vāyu), death.
02.07.79 | Therefore, of one struck on a marma, that limb should be cut off quickly at the place of a joint (sandhi).
02.07.80 | But of marmas that are severely struck, each according to its own, the action: each preceding one; or, 'īṣat ante' (slightly, at the end; this part unclear), of those struck, only the latter.
02.07.81 | And again, (it) is divided fivefold.
02.07.82 | Namely:
02.07.83 | Ūrvī (plural), kūrcaśiras (plural), the two viṭapa and the two kakṣādhara: twelve, measured by one's own finger-breadth (aṅgula).
02.07.84 | The two gulpha (ankle), the two maṇibandha (wrist) and the two stanamūla (base of the breast): six, of two finger-breadths (aṅgula).
02.07.85 | The two jānu (knee) and the two kūrpara (elbow): four, of three finger-breadths (aṅgula).
02.07.86 | The kūrca (plural), guda (anus), basti (bladder), nābhi (navel), hṛdaya (heart), the two nīlā, the two manyā, the mātṛkā (plural), the sīmanta (plural) and the śṛṅgāṭaka (plural): twenty-nine, measured by the palm (pāṇitala).
02.07.87 | The remaining: fifty-six, of the measure of an aṅgula-dala (probably half a finger-breadth).
02.07.88 | Others again say.
02.07.89 | Of the measure of a vrīhi (rice grain): the kṣipra.
02.07.90 | Of the measure of a kalāya (probably the pea): the stanarohita and the utkṣepa.
02.07.91 | Likewise others accept marmas even of the measure of a tila (sesame seed).
02.07.92 | And here there are:
02.07.93 | Even in marmavyāme (unclear), in this body, life guards the marmas.
02.07.94 | Therefore the discerning (man) should always attend only to what pervades life (āyur-vyāpi).
02.07.95 | A blow on a marma, even a slight one, generally afflicts exceedingly.
02.07.96 | Diseases that have a marma as their seat are likewise, set about (prakrāntāḥ) even with effort.
02.07.97 | He is greatly tossed about, sinking, empty, reels, trembles.
02.07.98 | He breathes upward with difficulty, with slack limbs, again and again.
02.07.99 | And his heart (hṛdaya) burns; it does not stay in one place.
02.07.100 | From injury to a marma he reaches death, with these signs (liṅga).
02.07.101 | Even a man pierced at a non-marma (amarma), afflicted by cutting, splitting and the rest,
02.07.102 | and, with much blood flowed out, he gives up life at once.
02.07.103 | Otherwise than this, however, one pierced even by hundreds of arrows lives.
02.07.104 | puṇyavācchviyatāyuś (unclear), and in no way does he not live, on a conjectural reading.
02.07.105 | Therefore one should treat, indeed, with effort, up to the last breath.
02.07.106 | The physician (bhiṣak), approved by good kinsmen and friends, having refused the patient.
02.07.107 | Thus the seventh chapter, named Marmavibhāga (the division of the marmas).
