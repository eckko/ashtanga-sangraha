05.08.1 | Now, therefore, we shall explain the preparation (kalpa) of medicines (bheṣaja).
05.08.2 | In a jāṅgala (dry, open country) or in a common region, according to the season, in land that is frequented by cold, sunshine, wind and water, level, clean, with water flowing to the right, free from cremation ground, shrine (caitya), temple, house and assembly hall, and from garden, anthill and saline soil, spread with kuśa grass (Desmostachya bipinnata) and rohiṣa grass (probably a fragrant Cymbopogon grass; disputed), with soil that is unctuous, black, golden-coloured, sweet and fragrant, ploughed (the printed 'mṛdāvaphāla' is unclear; on a conjectural reading, with a ploughshare), and not harmed by other, stronger trees: medicines are praised. Thus it is the general examination of land.
05.08.3 | In particular: stony, firm, heavy, dark, with thin and thick grass, mostly with trees and crops (sasya), abounding in its own quality.
05.08.4 | Unctuous, cool, with water near, mostly with unctuous grass, crops (sasya) and tender leaves, white, and abounding in the quality of water.
05.08.5 | Of various colours, having light flowers, with a very sparse growth of pale trees, and abounding in the quality of fire.
05.08.6 | Of the colour of a donkey and of ash, dry, mostly slight, hollow and saline, with a growth of little taste (rasa), and abounding in the quality of wind.
05.08.7 | Soft, level, having pits, with water of indistinct taste (rasa), with great trees at its edges, dark, and abounding in the quality of space (ākāśa).
05.08.8 | Among these, (ones) born in lands abounding in the qualities of water and earth are praised as purgative (virecana) and nourishing (bṛṃhaṇa).
05.08.9 | In (lands) abounding in the qualities of fire, wind and space: (ones) for vomiting (vamana).
05.08.10 | In (lands) endowed with all the qualities: both-way (ubhayatobhāga) ones.
05.08.11 | In (lands) abounding in the quality of space (ākāśa): pacifying (śamana) ones.
05.08.12 | Thus (they) become the most powerful.
05.08.13 | Among these, those which are born in their time, have attained full measure, taste (rasa), potency (vīrya) and smell, whose smell, colour, taste, touch and potency (prabhāva) have not been harmed by time, sunshine, mi (unclear), water, wind, weapons and creatures, whose roots are deep, and which stand in the northern direction.
05.08.14 | Of these, the branch and leaf (śākhā, palāśa), recently grown, are to be taken in the rainy season and in spring.
05.08.15 | In summer, the roots; or in the cold season (śiśira), of those whose leaves have fallen and sprouted.
05.08.16 | In autumn: bark, tuber (kanda) and milk (kṣīra).
05.08.17 | In winter (hemanta): the sāra (essence, core; probably heartwood).
05.08.18 | Fruit and flower according to the season.
05.08.19 | Others, again, say:
05.08.20 | Saumya (gentle) medicines (auṣadha) in the saumya seasons, āgneya (fiery) ones in the āgneya (ones).
05.08.21 | Thus (they) become unspoiled, with fuller taste (rasa) and potency (vīrya).
05.08.22 | Then, of auspicious conduct and good behaviour, clean, in white clothes, having worshipped the deity, the two Aśvins, cows and brahmins, having fasted, facing east or facing north, he should take (it).
05.08.23 | And having taken (them) and stored (them) in a vessel of suitable, good quality, he should keep them hung in slings (śikya) in houses with east and north doors, in places of one part windless and breezy, in places of constant flower-offering and bali rites, (them) not to be approached by fire, water, steam (upasveda), smoke, dust, mice, quadrupeds and so on, svavaśyāni (unclear; on a conjectural reading, under one's own control).
05.08.24 | He should prepare those (that are) with milk (sakṣīra).
05.08.25 | tadaṃmattāva (unclear); on a conjectural reading, (ones) whose year has not passed.
05.08.26 | But old (ones): pippalī (long pepper), viḍaṅga (false black pepper; Embelia ribes), jaggery (guḍa), honey (madhu) and ghee (ghṛta).
05.08.27 | And further:
05.08.28 | Milk (bauṣkyaṇa, unclear) is to be taken, and dung and urine, disease-free (nīruja).
05.08.29 | Of (those) possessing age and strength: dhātu, tail, horn, hoof and so on, on a conjectural reading.
05.08.30 | A fivefold decoction (kaṣāya) of medicines is to be prepared.
05.08.31 | Niryāsa (expressed juice), kalka (paste), niryūha (decoction), śīta (cold infusion) and phāṇṭa (hot infusion).
05.08.32 | They are strong in the order of precedence (yathāpūrvam).
05.08.33 | Among these, of (one) freshly pulled up, washed, crushed and pressed out through a cloth (tāntava), the own juice (svarasa) is niryāsa.
05.08.34 | But (that) ground with a stone, teeth and so on is kalka (paste).
05.08.35 | A powder that has fallen through a fine cloth.
05.08.36 | Of that, because the whole substance is not abandoned and because of quick use, there is no difference from kalka.
05.08.37 | Kvātha (decoction) is niryūha.
05.08.38 | As for that (niryūha): having split finely the herbs that are to be split, having cut those that are to be cut, having washed them with water, having put them into a pot that is clean, dry and smeared below, any one of copper, iron or clay, and, of the herbs made to take abundant water, ākalathya (unclear; on a conjectural reading, having reckoned), he should pour in as much water as makes a state of released taste (rasa), and should reduce it.
05.08.39 | Then, having placed it on the fire, seated comfortably on a large seat, constantly watching from all sides and stirring with a ladle, he should prepare it with a gentle fire reaching all around.
05.08.40 | And having taken it down, well strained, of suitable touch, he should use (it).
05.08.41 | And a substance together with milk and the like does not become one whose taste (rasa) is fully released; so, preceded by a decoction in water, he should boil it with milk and the like, as instructed, not scorched.
05.08.42 | But (that) soaked in cold water, kept overnight and strained is śīta (cold infusion).
05.08.43 | (That) crushed and soaked (abhisuta) in hot water and strained is phāṇṭa (hot infusion).
05.08.44 | Among these, having properly examined the taste (rasa), potency (vīrya) and so on of the medicine, and according to the strength of the disease, the patient and so on, he should resort to the preparation of niryāsa and the others (on a conjectural reading of 'kalpanāva nām') and to the dose (mātrā).
05.08.45 | And thus some say:
05.08.46 | There is no fixed rule for the dose (mātrā); the body, doṣa, strength, age
05.08.47 | Having considered (these) and place and time, he should administer the medicine (bhaiṣajya).
05.08.48 | Thus, enough.
05.08.49 | That has been taught thus, for the awakening of the knowledge of the intelligent.
05.08.50 | As, however, all physicians will understand, so it will be taught.
05.08.51 | The middle dose of the niryāsa (expressed juice) is, on a conjectural reading, four palas (pala: a unit of weight).
05.08.52 | Of kalka (paste) and cūrṇa (powder), a karṣa (a unit of weight); three palas for stirring it.
05.08.53 | For a decoction (niryūha): having put a pala of the drug on (the fire) in half a prastha (a unit of volume) of water, one should take it down when a quarter remains. For śīta (cold infusion) and phāṇṭa (hot infusion): a pala of the drug, steeped with six and with four palas of water.
05.08.54 | In sneha-cooking (the cooking of sneha, fat: ghee or oil) where the measure is not stated, the sneha should be used at a quarter of the total liquid, and the kalka (paste) at a quarter of that.
05.08.55 | Others, again, read.
05.08.56 | When the sneha is being cooked: with pure water, decoction and expressed juice (svarasa), in order.
05.08.57 | One should add the portion of the kalka (paste): a fourth, a sixth, an eighth.
05.08.58 | But from five onward, one should add the liquid separately, just equal to the sneha.
05.08.59 | A drug whose kalpa (manner of preparation) is not specified, one should make into kalka (paste).
05.08.60 | Then, having mixed (them) together and put (it) on (the fire), one should prepare (it) like a decoction (niryūha).
05.08.61 | There, when the sound stops, the sneha reaches clarity, there is the attainment of its own proper colour, smell and taste, and the medicine, when held between two fingers, is neither too soft nor too hard and does not stick to the fingers (on an emended reading, see notes): that is the time for taking it down.
05.08.62 | Moreover.
05.08.63 | Of ghṛta (ghee), the subsiding of the froth; of taila (oil), however, the arising of that.
05.08.64 | Of a leha (electuary), the having of threads, sinking in water, and no spreading.
05.08.65 | Then, having taken it down and (it) having become cool, having strained it through a new (ahata) cloth, having poured it into a clean, firm jar (kalaśa), having closed it with a lid or covered it with a white strip of cloth, well tied with thread and, on a conjectural reading, well protected, one should keep it well placed in a clean place.
05.08.66 | As for measure, again: six aṃśya are a marīci (a minute unit of measure).
05.08.67 | Those six are a sarṣapa (mustard seed; here a unit of measure).
05.08.68 | Those eight are a taṇḍula (rice grain; here a unit of measure).
05.08.69 | Those two are a dhānyamāṣa (a small unit of weight; literally 'grain-māṣa').
05.08.70 | Those two are a yava (barley; here a unit of measure).
05.08.71 | After this, by fourfold increase, the aṇḍikā, māṣaka and śāṇa (on a conjectural reading), karṣa, pala, kuḍava, prastha, āḍhaka, droṇa and vaha are reckoned.
05.08.72 | The dharaṇa, however, is a tenth part of a pala.
05.08.73 | The synonyms of the māṣaka (a unit of weight) are hema and dhānaka.
05.08.74 | Of two śāṇas: kṣudra, kola and vaṭaka.
05.08.75 | Of the karṣa: suvarṇa, akṣa, picu, biḍālapadaka (cat's-foot), pāṇitala (palm of the hand), tinduka, kavalagraha (mouthful-grasp) and ṣoḍaśikā.
05.08.76 | Of two karṣas: śukti and aṣṭamikā.
05.08.77 | Of the pala: muṣṭi (fist), prakuñca, caturthikā and bilvamātra (the extent of a bilva, bael).
05.08.78 | Of two palas: prasṛta and aṣṭamāna.
05.08.79 | Of the kuḍava: añjali.
05.08.80 | Of two kuḍavas: mānikā.
05.08.81 | Of the āḍhaka: pātra and kaṃsa.
05.08.82 | Of the droṇa: nalvaṇa, armaṇa, unmāna, ghaṭa, kumbha and kalaśa.
05.08.83 | Of two droṇas: śūrpa.
05.08.84 | The tulā, again, is a hundred palas.
05.08.85 | Twenty of those are one bhāra (a load).
05.08.86 | And here there is this:
05.08.87 | In what is to be measured dry, this is the measure; but double in the liquid and the moist (ones).
05.08.88 | Water for grinding and stirring, and also in sneha-cooking that is without liquid.
05.08.89 | One should make equal portions where the quantity is not stated.
05.08.90 | In the unspecified and well-known (ones), among bark and the rest, the root is to be taken.
05.08.91 | Sneha-cooking is of three kinds: manda (mild), cikkaṇa (smooth) and khara-cikkaṇa (hard-smooth).
05.08.92 | Manda when the residue (kiṭṭa) is like the kalka; cikkaṇa when it is like madana (probably wax).
05.08.93 | The last one, however, when (it) sinks a little and is black.
05.08.94 | Beyond this it is burnt (dagdha) and should be taken out; the raw (āma) one, however, causes agnisāda (the weakening of the digestive fire, agni).
05.08.95 | Mild (mṛdu) for nasya (nasal administration), hard (khara) for abhyaṅga (anointing, oil massage), and cikkaṇa for drinking and for basti (enema).
05.08.96 | The earth is for the most part pervaded by the mountains Himavat and Vindhya (on a conjectural reading, vyāptā).
05.08.97 | Among these, the first is saumya (of Soma's nature) and wholesome (pathya); the Vindhya medicine (auṣadha) is āgneya (of Agni's nature).
05.08.98 | Thus the eighth chapter, named Bheṣajakalpa (the preparation of medicines).
05.08.99 | Thus, in the Aṣṭāṅgasaṅgraha composed by the venerable teacher Vāgbhaṭa, son of the venerable Siṃhagupta, the fifth section, the Kalpasiddhisthāna, is complete.
05.08.100 | May it be an offering to Śrī Kṛṣṇa.
